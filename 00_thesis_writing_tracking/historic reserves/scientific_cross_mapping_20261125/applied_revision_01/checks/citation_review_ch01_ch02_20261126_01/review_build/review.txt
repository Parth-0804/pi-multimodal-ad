Abstract
       Industrial monitoring increasingly relies on data from cameras, acoustic signals,
and machine sensors. Although multimodal learning can leverage the strengths of each
data type, adding sources does not necessarily improve prediction accuracy or reliability,
particularly when data quality or relevance varies.
       This thesis investigates the design and evaluation of process-aware multimodal
fusion architectures. It presents a critical review of 94 full-text works and five empirical
studies encompassing industrial gear monitoring, robotic assembly, milling,
manipulation, and human–robot handover. The research employs pretrained visual
representations, handcrafted sensor features, and patch-based temporal encoders. It
evaluates feature-level, prediction-level, and adaptive fusion, and also examines grouped
assessment and controlled input degradation where applicable.
       In the REASSEMBLE dataset, analysis of 4,530 completed actions from 148
recordings demonstrated that logistic stacking increased failure average precision from
0.429 for its sensor component to 0.447. However, the difference from equal averaging
(0.446) was small, and the advantage of stacking remained statistically unresolved.
Subsequent experiments indicated that training budget, feature aggregation, and
complementarity between representations can influence predictive performance.
External evaluations revealed that the benefits of fusion were not consistently observed,
and adaptive reweighting did not reliably enhance robustness.
       The results indicate that the value of multimodal fusion depends on the
prediction task, the available information, and the evaluation protocol, rather than
solely on architectural complexity. Issues such as independent welding validation,
predictive audio fusion, early warning, and generalization of unchanged models to new
domains remain unresolved. Consequently, the conclusions are limited to the tasks and

Introduction


1.1     Context and Motivation

Industrial monitoring means tracking the state and behavior of a physical process by
analyzing data collected during or after it runs. Industrial and robotic systems produce
many types of data, such as visual observations, acoustic signals, force measurements,
vibration, and other sensor signals (Huo et al., 2022; Thoduka et al., 2024b). Each data
type shows a different aspect of the process and may respond differently to changes in
operation.
       Multimodal learning enables the integration of these diverse data types,
particularly when each source provides complementary or unique information. However,
adding data sources does not inherently improve predictions (W. Wang et al., 2020).
The benefit depends on the information each modality retains, the relevance of the data
to the prediction task, and the methods used for data integration.
       Robotic welding was the starting point for this research. A camera can observe
the weld pool, while acoustic and machine sensor measurements give information about
other parts of the welding process. Previous studies have explored combining visual and
acoustic data for weld assessment (Fan et al., 2026; Hua et al., 2026).
       The primary architectural challenge extends beyond welding. Industrial
monitoring systems require data representations that capture task-relevant information,



                                            3
Chapter 1. Introduction


fusion methods that effectively utilize complementary data from multiple sources, and
evaluation protocols that assess whether predictions remain reliable when some data are
missing or of low quality. Therefore, this research investigates multimodal fusion
architectures across a range of industrial and robotic monitoring tasks.



1.2     Problem Statement and Research Gap

The added value of additional modalities cannot be determined solely by the predictive
performance of the combined model; it is essential to compare the architecture’s
performance against credible unimodal predictors on identical prediction targets and
assessment observations.
       Prior work has shown that multimodal integration can improve prediction, but it
also identifies circumstances in which a jointly trained multimodal model will perform
worse than its unimodal counterparts (W. Wang et al., 2020). In one weld penetration
study, the image-only, audio-only, and fused models all achieved 100% accuracy on the
reported clean test set (Hua et al., 2026, Tables 1–3). There was therefore no
demonstrable benefit to the availability of an additional modality in terms of clean data
accuracy.
       Another challenge concerns the reliability of each data type. When an input is
missing, fallback mechanisms may be employed. If an input is present but degraded,
evidence is required to demonstrate altered reliability. While adaptive fusion can adjust
the contribution of each input, modifying these weights alone does not ensure improved
robustness (Ovadia et al., 2019; Q. Zhang et al., 2023).
       The fairness of these comparisons also depends on factors like the prediction
target, how the data is aligned over time, training methods, calibration, and whether
the test data is independent (Guo et al., 2017; Kapoor & Narayanan, 2023; Ovadia
et al., 2019; Varma & Simon, 2006). Differences in any of these areas can make it hard
to know if performance gains are generalizable.
       This thesis addresses this gap by investigating the added predictive value and

robustness of multimodal fusion under fair, task-specific experimental conditions. It
investigates how the selection of data representation, complementary information, and
fusion method interact, and whether observed benefits in one context are transferable to
other industrial and robotic monitoring tasks.
       Chapter 2 reviews existing research to lay out the scientific background and
highlight the unresolved comparisons that define this research gap.



1.3     Research Questions and Objectives

The main goal of this thesis is to study how to design and evaluate process-aware
multimodal AI systems for reliable industrial monitoring and anomaly detection. It
examines how using specific data representations and different fusion methods affects
prediction, robustness, and practical usefulness.
       The first version of the exposé set out this main research question:


      How can a process-aware multimodal AI architecture be designed and
      evaluated for robust industrial anomaly detection using robotic welding data
      as a case study?


       Four subquestions address the main architectural and methodological aspects of
this study.


   SQ1. Which lightweight or pretrained Transformer-based representation components
         are suitable for visual, audio, and sensor time-series data in a multimodal
         industrial anomaly detection pipeline?

   SQ2. How do unimodal baselines, simple concatenation fusion, modular late fusion,
         and process-aware gated cross-modal fusion compare in terms of predictive
         performance?

   SQ3. Can a gated cross-modal fusion architecture improve robustness when one or
         more modalities are missing, degraded, or unreliable?

   SQ4. How can multimodal architecture performance be evaluated beyond accuracy,
         including robustness, interpretability, modularity, and practical usability?

       These considerations result in four research objectives: evaluating the suitability
of specific data representations, investigating various fusion strategies, testing
robustness to input degradation, and assessing the appropriateness of selected
evaluation methods for multimodal monitoring systems. Broader architectural questions
persist, despite changes in the original application scope.



1.4     Methodology Overview and Contributions

This research integrates a critical literature review with empirical investigations of
multimodal representation and fusion architectures. The review encompassed 94 full
texts, screened and assessed in two stages, with emphasis on architectural approaches,
unimodal baselines, fusion methods, evaluation protocols, and strategies for handling
unreliable inputs.
       Dataset selection also considered modality correspondence, annotation
suitability, assessment independence, and computational viability. The empirical
component compared modality-specific representations and prediction models, then
evaluated alternative fusion strategies and a series of controlled follow-up experiments.
       The implemented architectures use pre-trained convolutional visual
representations, statistical sensor features, and patch-based temporal encoders.
Information is combined using feature concatenation, probability averaging, logistic
stacking, and context-conditioned weighting.
       Evaluation covers predictive performance, calibration, grouped assessment,
uncertainty, and how the system behaves when inputs are missing or degraded,
depending on the dataset and protocol. Tests on external datasets check if the chosen
fusion methods and data representations work for different prediction tasks.
       The principal contributions of this thesis are:

   • Comparative evaluation of multimodal representations: This includes

      empirical tests of pretrained visual features, statistical sensor representations, and
      patch-based temporal encoders. The study compares training budgets, feature

  • Comparative evaluation of fusion architectures: Feature-level,

       The empirical work then shifted to related industrial and robotic monitoring

       PHM2026 focused on building condition targets and sensor-based prediction.

prediction branch was therefore not included.
       In this thesis, process-aware fusion means adjusting how predictions are
combined based on the process context and available quality information. Although the
registered title mentions physics-informed, the architectures do not use explicit
differential equations or conservation law constraints.
       The empirical work focuses on estimating conditions and classifying outcomes
within set observation periods. Classifying completed actions and manipulation
episodes does not, by itself, provide prospective anomaly detection or early warning.
       Robustness evaluation is limited to experiments where inputs were removed or
degraded in a controlled way. Studies with external datasets focus on re-using
architectures, fitting to specific datasets, and selected transfer methods, rather than
testing if an unchanged model works in a new domain.
       Therefore, the conclusions only apply to the architectures, prediction targets,
and evaluation conditions studied. Independent welding validation, predictive audio

Critical Literature Review


Multimodal Learning in Industrial and Robotic

Monitoring

Industrial monitoring covers tasks such as fault detection, checking a system’s physical
condition, assessing manufacturing quality, and confirming that a robot has completed
its task. The traditional approach uses single sensor readings and engineered features to
characterise the system’s behaviour. Learned representations take this further by
utilising data from images, sound and sensor sequences. Multimodal learning examines
how information from different modalities can be combined to support a common
prediction task (Baltrušaitis et al., 2019; Huo et al., 2022; Thoduka et al., 2024b).
       The literature touches on several related areas of research: traditional feature
engineering, the use of pre-trained representations, modelling over time, feature
combination, prediction fusion, and adaptive methods (where the method can adapt to
the context). They differ in the information they require, their computational cost, the
level of supervision they need, and their sensitivity to input quality.
       Industrial and robotic monitoring provides a platform on which to compare these
approaches as the process can be monitored via a number of different modalities
(cameras, acoustic sensors, machine sensors, etc.). Cameras will obviously provide a
view of the visible process, whereas the acoustic and machine sensors provide


                                            10
Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


information which is not visible. The benefit of combining data depends heavily on
what each modality retains, the specific prediction you’re trying to make, and the
system’s test conditions (Goodarzi et al., 2023; Hua et al., 2026; W. Wang et al., 2020).
Simply increasing the amount of data does not necessarily lead to better predictions.
       Robotic welding is the principal driver of this thesis. Work on welding using
visual and acoustic data has shown that multimodal integration can improve prediction
performance, but also that, in some cases, adding a further modality provides no benefit
at all (Fan et al., 2026; Hua et al., 2026). The Intel Welding audit documented prior
examination of the proposed assessment population, so an independent assessment of
weld quality was not possible on that population (see Chapter 1). The review therefore
addresses a more general architectural question across a range of industrial and robotic
monitoring tasks: how physical measurements, visual data, and sensor data can be
combined to assess system condition and task outcome.
       This chapter is organised around four research sub-questions: (SQ1) the
appropriateness of the representations, (SQ2) the incremental value of data integration,
(SQ3) reliability in the face of missing or degraded inputs and (SQ4) the need for
evaluation criteria that go beyond prediction accuracy. The aim is to present the
scientific evidence, identify the gaps in the comparisons and describe the experimental
design requirements. The architecture is detailed in Chapter 3 and the experimental
methodology in Chapter 4.



2.1     Review Scope and Search Methodology

The review examines industrial fusion claims through the methodologies, comparison
tables, and experimental results of the papers. It focuses on the link between the
proposed architecture, the available physical observations, the prediction target, and the
assessment protocol.
       A research gap is defined as a comparison that the existing body of scientific
evidence does not address for a given target, input set and assessment population. The



                                           11
Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


ability to identify such a gap will, of course, depend on the search strategy and the
amount of published literature available.


2.1.1     Research Themes and Search Questions

The representation theme concerns what is being observed, what transformations are
being applied, and whether the representation retains the information relevant to the
task at hand. The fusion theme is about whether adding further inputs improves
performance on a standard prediction task compared with a set of plausible single-input
references.
        The robustness theme looks at which absence, corruption, and
distribution-change regimes were assessed. The evaluation theme looks at assessment
independence, thresholds, uncertainty, resource measurement and reproducibility.
        These themes intentionally include negative and inconclusive findings.
Comparisons where an additional modality yields minimal or no benefit can provide
valuable insights into representation suitability, redundancy, and the limitations of the
evaluated fusion procedure.
        The review clearly distinguishes between two objectives that are often confused.
Training with multiple modalities to deploy a single-input model is a privileged training
scenario. Combining multiple available inputs at deployment is a multimodal inference
problem. Cross-modal distillation and reconstruction link the two objectives but the
acquisition assumptions are very different.
        The taxonomy of Baltrušaitis et al. (2019) is the organising terminology. The
industrial claims are then assessed against the original studies rather than being entirely
inferred from that survey.

2.1.5     Limitations of the Review

The papers reviewed are by no means exhaustive and are not statistically independent.
Several inspection studies use the same MVTec 3D-AD or Eyecandies dataset, so
improvements on those benchmarks are not necessarily indicative of improvements on
independent industrial installations. The preprints are also version-specific, and some
publication details are still provisional.
        When relatively few studies examine a particular condition, the body of evidence
is such that any conclusions about that condition are necessarily weak.
        The main MVTec 3D-AD table gives 249 normal and 948 anomalous test
samples (total 1197) (Bergmann et al., 2022, Table 1). The welding penetration paper
reports inconsistent acquisition and training numbers, and the light inspection paper
reports different NwfDD values in its main table and its ablation table (Hua et al., 2026,
pp. 6708, 6711); (Z. Zhang et al., 2024, p. 6661, Tables IV, VI–VII).
        These inconsistencies reduce the precision of the comparisons. The studies
remain useful when their methods and reported findings are interpreted within clearly
stated limits.



2.2      Industrial Monitoring Tasks and Supervision

Different monitoring tasks rely on different assumptions about training data, available
observations, and acceptable levels of prediction error. These assumptions must be
understood prior to comparing representation learning and fusion architectures.
        The definition of the prediction target is absolutely crucial, as performance on
one industrial task cannot be considered valid evidence of performance on another.


2.2.1     Anomaly Detection, Defect Classification, and Failure

          Classification

Normal-only anomaly detection learns a reference for nominal observations. Supervised
defect classification, by contrast, uses labelled fault or quality classes. MVTec AD and

                                             15
Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


MVTec 3D-AD are examples of the normal-only approach, whereas welding penetration
recognition is an example of supervised classification (Bergmann et al., 2019, 2022; Hua
et al., 2026).
        HANDOVER defines five completed-interaction outcome classes: success, no
approach, no grasp, no release and object drop (Thoduka et al., 2024b). A task failure
in the operational domain doesn’t necessarily mean that some physical damage has
occurred or that we can identify the root cause of that damage.
        Supervision is thus an important aspect of the evaluation. The weakly
supervised inspection study brings the MVTec data back in, as training requires
image-level anomalous labels, which of course changes the normal-only setting
(Z. Zhang et al., 2024, p. 6659).
        ZSG-IAD trains on reports and masks before evaluating on held-out categories
or datasets. The zero-shot terminology refers to that assessment boundary rather than
to the complete absence of anomaly supervised training (Q. Chen et al., 2026, PDF
pp. 6, 8–9).
        These differences establish the need to compare architectures against their
respective target definitions and under their respective supervision conditions.


2.2.2     Condition Estimation and Regression

Continuous prediction calls for attention to measurement units, reference observations,
and the horizon at which the target is defined. Remaining useful life is a very different
prediction problem from instantaneous defect probability.
        Al-Dulaimi et al. (2019) look at a hybrid CNN-LSTM on simulated C-MAPSS
trajectories using RMSE and an asymmetric prognostic score. Both branches of their
model process the same set of selected telemetry channels. The study therefore provides
evidence of complementary representations of the sensor data rather than any physical
camera/sensor complementarity.
        The synthetic robot benchmarks of Altinses and Schwung (2025) examine
cross-modal prediction, reconstruction, and downstream regression. They show the


                                            16
Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


importance of relationships between representations, though errors on different
reconstructed outputs do not necessarily tell you which modality is more useful for a
given common prediction task.
        This issue is particularly relevant to proxy supervision. A model may accurately
predict a derived condition index, yet the relationship between that index and actual
physical deterioration may remain uncertain. A defensible regression comparison must
therefore define the target construction, measurement scale, fitted pre-processing, and
common assessment observations before interpreting numerical improvement as
indicative of enhanced condition monitoring.


2.2.3     Dataset-Provided Labels, Derived Targets, and Proxy

          Supervision

Reference labels have very different evidential roles. Post-build CT provides flaw
localisation for the voxel-level monitoring study by Petrich et al. (2021). Microscopy
and density assessments are used to define quality classes in J. Li et al. (2022).
Operando X-ray imaging is used to define process-state labels in Pandiyan et al. (2022).
        These studies show how an independent instrument can establish or validate a
prediction target without necessarily being available at the time of model inference. It
is vital to distinguish between supervision and predictive inputs when describing the
information available to a monitoring architecture.
        For derived labels, predictive performance must be distinguished from
independent physical validation. If the target is computed from the same measurements
supplied to the model, then a low prediction error may reflect the model recovering that
calculation rather than identifying an independent physical state.
        At the regional level, a single quality label may be assigned to many adjacent
observations. Their spatial or temporal dependence affects the choice of an appropriate
assessment unit.
        The empirical work in this thesis therefore distinguishes between native dataset
annotations, independent reference measurements, and analytical proxies. Each target

Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


type yields different conclusions regarding physical state and operational outcome.


2.2.4     Retrospective Assessment and Online Monitoring

The observation horizon is what determines when the model’s forecast becomes available.
A predictor assessed after the full observation period has elapsed is effectively given a
retrospective assessment unless its inputs are restricted to an earlier decision time.
        HANDOVER is very clear that its baselines classify outcomes post-trial, with
causal online prediction still being work in progress (Thoduka et al., 2024b, PDF p. 7
Section VI). A post-pass photo, a layer summary and the completed action all provide
different information horizons from prospective monitoring (Petrich et al., 2021;
X. Zhang et al., 2026).
        Retrospective classification is still useful for assessing representations and fusion
techniques, though its use in an operational context differs from that of an early
warning system.
        Computational delay must also be distinguished from observation time. DMAW
uses 20 frames at 37 fps and reports a much shorter forward-pass latency. As a result,
approximately half a second of observation is distinct from computational delay (Fan
et al., 2026, PDF pp. 6–7).
        Similarly, simply varying the input duration of a CNN–LSTM does not, by itself,
mean that a warning can be generated before a defect occurs (Pandiyan et al., 2022).
Active monitoring requires a defined information cut-off, acquisition delay and
end-to-end prediction workload.



2.3      Multimodal Data in Industrial Applications

Complementarity is determined by the additional task-relevant information provided by
an observation or representation, rather than by differences in tensor shape or the
number of acquisition sources.
        Different representations of the same physical quantity can contain


                                             18
Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


complementary information. Likewise, physically distinct sensors can provide redundant
or only very weakly relevant information for a given prediction target.
        The review therefore distinguishes between physical acquisition channels and
derived representations and considers ordinary pairing separately from cases requiring
stronger correspondence, such as cross-modal reconstruction or monitoring of a localised
process.


2.3.1      Visual and Video Observations

RGB images, thermal images, process videos and geometric observations all have
different acquisition assumptions. Depth, depth-derived point clouds, and estimated
surface normals can all be multiple views of the same geometric source.
        GPAD combines RGB-depth features with point cloud priors (M. Li et al., 2026,
PDF pp. 4–7). This is an example of integration across representations that do not
necessarily require three separate physical acquisition sources.
        RGB-geometry benchmarks describe appearance and shape rather than
controller dynamics or motor current (Bergmann et al., 2022; Bonfiglioli et al., 2023).
        Physical camera-plus-measurement precedents include thermal imaging with
vibration, welding images with sound, tool images with acoustic emission, and robot
video with force/torque and gripper state (Hua et al., 2026; Huo et al., 2022; Thoduka
et al., 2024b; X. Zhang et al., 2026).
        Their prediction units range from static inspection through to process windows
and full trials. In the tool wear study, the camera is used post-cutting pass, with the
preceding acoustic emission capturing the active cutting (X. Zhang et al., 2026, PDF
pp. 19–22).
        The representation of visual observations also influences the information
available. Both pooled frame features and video Transformers process multiple images,
but they differ in how they treat temporal relationships.
        These distinctions between them lead us to explicitly consider visual acquisition,
observation windows, and aggregation within multimodal monitoring architectures.

2.3.2     Sensor Time Series and Process Measurements

Machine measurements can vary enormously in their physical origin and in their
semantic meaning. An accelerometer measures vibration, a pressure sensor measures
fluid behaviour, and controller logs can contain commands, estimates, or tracking errors
rather than a direct measure of a physical quantity.
        The harmonic-drive study combines high-frequency vibration with servo
information, whereas PGMTKD transfers pressure-vibration teacher knowledge to a
pressure-only student (C. Li et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2024).
        Both papers address incomplete information, but their sensor availability and
deployment reasons differ.
        Representations can also inflate the apparent number of modalities without
including any new measurements. Kullu and Cinar (2022) combine current and
vibration using both raw and STFT representations. Wan et al. (2024) derive statistical
’vibration’ features from two acoustic sensors without acquiring an accelerometer
channel at all. C. Wang et al. (2025) combine source domain features with the decisions
from the vibration data.
        These studies are useful as architectural comparators, as they examine different
information representations. Their results should, of course, be treated as distinct from
comparisons that incorporate additional physical acquisition sources.
        An acquisition inventory and a description of the retained representations are
required prior to attributing any predictive gain to modality diversity.


2.3.3     Audio Observations

Audio can pick up machine/process events that are only very weakly visible. The
relationship to fault state depends heavily on background conditions, mic location, and
sync.
        MIMII deliberately incorporates factory background recordings at a range of
signal-to-noise ratios. Its fan baseline AUC drops from .94 at +6 dB to .70 at −6 dB
(Purohit et al., 2019, Table 3).

Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


        ToyADMOS provides miniature machine recordings and an explicit noise-mixing
protocol, making it a useful reference for acoustic monitoring (Koizumi et al., 2019).
        In process monitoring, airborne acoustic signals can be paired with either a melt
pool camera or a photodiode. Signal-to-image encoding in the study by J. Li et al.
(2022) is an acoustic-optical-signal experiment, and Xie et al. (2025) use actual camera
images.
        DCASE2020 Task2 provides audio-only baselines under an evaluation protocol
that differs from that of later domain shift challenges (Koizumi et al., 2020).
        Together, these studies motivate acoustic representations as candidates for
industrial multimodal monitoring. Their utility will depend on the availability of
appropriate recordings, well-defined targets, and correspondence with other
observations. The thesis retains this scientific motivation even in the absence of a fully
realised predictive audio comparison in the empirical work.


2.3.4     Synchronisation, Correspondence, and Modality

          Availability

Fusion requires observations that refer to a compatible event, region or time interval.
Predicting one modality from another or combining aligned spatial discrepancy maps
requires stronger correspondence.
        RGB-point cloud feature mapping uses registered benchmark geometry. Process
monitoring may instead require time synchronisation and projection onto a common
spatial grid (Costanzino et al., 2024; Petrich et al., 2021).
        The digital twin study maps sensor features to robot TCP positions, showing
how position can establish correspondence even if it is not, in any normal sense, a
predictive feature (L. Chen et al., 2023).
        Modality availability has several levels. A file can exist without having a usable
paired observation for a given prediction unit. A file can have a successfully paired
observation which is still degraded.
        The acoustic-photodiode study explicitly lacks modality registration, which

                                             21
Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


limits claims about correspondence between its two encoded streams (J. Li et al., 2022).
        For camera and machine sensor monitoring, we can have an explicit pairing rule
that picks up unmatched events even if both underlying files are present. Modality
completeness should therefore be assessed on a prediction-unit basis rather than
inferred from the presence or absence of the files.



2.4      Representation Learning for Industrial

         Monitoring — SQ1

SQ1 concerns the relationship between representations and the requirements of an
industrial prediction task.
        The literature reviewed supports both engineered and learned representations.
Which is more appropriate will depend on the level of supervision, the level of
preprocessing, the level of independent sample support, the information required, and
the resources available for model fitting.
        This section sets out the scientific justification for considering these alternatives.
The details of their architecture and implementation are given in Chapter 3.


2.4.1     Engineered Features and Conventional Machine Learning

Engineered summaries impose a constraint on the information that a predictor can use.
Measures of location, spread, amplitude, and trend can describe an operating state
without requiring a high-capacity sequence model to be estimated from a small number
of independent observations.
        The literature is replete with evidence of compact representations. Hand-crafted
local geometry is competitive in 3D inspection, and a hydraulic CNN paper reports
1.7% error for its best single sensor versus 20.5% error for all seventeen sensors
(Goodarzi et al., 2023; Horwitz & Hoshen, 2023).
        These results highlight the necessity for robust conventional references and
careful selection of predictive information. The appropriateness of the specific

                                              22
Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


220-feature REASSEMBLE representation is established through task-specific
comparisons in Chapter 6, rather than assumed based on prior work.
        Industrial vision also offers lightweight alternatives. The weakly supervised
inspection network uses a single camera, internal anomaly-aware features, and a
compact CNN. Reported computational cost is 2.33 million parameters and 5.65 ms per
image (Z. Zhang et al., 2024, p. 6661, Table V).
        The study provides some relevant evidence on representation eﬀiciency, but the
anomalous training labels and modified MVTec supervision differ significantly from
normal-only inspection.
        Conventional and learned representations should be compared using the same
prediction target, assessment observations, and appropriately fitted preprocessing,
rather than ranked solely by model family.


2.4.2     Pretrained Visual Representations

Pretrained visual representations provide a strong scientific explanation for transferring
knowledge learned from large image collections to industrial applications with far less
supervision. The features they learn may well contain the local appearance, texture,
and object configuration relevant to the downstream prediction task.
        PatchCore successfully employs pre-trained, locally aggregated intermediate
CNN features together with a memory of nominal patches for industrial inspection
(Roth et al., 2022, Section 3).
        This provides evidence for investigating transferred spatial representations,
though its nominal-only patch retrieval differs significantly from supervised action
classification.
        RT-DETR provides another architectural precedent in the form of its multi-scale
visual backbone (Y. Zhao et al., 2024, Sections 4.1–4.2). Features at several spatial
scales would be viable candidates for the prediction target.
        Its published detection accuracy and computational performance pertain to the
complete detection architecture rather than the downstream classification performance


                                             23
Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


of the extracted CNN features. Chapter 3 therefore distinguishes between the
components of the first-generation detector and those used in the thesis implementation.
        Asymmetric student-teacher and cross-modal mapping methods also provide
further evidence for task-specific use of the learned visual representations (Costanzino
et al., 2024; Rudolph et al., 2023).
        Vision-language inspection introduces semantic information as another potential
source of representation value. DSE-AD combines historical and current rail images
with normal and abnormal position prompts and assesses pixel localisation on a further
rail domain (Lei & Qi, 2025).
        Its pair-reference and prompt assumptions differ significantly from those of
isolated image classification.
        Together, these studies point to pretrained visual features as plausible
candidates, while also highlighting the need to assess their actual information content,
trainable parameters, and observation horizon in the downstream monitoring task.


2.4.3     Temporal and Time-Series Representations

Temporal representation learning provides an opportunity to retain sequence
information that would otherwise be lost in engineered summaries.
        PatchTST addresses this through patch-based time-series representations and
shared modelling across channels (Nie et al., 2023, Sections 3.1–3.2).
        On the Weather dataset at a forecasting horizon of 96, the patch-plus-channel
independent model obtains an MSE of .152, compared with .164 without patching (Nie
et al., 2023, Table 7).
        This provides positive evidence for patch-based representation learning in the
forecasting setting under consideration. However, the relevance to industrial
classification warrants separate investigation, as the prediction target, normalisation,
supervision, and aggregation differ.
        The exact formulation of PatchTST and its adaptation to the thesis experiments
are given in Chapter 3.


                                            24
Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


         Video representation learning looks at another temporal structure. TimeSformer
and Video Swin model the spatial and temporal relations between video frames
(Bertasius et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2022).
         These approaches provide an alternative to simply averaging independently
encoded frames. The industrial CNN-LSTM remaining useful life study also shows the
importance of observation length within its simulated prediction task (Al-Dulaimi et al.,
2019).
         The literature therefore motivates comparisons that explicitly account for
sequence length, observation windows, temporal ordering, normalisation, pre-training,
and fitting budget.
         A Temporal Transformer and a compact statistical representation could well be
preserving different aspects of the same sensor stream. Looking at their individual and
combined performance helps assess whether the additional representation contains
useful information for the task at hand.


2.4.4      Audio Representations

Acoustic representations include MFCCs, log-mel spectra, wavelet maps, GASF
transforms and learned features.
         The Welding Penetration Network uses VMD and GASF; DMAW uses
Transformer representations adapted to the welding observations (Fan et al., 2026; Hua
et al., 2026).
         These are alternative forms of acoustic representation. An image-shaped acoustic
representation is still one derived from sound rather than being an independently
acquired camera stream.
         The Audio Spectrogram Transformer is a pretrained candidate for acoustic
representation learning (Y. Gong et al., 2021). The results on the general audio
benchmark are suﬀiciently promising to warrant industrial investigation. However, the
appropriateness of such representations will depend heavily on the particular acoustic
environment and the specific prediction task.


                                              25
Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


         MIMII and ToyADMOS both go some way to establishing the importance of
background composition and noise environment (Koizumi et al., 2019; Purohit et al.,
2019).
         Together, these studies lead one to consider recording quality, normalisation, and
consistent observation intervals when looking at audio representations. The
REASSEMBLE correspondence checks precluded the corresponding predictive
comparison in this thesis (Chapter 6).


2.4.5      Frozen Representations, Fine-Tuning, and

           Computational Restrictions

Freezing an encoder, fitting a small prediction head and then adapting the entire
network all consume different amounts of statistical and computing resources.
         Benchmark methods based on pretrained features show that you can get useful
representations without going through the entire original representation-learning
process on the industrial dataset (Costanzino et al., 2024; Roth et al., 2022).
         The computational cost of pretraining is still part of the representation’s
provenance even if it’s external to the downstream experiment.
         Frozen feature extraction can therefore enable comparisons under a constrained
fitting budget. Fine-tuning provides a very different opportunity in that it allows you to
adapt the representations to the particular target observations. Which of the two is
ultimately more effective can only be determined by empirical comparison, rather than
by assuming one approach is in principle better than the other.
         Distillation also makes a further distinction between training and deployment.
The PGMTKD multimodal teachers are used to enrich a pressure-only student. The
gated acoustic-vibration study reports a distilled LightGBM student, though the nature
of the teacher features and the timing of the inputs remain ambiguous (Huang & Tong,
2026; C. Li et al., 2025).
         Resource comparisons should therefore account for feature extraction,
preprocessing, trainable components, and any teachers required during inference, rather

                                             26
Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


than just the final prediction model.
        For SQ1, these results are suﬀicient to justify making explicit comparisons of the
representation and fitting procedures under plausible computing budgets.



2.5      Multimodal Fusion Strategies — SQ2

Fusion strategies can be broken down according to where the information is fused. Early
integration fuses raw observations or raw features. Intermediate integration fuses the
learned representations. Prediction-level fusion fuses the outputs (e.g., class probability
estimates, anomaly scores). Hybrid architectures use more than one level of integration.
        These provide an operational taxonomy for comparing the studies reviewed.
Adaptive reweighting refers to whether the combination varies according to the
observation or its context and can therefore operate at different fusion levels.
        Terminology from the source papers is kept where appropriate, and comparisons
are based on the actual location and mechanism of information exchange.


2.5.1     Unimodal References and Incremental Modality Value

The strongest credible single-input predictor serves as the benchmark for investigating
SQ2.
        The original welding motivation is why this reference is so important. DMAW
fuses the image and audio Transformer representations to classify the four welding
states. The reported accuracy is 96.1% with fusion, 90.2% for the image model and
84.4% for the audio model (Fan et al., 2026, PDF p. 7, Table III).
        This supports a predictive benefit from the assessed multimodal pipeline. The
grouping of the assessment windows by welding run is still not resolved in the
evaluation.
        In contrast, the weld penetration study reports 100% clean test accuracy for its
image-only, audio-only, and fused models (Hua et al., 2026, Tables 1–3).
        The additional modality therefore provides no measurable gain in accuracy


                                            27
Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


under that comparison.
        A similar distinction is made in the case of robotic monitoring. The
HANDOVER study reports 69.1% accuracy for video plus force/torque and 67.9% when
gripper state is added to the I3D-D family (Thoduka et al., 2024b, PDF p. 7, Table III).
        These are five-class outcome accuracies averaged over five training runs, rather
than binary failure average precision. The comparison does, however, show that adding
an observation can reduce predictive performance.
        The tool wear study reports an MAE of 6.6183 for their proposed model, 8.3002
for the visual ResNet50 and 60.5118 for the acoustic reference. All the other
multimodal architectures evaluated have errors in excess of 45 (X. Zhang et al., 2026,
PDF p. 24, Table 1).
        The strong visual reference makes the improvement scientifically informative,
though the architectural differences mean you cannot attribute all of it to fusion.
        Transferable results on the diﬀiculties of multimodal optimisation and on
industrial all-channel losses underline the need to establish incremental gains against a
strong admissible reference (Goodarzi et al., 2023; W. Wang et al., 2020).
        Collectively, these studies support investigating the value of conditional fusion,
rather than assuming that including additional modalities will necessarily improve
prediction.


2.5.2       Feature Concatenation

Feature concatenation combines the representations with no explicit mechanism for
adaptive allocation and is therefore a key reference point for any more sophisticated
form of integration.
        Effectiveness depends on feature scale, dimensionality, regularisation, and the
quality of the constituent representations.
        M3DM’s direct concatenation ablation is worse than its point cloud reference,
while the full hybrid system outperforms both individual inputs (Y. Wang et al., 2023,
Table 4).


                                              28
Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


        MADFlow also provides a further protocol-specific comparison between
alignment and concatenation (Y. Li et al., 2025).
        CFA provides transferable evidence from forecasting. For DLinear on the Energy
dataset, the first concatenation gives an MSE of .670, compared with .263 without text
and .243 with constrained fusion (S. Lee et al., 2026, PDF p. 4, Table 4).
        The constrained fusion method also performs worse in a number of the evaluated
scenarios, which again suggests that the benefits of an integration mechanism are highly
task-dependent.
        Manufacturing fusion can similarly improve one architecture at the expense of
the other under the same broad prediction task (J. Li et al., 2022, Table 3).
        These results establish concatenation as a valid comparator. It should be
appropriately normalised and fitted, rather than implemented as an artificially weak
baseline against which more complex methods are compared.


2.5.3     Prediction-Level and Late Fusion

Prediction-level fusion involves fusing the outputs of modality-specific predictors via
techniques such as averaging, stacking, or evidence aggregation. This is a classification
of where the fusion occurs; the individual predictors could be trained independently or
as part of a wider training regime.
        Thermal-vibration diagnosis provides direct evidence of physical camera-sensor
prediction-level fusion. They use separate CNN softmax outputs with weighted
Dempster-Shafer aggregation and report a clean 100% diagnostic rate, compared with
99.06% for IRT and 96.32% for vibration (Huo et al., 2022, Table VII).
        The weights were fixed, and the sample-level cross-validation did not establish
independence between physical runs.
        HANDOVER compares late outputs with intermediate sensor integration in the
RGB and optical flow branches (Thoduka et al., 2024b, PDF pp. 5–7).
        Cross-modal feature mapping and CPIR combine discrepancy maps, which need
not be probabilities (Costanzino et al., 2024; Shangguan et al., 2025).


                                            29
Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


        Some papers also use the term ’late fusion’ to describe representation-level
integration prior to classification (W. Wang et al., 2020). That underlines the
importance of knowing what information you are actually fusing.
        Stacked generalisation provides a scientific basis for learning a second-stage
combination from cross-validated predictions. Probability outputs contain predictive
information beyond the hard class labels so that a second-stage learner can estimate the
joint utility of the outputs (Ting & Witten, 1999, Sections 2–3).
        The referenced study looks at a linear least-squares combiner; this thesis looks at
logistic stacking. F2 is low-dimensional because it combines two scalar branch
probabilities. Its suitability still requires task-specific evaluation.
        The construction of grouped prediction banks, calibration, and the common
outer assessment remain key to evaluating the learned combination. The exact form of
the F2 and the fitting procedure are given in Chapters 3 and 4 respectively.


2.5.4     Gated and Context-Conditioned Fusion

Adaptive fusion is scientifically justified when the utility of the individual inputs varies
from observation to observation.
        Gated Multimodal Units do provide evidence of transferable conditional
representation integration. The architecture fuses visual and textual information and
achieves a macro F-score of .541, compared with .521 for the concatenation baseline in
the movie-genre classification task (Arevalo et al., 2017, Section 3.1, Table 2).
        This finding points to the need to look at conditional allocation, though the
mechanism at the application and representation level is quite different from the
probability- and logit-based gates that have been the subject of this thesis.
        Adaptive integration has also been looked at in an industrial context. DMAW
achieved 96.1% accuracy, compared with 90.5% for summation and 92.6% for its gate
comparator (Fan et al., 2026, PDF p. 9, Table V).
        TC-MAF provides another approach whereby the auxiliary RGB evidence is
bounded around a multimodal anchor using normal training dispersion and local


                                              30
Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


disagreement (Deng et al., 2026, PDF pp. 3–5).
        These architectures illustrate various approaches to controlling information
contribution.
        An important evaluation dimension is what governs the allocation. GCCF-AD is
described as dynamic fusion, but the formulation they report is a learned scalar with no
explicit sample-quality input (An et al., 2026, p. 149226, Eq. 8).
        PGMTKD uses training losses to assign teacher contributions, and task loss
balancing focuses on optimisation rather than any direct measure of input quality at
deployment (C. Li et al., 2025; X. Zhang et al., 2026).
        GAF-Net uses progressive feature feedback, and trustworthy fusion employs
evidence-based aggregation (Huang & Tong, 2026; Ying et al., 2025).
        The scientific distinction is what contributions can vary, what information
determines that variation, and whether that variation improves predictive performance
relative to a fixed combination.
        Process-conditioned weighting provides an additional way to incorporate
pertinent context, without requiring explicit physical law-constrained modelling.


2.5.5     Comparability and Fairness of Fusion Evaluations

A shared dataset name does not in itself constitute a matched experimental comparison.
        Cohort construction, supervision, observation windows, pretraining, feature
dimensionality, augmentation, and selection exposure could all contribute to differences
between architectures.
        The tool wear study alters the architectural components as part of the fusion,
and the incomplete federated imagery study alters the client data access as well as the
combining mechanism (X. Zhang et al., 2026; X. Zhao et al., 2026).
        These are comparative assessments of fitted pipelines. Their interpretation
differs from an experiment intended to isolate attention, modality contribution, or
quality conditioning.
        Comparisons using the same evidence sources can support a narrower


                                            31
Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


attribution. TC-MAF retains the same evidence sources, comparing bounded fusion
against static averaging, weighted scores, rank fusion, and a learned gate (Deng et al.,
2026, PDF p. 7, Table 3).
        On the flip side, the disagreement-aware study uses a locally released code
baseline that performs worse than the published M3DM result (J. Xu et al., 2026, PDF
p. 10, Table 4).
        Distilled predictors can also access training information that is not available to
standard unimodal models (C. Li et al., 2025).
        These settings remain scientifically valuable, provided that the information and
fitting conditions are clearly specified. Pipeline performance, incremental modality
value, and the isolated effect of a fusion operator represent distinct evaluation questions.



2.6      Reliability and Robustness — SQ3

The reliability of a multimodal architecture depends on both the prediction setting and
the type of intervention acting on its inputs.
        Missing channels, corrupted measurements, asynchronous observations and
changes in operating conditions all represent different reliability issues. The evidence
reviewed is therefore grouped by the particular condition under consideration rather
than assigned to a single general robustness category.


2.6.1     Missing Modalities

Planned single-input deployment differs from an unannounced loss of an input in a
model that normally expects complete observations.
        PGMTKD’s pressure-only student and incomplete RGB-geometry methods do
provide evidence for reduced input inference. ModDrop and the missing-modality
Transformers add further transferable mechanisms and counterexamples (C. Li et al.,
2025; M. Ma et al., 2022; Neverova et al., 2015; Sui et al., 2026).
        The effectiveness of these mechanisms will, of course, depend on the training


                                             32
Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


conditions and the specific missing-input patterns they have been assessed on.
        TC-MAF explicitly evaluates unavailable 3D input. I-AUROC drops from .9788
to .9330 and P-AUPRO from .9901 to .9586 (Deng et al., 2026, PDF p. 7, Table 3).
        This provides a quantified example of performance under a given missing
modality condition.
        Federated substation monitoring varies the proportion of incomplete clients to
80% (X. Zhao et al., 2026, Figure 8).
        Its training and client-availability setting differs from an arbitrary inference-time
camera failure.
        ZSG-IAD reports input subset comparisons without fully defining the shared
checkpoint and training exposure (Q. Chen et al., 2026). Missing training images in the
hot rolling regime constitute another availability regime (L. Ma et al., 2025).
        These studies collectively indicate the need to explicitly specify available
modalities during both training and inference (i.e., availability masks), the policy for
replacement or fallback, and the system’s response when all required inputs are absent.


2.6.2     Noise and Partial Degradation

Physical monitoring studies do provide evidence of a number of controlled degradation
mechanisms. Thermal-vibration diagnosis examines Gaussian noise and vibration
bias/stuck faults, while welding penetration recognition examines artificial interference.
Acoustic-thermal and acoustic-vibration studies look at other noise settings (Hua et al.,
2026; Huang & Tong, 2026; Huo et al., 2022; Ying et al., 2025).
        These interventions vary in the observations affected, the degree of corruption
and the extent to which the model is exposed to degradation.
        At −2 dB, noisy IR thermography gives 95.26%, which jumps to 97.6% when
combined with fault-free vibration. Noisy vibration alone gives 36.86%, jumping to
94.2% when combined with fault-free IR thermography (Huo et al., 2022, Table VIII).
        The comparison shows the value of a fault-free companion modality when
another observation is synthetically corrupted.


                                             33
Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


        The welding penetration study reports a fused accuracy of 93.33% under its
interference condition (Hua et al., 2026, Table 3).
        The corresponding corrupted unimodal comparisons are required in order to
assess the incremental benefit of fusion in the interference condition. The reported
intervention also leaves some uncertainty about the corruption operator, magnitude,
and exposure matching.
        Synthetic signal-to-noise interventions provide some controlled experimental
evidence, but the conclusions are specific to the observations affected and the severities
tested. Natural simultaneous degradation and ordinary defective objects are quite
different phenomena.
        These distinctions lead directly to a comparison of predictions from degraded
input against those from static fusion and single-input predictors under the same
reliability condition.


2.6.3     Temporal Misalignment and Distribution Shift

Temporal misalignment and distribution shift both affect multimodal prediction, albeit
in different ways.
        A clock offset can break correspondence between observations, whereas a
distribution shift can change the conditions under which the learned representation or
predictor is evaluated.
        Registration studies have established the importance of alignment in multimodal
monitoring. OmniFuser’s repeated within-pass images provide a way to construct paired
observations rather than directly test a synchronisation failure (L. Chen et al., 2023;
Petrich et al., 2021; Z. Wang et al., 2025).
        The literature also contains examples of evaluation under changed acquisition
conditions. HANDOVER trains on one robot and evaluates on a different robot, and
there’s a rail localisation study that evaluates a Subway-trained model on high-speed
rail (Thoduka et al., 2024b, PDF p. 7, Table V); (Lei & Qi, 2025, PDF p. 10, Table III).
        Target-adaptive methods, target-validated condition studies and independent


                                               34
Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


build experiments are all different forms of access to the assessment domain (Bevans
et al., 2023; Chu et al., 2024; Huang & Tong, 2026; C. Wang et al., 2025).
        Rebuilding normal memories on Eyecandies using fixed fusion hyperparameters
is evidence of recipe transfer, rather than deploying an unaltered model to an
untouched target installation (Deng et al., 2026; J. Xu et al., 2026).
        These distinctions between them are important for interpreting the external
dataset work in this thesis. Architecture reuse, representation transfer, temporal offset
tolerance, and unchanged model generalisation each require distinct evaluation
protocols.


2.6.4     Quality-Aware and Uncertainty-Aware Fusion

Quality-aware fusion is driven by the idea that the predictive utility of an input may
vary with its quality. Signal quality, model confidence, calibrated probability and
evidence consistency are all different beasts, though.
        QMF furnishes a theoretical basis for studying sample-dependent weighting via
generalisation upper bounds under stated relationships between uncertainty and loss,
assuming a common training and test distribution (Q. Zhang et al., 2023,
Sections 3.1–3.2).
        The transferable UDML experiments train on controlled Gaussian corruption
intensities and recognise that sample diﬀiculty can be confused with modality
corruption (Wei et al., 2026, PDF pp. 4–5, 9).
        These results lead us to ask whether the quality information we have available
helps us identify when a particular observation is no longer useful.
        QMF reports an NYU scene classification accuracy of 70.09% on clean inputs,
compared to 70.30% for concatenation. At corruption intensity ten, it reports 55.60%
compared to 51.99% for late fusion (Q. Zhang et al., 2023, PDF p. 8, Table 1).
        These non-industrial RGB-depth comparisons do show a regime-specific
advantage under corruption. The clean and corrupted comparisons quoted use different
reference methods, so they should not be read as a clean-versus-corrupted trade-off


                                            35
Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


against the same reference method.
        The effect of the adaptation mechanisms also differs across prediction endpoints.
The disagreement add-on increases the local MVTec image AUROC from .779 to .800
and, at the same time, reduces the Eyecandies image AUROC from .810 to .794, even
though the pixel metrics have improved (J. Xu et al., 2026, PDF pp. 10–11, Tables 4–5).
        TC-MAF uses normal-training dispersion and local disagreement to control the
contribution of auxiliary evidence (Deng et al., 2026).
        Collectively, these findings establish a scientific basis for reducing reliance on a
stream when the observable context is expected to indicate errors. They also underscore
the need to assess the relationship between available quality indicators and actual
predictive reliability.
        QMF’s theoretical justification does not validate the use of arbitrary quality
proxies, and clean calibration can actually degrade after a distribution shift (Ovadia
et al., 2019; Q. Zhang et al., 2023).
        For the empirical investigation, the relevant comparison is whether
quality-conditioned weighting improves performance over a static model with the same
level of corruption exposure, while still maintaining an acceptable level of clean
performance.


2.6.5     Modality Dropout, Augmentation, and Fallback

          Mechanisms

Different robustness mechanisms deal with different aspects of input availability.
        Training-time modality dropout exposes the model to certain absence patterns.
Imputation produces substitute observations, and deterministic fallbacks route the
prediction to an available unimodal component.
        These approaches can generate similar outputs under certain conditions without
relying on the same learned recovery mechanism.
        Fuzzy regularisation works by introducing failures via partial corruption and by
replacing the entire input with a constant or noise. The evaluation is of Gazebo pose

                                              36
Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


regression using random sample splits of overlapping windows (Altinses & Schwung,
2023, PDF pp. 3–5).
        The investigation provides evidence for controlled input regularisation, with
independent physical deployment outage performance remaining unaddressed.
        OmniFuser describes dropout and masking without any quantified outage table
(Z. Wang et al., 2025, PDF p. 10).
        Generated replacement observations can also have a negative impact on
performance. Replacing the clean depth with CycleGAN output worsens the synthetic
robot benchmark’s MSE (from 2.1745 × 10−3 to 13.472 × 10−3 ) (Altinses & Schwung,
2025, Table 7).
        These results motivate individual evaluation of learned recovery, training
exposure, and explicit fallback policies. Static controls, with equivalent degradation
exposure and predictions from the surviving input, provide a useful baseline for
assessing adaptive robustness.



2.7       Evaluation Beyond Accuracy — SQ4

The practical value of a monitoring architecture will depend entirely on how its
predictions are evaluated, chosen, and interpreted.
        Accuracy in prediction can be affected by class distribution, assessment grouping
and decision thresholds. A single aggregate score therefore only provides one facet of
the evidence needed to assess the performance of a multimodal industrial monitoring
system.
        SQ4 looks at assessment independence, discrimination, calibration, uncertainty,
interpretability, computational viability and reproducibility.


2.7.1     Grouped Evaluation and Leakage Prevention

The independent assessment unit may be a weld, a tool, a participant, a recording, a
machine or a build.


                                            37
Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


        Randomly partitioned observations can end up with shared acquisition
contextual information or raw samples. The fuzzy-regularisation study forms 20-step
windows with a stride of one and then randomly divides the resulting examples
(Altinses & Schwung, 2023, PDF pp. 4–5).
        Its assessment therefore doesn’t establish performance on unseen trajectories.
        Weld run grouping is still unresolved in the closest welding studies (Fan et al.,
2026; Hua et al., 2026). These examples do show a potential dependence between the
observations but do not establish any particular level of leakage.
        The HANDOVER authors report an explicit participant-disjoint oﬀicial split.
An adapted learned fusion implementation would still require participant information in
order to build the corresponding inner partitions (Thoduka et al., 2024b).
        Normalisation, feature selection and model parameter optimisation all require an
appropriate split between model development and model assessment (Kapoor &
Narayanan, 2023; Varma & Simon, 2006).
        Out-of-fold stacking provides a mechanism for constructing second-stage
prediction inputs without any in-sample predictions. Its validity is of course also
dependent on the way in which the underlying fitting, selection and calibration folds are
constructed.
        Repeatedly selecting follow-up experiments after looking at the assessment
results introduces further selection bias, even if the individual model fits would have
ruled out their outer test labels.
        These results motivate the development of assessment protocols that account for
the physical origin and dependence of observations throughout the modelling process.


2.7.2     Discrimination, Calibration, and Decision Thresholds

Ranking, probability quality and thresholded decisions are three different aspects of a
predictor.
        AUROC measures discrimination across thresholds rather than performance at a
single operational false alarm rate or the quality of the predicted probabilities.


                                            38
Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


        MVTec AD shows that useful ranking can exist alongside fairly poor thresholded
localisation. The strict FPR metrics highlight a different aspect of detection
performance (Bergmann et al., 2019; Cheng et al., 2025; Costanzino et al., 2024).
        The rail vision-language study reports pixel-level metrics and optimal threshold
F1 (Lei & Qi, 2025, PDF p. 7). These metrics address a completely different evaluation
problem than image-level alarm accuracy at a threshold chosen from the training or
validation set.
        In manufacturing, Petrich et al. (2021) report an accuracy of .9921 and a
precision of .2487 on an imbalanced layer, which is a clear example of the impact of
class distribution and threshold selection.
        Validation-fitted calibration can improve in-distribution probability quality, while
dataset shift can damage confidence estimates (Guo et al., 2017; Ovadia et al., 2019).
        Proper scoring rules (negative log-likelihood, Brier score, etc.) provide a basis for
assessing probability forecasts (Gneiting & Raftery, 2007). Their interpretation differs
from that of discrimination or thresholded classification.
        A generated confidence field or evidence mass cannot replace probability
evaluation. Metrics should align with the prediction target and class distribution, and
operational thresholds should be selected using admissible training or validation
observations.


2.7.3     Uncertainty and Statistical Comparisons

Seed variation, acquisition group sampling and model selection uncertainty are all
different sources of variation.
        HANDOVER’s reported standard deviations are for five training runs on a fixed
test set, whereas a group bootstrap of saved predictions samples assessment units
conditional on the fitted models (Thoduka et al., 2024b).
        These approaches capture different facets of uncertainty and do not necessarily
encompass all the variation expected from retraining or deployment in a different
domain.


                                              39
Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


        Cross-validation folds share training observations. Under the assumptions stated,
the classic variance result shows that no universally unbiased estimator of
cross-validation variance exists; ignoring covariance can substantially underestimate
uncertainty (Bengio & Grandvalet, 2004, pp. 1096–1098, 1102–1104). Conditional
uncertainty intervals can still be useful when the estimand and dependence assumptions
are stated explicitly.
        Small fusion effects, ambiguous repetition counts and conflicting descriptions of
significance all warrant the same level of caution (Gu et al., 2024; J. Xu et al., 2025).
        Paired performance differences on common assessment observations provide a
much more direct comparison than unrelated score intervals. An interval which contains
zero doesn’t demonstrate equivalence, and a positive point estimate doesn’t establish
practical importance without an appropriate criterion.


2.7.4     Interpretability and Its Limits

Prediction scores, coeﬀicients, gate traces and spatial maps can all tell you something
regarding how the model is behaving.
        The attention debate has both counterexamples to faithful explanation and
evidence that the learned attention weights do, in fact, contain useful information. This
leads to the conclusion that interpretability should be evaluated on a task-specific basis
rather than drawing general conclusions from the terminology of the architecture itself
(Wiegreffe & Pinter, 2019).
        ZSG-IAD measures schema validity, language consistency and
coarse-to-final-mask overlap (Q. Chen et al., 2026). These provide evidence of various
forms of self-consistency.
        Their interpretation differs from both independent physical defect causation and
calibrated confidence.
        HANDOVER leaves the use of human action prediction as a means of
explanation as a future area of research (Thoduka et al., 2024b).
        Within this thesis, observable quality responses and inspectable fusion


                                            40
Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


coeﬀicients provide descriptive transparency. However, expert understanding, causal
attribution, and operator usability require additional assessments.
        Process context should be distinguished from the explicit physical equation
constraints (e.g. PGMTKD’s pressure model (C. Li et al., 2025)).


2.7.5     Modularity, Reproducibility, and Computational Cost

Modular architectures enable you to swap in and out individual representations and
prediction components and compare them. Their value for scientific investigation comes
from the fact that you can identify which component is different between conditions.
        Modularity does not establish predictive robustness or an advantage over a
matched end-to-end architecture.
        An identified version, accessible source code, executable processing and
independently reproduced results all provide various levels of reproducibility assurance
(Pineau et al., 2021).
        The difference between local and published M3DM results remains unresolved. It
motivates checks of dependencies, valid point filtering, checkpoints, and scoring
conventions (J. Xu et al., 2026).
        Computational comparisons of course also require a common measurement
boundary. The CPIR paper reports lower FPS than CFM, which is a clear performance
vs speed trade-off (Shangguan et al., 2025).
        The reported 5.65 ms for the lightweight image network doesn’t include the
independent sensor branch or acquisition delay (Z. Zhang et al., 2024, p. 6661, Table V).
        TimeSformer’s compute requirements depend on the ImageNet pretraining and
the workload it is evaluated on (Bertasius et al., 2021).
        Hardware, batch size, caching, synchronisation and the processing stages
included all determine which computational measurements can be compared.
        These results indicate the necessity for explicit resource accounting and
reproducibility documentation. Prospective deployment and operator usability remain
open questions for validating a successful offline forward pass.

2.8       Critical Synthesis and Research Gap

The reviewed literature shows that multimodal observations and learned representations
can benefit industrial and robotic monitoring. Their value, however, depends on the
prediction task, the information each representation contains, and the conditions under
which the integration method is evaluated.
         The previous sections have identified four related scientific questions: which
representations are appropriate for a given target, whether combining their predictions
is valuable, whether that value is maintained as observation quality degrades, and how
the experimental assessments should be conducted.
         This section synthesises existing findings and outstanding scientific questions to
establish design requirements for the empirical work.


2.8.1      Findings Supported Across the Reviewed Studies

There are tangible observations that can be used to improve the performance of a
task-specific monitoring pipeline. DMAW, thermal-vibration diagnosis, HANDOVER
and tool wear prediction are all good examples under their respective assessment
protocols (Fan et al., 2026; Huo et al., 2022; Thoduka et al., 2024b; X. Zhang et al.,
2026).
         Controlled evidence integration can also improve RGB–geometry inspection
under intact-input conditions. TC-MAF separately reports performance when the 3D
input is unavailable (Deng et al., 2026).
         The literature therefore establishes that useful industrial multimodal fusion
mechanisms and approaches to dealing with incomplete observations do in fact exist.
         Representation learning offers a number of scientifically sound options for
extracting information relevant to the task. Pretrained convolutional features,
patch-based temporal representations, and hand-crafted features are all candidates with
some supporting evidence under certain learning and evaluation regimes (Horwitz &
Hoshen, 2023; Nie et al., 2023; Roth et al., 2022).



                                             42
Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


        Their suitability for another industrial prediction target will, of course, depend
on several factors, including the observations, the supervision, the aggregation, and the
compute resources involved.
        Static and learned fusion mechanisms both offer different avenues for exploiting
complementary information. Stacked generalisation motivates learning combinations of
the outputs of individual predictors. In contrast, gated representation learning and
quality-aware fusion justify context-dependent allocation (Arevalo et al., 2017; Ting &
Witten, 1999; Q. Zhang et al., 2023).
        A further well-established distinction concerns input reliability. Planned
single-input deployment, complete inference-time absence, controlled corruption, and
target domain adaptation all address different issues (Altinses & Schwung, 2023; C. Li
et al., 2025; X. Zhao et al., 2026).
        The consistent finding across the reviewed studies is a conditional benefit under
specific tasks and evaluation regimes, rather than universal superiority of any single
representation or fusion architecture.


2.8.2       Unresolved Questions and Research Gap

The evidence reviewed also contains significant negative and, indeed, conflicting
findings.
        The same clean-test accuracy is observed for the unimodal and fused welding
models. HANDOVER performance drops when the gripper state is added. Some
alternative multimodal tool-wear models perform worse than the visual reference,
although the proposed model improves on it. The Eyecandies image scores also go
down. All of this calls into question the notion of an unqualified fusion benefit (Hua
et al., 2026; Thoduka et al., 2024b; J. Xu et al., 2026; X. Zhang et al., 2026).
        Category-specific geometry losses, poor hydraulic channel combinations, and the
diﬀiculties of multimodal optimisation all reinforce this conclusion (Bonfiglioli et al.,
2023; Goodarzi et al., 2023; Horwitz & Hoshen, 2023; W. Wang et al., 2020).
        Several mechanisms could account for these results: redundancy, inappropriate


                                             43
Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


representations, optimisation diﬀiculties, noise, supervision and limited independent
sample support. The studies do not point to a single underlying explanation.
       An important unresolved issue is the distinction between a representation’s
standalone strength and its complementary predictive value. A representation that is
weak in isolation may still provide information useful for another predictor. Conversely,
a strong additional representation does not guarantee that a fusion mechanism can
effectively utilise that information.
       Reliability introduces a related source of uncertainty. Adaptive weighting is
justified when the available context can identify changes in unimodal prediction
reliability (Wei et al., 2026; Q. Zhang et al., 2023). The value of conditioning
information, its relationship to branch errors, and its comparison with
exposure-matched static alternatives must be investigated on a task-specific basis.
       External evaluation adds another boundary. Re-using a procedure with a new
dataset-specific fitting, re-using the learned representation weights and then evaluating
an unaltered predictor in a different domain are all different experimental questions.
You cannot just take the results of one and treat them as equivalent evidence of
generalisation.

Research gap

Existing work shows that multimodal representations and fusion techniques can improve
performance in industrial and robotic monitoring under certain experimental conditions.
The body of work also shows that additional observations and more complex fusion
techniques do not necessarily outperform a very good unimodal or static baseline. The
degree to which the results can be generalised across applications is limited by
differences in the prediction target, the quality of the representation, the nature of the
supervision, the training regime and the reliability of the inputs (Goodarzi et al., 2023;
Hua et al., 2026; Thoduka et al., 2024b; W. Wang et al., 2020; Q. Zhang et al., 2023).
       This thesis addresses the conditional effectiveness of multimodal fusion under
comparable, task-specific experimental conditions. It examines how representation
suitability, complementary predictive information, fusion complexity, and input
reliability interact. It also asks whether benefits observed in one study transfer to other


                                             45
Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


industrial and robotic monitoring tasks. Rather than assuming that one architecture is
universally best, the thesis identifies which approaches provide benefits under the
conditions tested and assesses how far those findings can be generalised.
        This is defined by the specific unresolved comparisons in the reviewed evidence.
It by no means suggests that multimodal fusion, adaptive weighting, or robust
industrial monitoring have not been examined previously.


2.8.3     Derived Research Design Requirements

The previous evidence and the remaining questions address five requirements for the
research design regarding multimodal monitoring architectures.
        These requirements take the findings from the literature and turn them into
evaluation criteria relevant to the issues of representation, fusion, robustness, and
methodology raised in the original research proposal.
        They provide an analytical framework for assessing the empirical work, rather
than claiming that every historical experiment was originally designed in accordance
with those exact specifications.


R1. Target validity and modality correspondence

The prediction target must have a clear physical or operational interpretation. Its origin,
observation horizon, and relationship to the available inputs must be explicitly defined.
        Native outcome annotations, independent reference measurements, and
analytically derived proxies must be distinguished. For multimodal observations, the
matching procedure must determine whether they correspond to a compatible event or
observation interval.
        This directly follows from the supervision and correspondence distinctions
discussed in Sections 2.2 and 2.3 (including the industrial studies that use independent
reference instruments or registered observations (L. Chen et al., 2023; Pandiyan et al.,
2022; Petrich et al., 2021)).




                                            46
Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


R2. Representation suitability under explicit fitting conditions

Modality-specific representations should be evaluated using clearly defined observation
windows, aggregation schemes, preprocessing, supervision, pretraining, and fitting
budgets.
       Conventional references and learned representations should be compared on the
same prediction targets and assessment observations. Where possible, assess
complementary usefulness separately from standalone performance.
       This is a direct consequence of evidence for engineered features, transferred
visual representations, and patch-based temporal learning under a variety of
experimental conditions (Horwitz & Hoshen, 2023; Nie et al., 2023; Roth et al., 2022).
       It directly supports the representation investigation in SQ1.


R3. Incremental fusion value under comparable experimental conditions

Fusion architectures should be benchmarked against credible unimodal references and
simple integration approaches on the same assessment observations.
       The impact of any additional information should be disentangled, where possible,
from changes in model capacity, fitted probability transformations, calibration, and
training.
       For prediction-level stacking, we need grouped out-of-fold prediction
construction and appropriate separation of fitting and assessment. Incremental
within-fold ordering must be distinguished from pooled score alignment.
       A matched single-input second-stage control can further reduce ambiguity about
the need for any additional fitting. Where such a control is not available, the resulting
attribution boundary must remain explicit.
       This is a direct consequence of the positive and negative fusion comparisons and
the methodological underpinnings of stacked generalisation (Hua et al., 2026; Thoduka
et al., 2024b; Ting & Witten, 1999; W. Wang et al., 2020).
       It directly supports SQ2.




                                            47
Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


R4. Reliability-aware comparison of fusion mechanisms

Known modality absence, degraded (but available) inputs, temporal misalignment and
distribution shift should all be considered as separate reliability conditions.
       The impacted observations, corruption procedures, severity, training exposure,
availability information and fallback policies all need to be documented.
       When evaluating adaptive weighting, it should be compared against appropriate
static alternatives and available unimodal references. A comparable level of exposure to
corruption is necessary to determine whether improvements result from adaptation or
from differences in training conditions.
       Clean performance should be used as a benchmark when assessing improvements
in robustness.
       This is a direct consequence of the missing input studies, the controlled industrial
degradation studies, and the theoretical underpinning of quality-aware weighting
(Altinses & Schwung, 2023; Deng et al., 2026; Huo et al., 2022; Q. Zhang et al., 2023).
       It directly supports SQ3.


R5. Methodological validity and bounded cross-dataset interpretation

Evaluation should account for dependence between observations, the prediction target,
class distribution, discrimination, calibration, uncertainty, computational requirements,
and reproducibility.
       Where group identifiers are available, assessment and model development
procedures should align with the relevant physical or operational grouping.
       External evaluations must be able to distinguish between dataset-specific
refitting, supervised representation transfer, and unchanged model generalisation.
Differences between datasets should be interpreted in terms of their targets, observation
windows, available information, and assessment protocols, rather than through a
common ranking of performance scores that are inherently incompatible.
       This is a direct consequence of the literature on assessment independence,
calibration, selection uncertainty, reproducibility and target domain access (Bengio &


                                             48
Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


Grandvalet, 2004; Guo et al., 2017; Kapoor & Narayanan, 2023; Ovadia et al., 2019;
Pineau et al., 2021; Varma & Simon, 2006).
        It is primarily an SQ4 product and forms the basis on which the SQ1–SQ3
evaluations are carried out.
        The requirements stipulate a direct link between the literature review and the
following technical and methodological chapters.
        Chapter 3 covers the representation and fusion elements pertinent to R2–R4.
Chapter 4 details the dataset admission, fitting, assessment and evaluation procedures
necessary to investigate R1–R5.
        Not every dataset available to us will support all the requirements to the same
degree. In particular, independent physical reference measurements, full modality
correspondence, assessment group identifiers, matched architectural controls and
reliability experiments may all be missing.
        Where a requirement cannot be fully met, the corresponding empirical
conclusion must be qualified rather than deemed satisfied.

3.2.2     RT-DETR Architecture and Component Roles

RT-DETR is a real-time detection architecture with a convolutional backbone, a hybrid
encoder for multiscale feature interaction, and a query-based decoder that predicts
detections (Y. Zhao et al., 2024). These blocks have different purposes. Backbone maps
encode local and hierarchical image patterns. The hybrid encoder combines within- and
across-scale information. The decoder and prediction heads produce object-level
outputs. A checkpoint trained for detection may supply weights to any chosen subset of
these blocks, but the checkpoint’s origin does not establish which blocks a downstream
program executes.

3.2.3     Backbone Extraction Versus Hybrid-Encoder or

          Full-Detector Execution

The core REASSEMBLE visual extractor uses the ResNet18-vd CNN backbone inside
the pretrained RT-DETR-R18vd checkpoint (PekingU/rtdetr_r18vd). After loading
the detector, the implementation selects its backbone and freezes its weights. It
spatially averages three feature maps to form an 896-dimensional descriptor per frame,
then averages the descriptors of 16 frames. A separately trained binary head predicts
the action outcome. This forward path bypasses the hybrid Transformer encoder,
detection decoder, and box heads.
        PHM’s scalar-regression route is different. It invokes the Ultralytics prediction
path with a frozen RT-DETR-L model and hooks three multiscale maps. Pooling
produces 768 features; the saved extraction adds latency, making the head input
769-dimensional. The PHM head therefore receives both image content and a timing
covariate. REASSEMBLE calls only the detector’s CNN backbone for the core
comparison. Later CNN-versus-spatial-encoder studies are additional recipes with their
own feature widths and budgets.


3.2.4     Frame Sampling, Feature Aggregation, and Temporal

          Modelling

Fixed sampling selects a reproducible number of frames across a complete action or
episode. It makes sequence cost predictable and prevents longer clips automatically
producing more visual tokens, but can miss brief contact or failure events. A frame
mean is invariant to permutation. Boundary pooling or temporal bins retain some
coarse position; a GRU or temporal Transformer learns order-dependent interactions.
Mean pooling is a low-capacity reference for an outcome attached to a complete
window; it does not require frame-level labels. Boundary pooling tests whether a coarse
position restriction preserves more useful evidence than equal weighting. TimeSformer’s
divided spatial–temporal attention outperforms its other tested attention schemes on


                                            56
Chapter 3. Technical Foundations and Candidate Models


Kinetics-400 and Something-Something V2, motivating investigation of temporal
organization (Bertasius et al., 2021, Table 1), but does not justify a particular boundary
rule. Matched sampling and fitting are needed before attributing a difference to learned
temporal modelling.
          The core REASSEMBLE route uses 16 deterministic hand-camera frames resized
to 640 × 640, divided by 255, without additional ImageNet mean/standard-deviation
normalization. These are actual recipe choices rather than generic defaults for every
RT-DETR implementation. Dataset examples in Chapter 6 show sampled observations;
they are illustrative, not independent evidence of label correctness or a performance
effect.


3.2.5       Frozen Extraction and Visual Fine-Tuning

Frozen extraction keeps encoder parameters fixed while fitting a head. Fine-tuning
changes some or all encoder parameters. A comparison also depends on
normalization-layer state, augmentation, learning rates, early stopping, batch size, and
optimization steps. A fixed embedding cache can make nested evaluation practical; an
end-to-end model may require a new backbone fit inside every inner split. The
additional computational burden is not a reason to treat an unmatched budget
comparison as an isolated test of freezing.
          Generic COCO initialization, REASSEMBLE donor initialization, and
target-domain head fitting are distinct interventions. A cached donor embedding proves
that extraction occurred; it does not prove that a downstream classifier and fusion
comparison completed. In ViFailback, donor feature extraction preceded the completed
downstream fits. Chapter 8 compares those fits with the matched target-task control.

3.3.3     Patching, Sequence Length, Padding, and Aggregation

For sequence length L, patch width P , and stride S, an unpadded regular grid contains
1 + ⌊(L − P )/S⌋ patches when L ≥ P . Padding conventions can change that count. A
patch embedding projects its values to a hidden vector, adds positional information,
and passes the sequence through Transformer blocks. Pooling must specify whether all
patches or only valid patches contribute.
        REASSEMBLE resamples the action to 512 progress positions and 22 channels.
Its scratch classifier uses width 32, two layers, four attention heads, feed-forward width
64, patch width 32, and stride 16, followed by patch averaging and channel
concatenation before a 128-dimensional projection. Scaling is disabled inside the model
because standardization is supplied externally. The implementation passes an
observed-value mask to the model. The call alone does not establish that attention
excludes every missing position.
        PHM’s custom default uses patch 16 and stride 8 with variable-length minute
sequences. Its patch validity is based on any valid time within a patch, followed by
masked aggregation. The source raises a batch-length-dependent grid concern; its
numerical effect on historical scores remains unmeasured. PHM also predicts sorted
profiles with positive increments, which remove tooth identity. That target
representation cannot support a tooth-localization claim.

interchangeable substitutes. TS2Vec uses a dilated CNN with hierarchical contrastive
learning; substituting a Transformer in its backbone ablation lowers average
classification accuracy (Yue et al., 2022). MOMENT pretrains a masked-reconstruction

3.3.4    Representation Learning Versus Direct Prediction

A learned encoder can be used to export an embedding, to predict the target directly, or
to support a fusion head. These roles differ in where supervision enters. An encoder
trained on all labels before a cross-validation split leaks target information even if a
later classifier is refitted correctly. Conversely, a generic frozen encoder can be cached
without using target labels, provided the cache remains tied to the correct sample
identities and preprocessing. Checking for leakage in each fold requires identifying
which samples were used to fit the preprocessing steps and which samples and labels
were used to train the encoder and the final predictor.



3.4     Prediction Models and Reference Baselines

3.4.1    Constant and Simple Reference Predictors

A regression constant estimated from training data establishes whether a learned model
improves on a basic location predictor under the selected loss. The training mean
minimizes squared error, while the median minimizes absolute error in the training
sample. For classification, a constant probability or a process-context model can expose
target prevalence and contextual predictability. These controls are particularly
important when a condition proxy is correlated with run order or when action identity
predicts failure frequency.

Training-time dropout can affect the gate’s behavior when both inputs are present. In

4.6     Grouping, Data Splits, and Leakage Prevention

The split unit determines the scope of the generalization claim. The principal PHM
sensor protocol holds out gear experiments, REASSEMBLE groups actions by
recording, MSM examines experiment and date groupings, and ViFailback separates
tasks. HANDOVER retains the published trial partitions. The available identifiers
allow neither independent verification of the reported participant separation nor
construction of participant-disjoint inner folds. For MSM, a date can span multiple
tools or operators, and physical equipment may recur across dates. A claim about a
new machine or factory requires grouping information at that level.
       Within an outer split, feature scaling, imputations estimated from data, model
selection, calibration, thresholds, and fusion fitting must use training-side information.
For stacking, the training bank contains predictions from models that did not fit the
corresponding labels. If a branch recipe is selected, that selection also belongs inside the
permitted training hierarchy. Even with an out-of-fold prediction head, assessment
information can enter the model through an encoder or normalizer fitted on the full
dataset. Each base prediction for the original REASSEMBLE stacker came from a
branch fitted without data from the corresponding recording. Within each outer split,
internal stacker validation reused the same out-of-fold (OOF) prediction bank built
from the outer training recordings. A branch that supplied a prediction for a stacker
training row could have been fitted on recordings held out for that stacker validation
split. The outer-assessment recordings remained excluded from fitting. Full
independence at the deeper validation stage has yet to be demonstrated. This
dependency limits the claim of strict nesting.
       The original REASSEMBLE cohort comprises 4,530 actions, 509 failures, and
148 recordings. Its five held-out partitions together cover this population. A later
753-action comparison uses one of these exposed outer partitions, not a new external
holdout. Cohort reconciliation uses sample and recording identities to distinguish
overlapping and identical populations. An earlier summary listed 28 recordings. The
sample identifiers establish that this subset contains 26.

4.7     Model Fitting, Selection, Calibration, and

        Threshold Procedures

Each comparison defines trainable components, initialization, selection candidates,
training budget, and stopping rules. An epoch count alone does not establish matched
optimization if samples, batch sizes, early stopping, or cached representations differ.
The experimental programme included original comparisons, exploratory pilots and
matched short-budget diagnostics. Some larger experiments completed only a subset of
the planned fits.
       Calibration maps raw model scores to probabilities using training-side
predictions. Discrimination and calibration are different properties: a monotonic score
transformation can preserve ranking while changing probability errors. The core branch
calibrators use sigmoid logistic maps on selected inner out-of-fold scores. The original
softmax gates have no free final intercept or post-gate calibrator, whereas F2 has a free
intercept and no additional post-stacker calibration. These function-class and
post-processing choices remain visible in the comparison (Guo et al., 2017).
       A decision threshold is selected using permitted training predictions and a stated
objective, such as balanced accuracy. The original implementation searches a grid from
.01 to .99. Such a threshold is a protocol choice, not a cost-optimized industrial alarm
policy. At that threshold, sensitivity, specificity, precision, and false-alert burden give a
clearer account of performance than accuracy alone. Assessing operational acceptance
also requires information about stakeholder costs.

4.9     Robustness Evaluation Design

A robustness test specifies how the failure occurs, which modality and stage it affects,
and how severe it is. It also reports the number of realizations, any exposure to the
failure during training, and the observations and metric used. The availability mask
identifies a stream declared missing. A corrupted stream can remain available and
supply a prediction. Corrupting pixels targets a different stage from corrupting cached
visual features. Sensor corruption can affect raw channels, statistics, or logits.
Recompute any quality features meant to describe the corrupted observation.
       Clean and degraded absolute scores are both needed. A smaller clean-relative
drop can coexist with worse absolute performance. A gate can shift weight away from a
corrupted modality while still underperforming the intact unimodal predictor. The
relevant controls include the surviving branch, a static fusion model with matched
augmentation exposure, and any simpler parameterization needed to isolate adaptation.
Training with modality dropout establishes exposure to missing inputs. Recovery is
measured on held-out cases where the input is absent.
       The original two-input gate routes a single surviving branch with weight one.
With fixed branch outputs this makes single-input predictions identical regardless of
gate training. Both missing inputs raise an error; no predictive result is attributed to
that state. These tests leave open the effects of clock offsets, previously unseen
machines and novel failure mechanisms.

4.11      Computational Constraints and Exploratory

          Versus Confirmatory Analysis

Resource limits motivated cached features, compact heads, small selection grids, and
bounded follow-ups. Those choices define the tested system. They cannot justify
declaring an undertrained architecture intrinsically unsuitable or an unfinished arm
inferior. Timings must state whether they measure warm extraction, a head fit, or an
end-to-end pipeline, with device and preprocessing included where available.
       The work is primarily exploratory and retrospective. Repeated exposure to the
same assessment cohort, follow-ups selected after inspecting results, and later protocol
adjustments limit confirmatory interpretation. A future confirmatory study should
freeze targets, cohort construction, selection rules, primary contrasts, corruption
operators, and claim criteria before the assessment is opened. The conclusions are
conditional on this exploratory development history and on the saved evidence available
for each study.

5.6.1     Motivation and Target Definition

Without organizer-supplied continuous damage labels, an explicit and reproducible
candidate-area statistic offered an engineering starting point. The implementation
measures dark, horizontally extended regions within a chosen flank ROI. Its percentage
denominator is the ROI area, so a change in ROI changes the numerical scale even if
the same pixels are selected. The term “candidate” is substantive: reflectance,
machining texture and acquisition artifacts may satisfy the image rule without being
physical damage. The target can consequently support a study of proxy prediction and
measurement sensitivity. It cannot by itself support a claim about tooth material loss
or maintenance necessity.


5.6.2     Image Processing and Aggregation

The inspected target code converts RGB to Lab luminance, applies CLAHE, estimates
a smooth background, and obtains a dark residual. Robust standardized residuals
define candidate pixels; morphological opening and closing and component-size/aspect
criteria retain extended regions. Candidate pixels divided by ROI pixels give the
per-view percentage. Historical v2 view maxima and the v3 deterministic-view rule then
produce tooth values, followed by the top-three run mean.
        The PHM scalar baseline invoked the Ultralytics RT-DETR-L prediction path
and recorded intermediate maps from three layers. The regression head used 768
spatially pooled image features and one additional value, extraction latency. Latency
depends on the computing environment and can confound interpretation of the
image-regression result. Separate PHM detection and multitask experiments used
heuristic pseudo-box supervision. The core REASSEMBLE classifier used the
ResNet18-vd CNN backbone inside its RT-DETR-R18vd checkpoint (Chapter 6).

6.1       Study Objectives and Research-Question

          Mapping

SQ1 concerns implemented visual and temporal representations relative to engineered
sensor statistics. SQ2 concerns early, late and adaptive fusion under shared recording
groups. SQ3 distinguishes known missing modalities, synthetic degradation of a present
modality and broader distribution shifts. SQ4 concerns the controls needed to interpret
these comparisons: native labels, group separation, inner selection, calibration, paired
uncertainty and computational budgets. The evaluation therefore includes simple
reference predictors and a strong unimodal competitor before interpreting multimodal
gains.
         The task is retrospective classification of an already completed action. It is
relevant to monitoring and automated inspection of industrial manipulation, but it is
not a direct replication of rotating-machine damage diagnosis. The observation horizon
includes the action’s end. Consequently, a representation that exploits late frames can
be useful here without demonstrating prediction before an unsuccessful event. The
dataset paper supplies acquisition context; the retained manifests and implementation
establish this thesis’s specific population and task (Sliwowski et al., 2025).

6.4.5     Group Definitions and Split Checks

Five outer folds hold out whole recordings, with four recording-disjoint inner folds for
selection. Outer assessment populations contain 753, 964, 1,037, 906 and 870 actions;
their failure counts are 77, 109, 125, 99 and 99, respectively. The corresponding
recording counts are 26, 31, 32, 29 and 30. Summing the assessment rows recovers all
4,530 actions, 509 failures and 148 recordings exactly.
        Within each outer training set, the visual and sensor branches supplied
out-of-fold (OOF) predictions for training the logistic stacker. Each branch predicted an
action using a fit that excluded its recording. During inner validation of the stacker,
however, every split reused this bank. Some rows used to train the stacker could
therefore come from branch fits that had seen recordings assigned to the stacker’s inner
validation split. This indirect dependency affects internal model and threshold
assessment. It does not extend to the outer test: those recordings were excluded from
branch fitting, calibration, stacker training and threshold selection. Fully independent
inner validation would require rebuilding the branch prediction bank within every
stacker-validation split, including repeated branch fits and calibration across all five
outer folds. This additional nesting was beyond the training budget, so the outer scores
evaluate the procedure that reused the outer-training bank.



6.5      Task Formulation, Model Inputs, and Shared

         Evaluation Protocol

The positive class is failure. The principal ranking measure is average precision (AP),
computed by the average-precision implementation rather than trapezoidal integration
of a precision–recall curve. AUROC, balanced accuracy, macro-F1, failure recall and
precision, Brier score and fifteen-bin equal-width expected calibration error (ECE)

describe other aspects of performance. Precision–recall reporting is particularly
important at the observed minority-class prevalence (Saito & Rehmsmeier, 2015).
        Unless a later subsection states otherwise, reported core scores pool each action’s
outer-held-out probability across the five folds. Pooled AP is not the arithmetic mean
of five fold AP values. Training-only selection and sigmoid logistic calibration use
appropriate inner outputs. Hard-decision thresholds maximize inner balanced accuracy
over 0.01–0.99, with ties resolved toward 0.5. Calibration and threshold selection are
separate operations. Two thousand paired recording-cluster bootstrap resamples
quantify uncertainty conditional on the retained fitted models; they do not refit the
entire procedure or estimate all training-seed uncertainty. Nineteen structured
label-shift permutations preserve recording/action structure under their conditional
exchangeability assumption, giving a smallest plus-one probability of 0.05.

The unimodal study establishes a reference hierarchy before fusion. Class-prior, majority
and action-type predictors reveal what can be achieved without detailed observations.

and motion. The two neural branches test different inductive biases within bounded

6.6.2     Visual Representation Configurations

The core visual extractor loads the COCO-pretrained RT-DETR-R18vd checkpoint
(PekingU/rtdetr_r18vd) at the recorded revision. From the loaded detector instance,
it selects wrapper.model.backbone.model, the checkpoint’s own ResNet18-vd CNN
backbone, and freezes its parameters. The forward pass reads this backbone’s feature
maps and bypasses the hybrid Transformer encoder, detection decoder, and box heads
(Y. Zhao et al., 2024).
        Frames are resized to 640 × 640 using bilinear interpolation, converted to RGB
and divided by 255 without ImageNet mean/standard-deviation normalization. Spatial
means from three scales concatenate to 896 features per frame; the sixteen frame
descriptors are averaged. A frozen extractor feeds a 896 → 128 → 1 ReLU/dropout
head with 114,945 trainable parameters. Whole-action averaging discards temporal
order. Weighted binary cross-entropy, AdamW with learning rate 0.001 and weight
decay 0.001, batch size 128 and gradient clipping at one are used. Inner selection
chooses five or ten epochs; outer budgets are 5, 5, 10, 10 and 5.


6.6.3     Statistical Sensor and PatchTST Configurations

The statistical representation computes ten features per channel: mean, standard
deviation, median, minimum, maximum, lower and upper quartiles, range, RMS and
progress slope. Twenty-two channels therefore produce 220 features. A
training-standardized, class-balanced L2 logistic model selects C from 0.1, 1 and 10 by
mean inner AP. A subsequent unweighted C = 1 logistic sigmoid calibrates selected
out-of-fold raw scores.
        The neural branch is a scratch-trained Hugging Face PatchTST-style model with
context 512 × 22, patch length 32, stride 16, hidden width 32, two layers, four heads

                                           99
Chapter 6. REASSEMBLE Case Study: Representation, Fusion, and Robustness


and feed-forward width 64. Internal scaling is disabled; layer normalization and dropout

projection and binary head, giving 109,505 trainable parameters. The observed mask is
passed but does not suppress attention in this configuration. Inner selection allows
three or six epochs and chooses six in every outer fold. This is a compact adaptation of
the patching idea, not a claim to reproduce all settings of the original PatchTST paper
(Nie et al., 2023).

6.7      Experiment B: Fusion Strategies

Six fusion families share the cohort and recording structure: probability averaging,
stacking, representation concatenation and three context-dependent gates. Their input
spaces and calibration stages differ.


6.7.1     Objective and Hypotheses

The first hypothesis is that the combined visual–sensor pipeline improves held-out
failure ranking relative to the retained sensor pipeline. The second is that action or
quality context improves allocation between branches. These questions are distinct: a
fixed map can use complementary predictions without an effective per-instance

observed ordering changes beyond a strictly increasing transformation of the same

6.8      Experiment C: Missing and Degraded

         Modalities

The core robustness study froze the clean-trained statistical sensor and visual predictors
(U1 and U2), equal averaging (F1), the stacker (F2), and the two gates using quality
descriptors (F5 and F6). It perturbed one input at a time and also tested known loss of
a full input stream.


6.8.1     Robustness Questions and Tested Conditions

The central question is whether a gate both changes allocation in the expected direction
and limits predictive deterioration relative to F2. Absolute performance at high severity

                                            105
Chapter 6. REASSEMBLE Case Study: Representation, Fusion, and Robustness


is also necessary: a weak clean model can show a small decline merely because little
useful ranking remains to lose. An intact unimodal branch supplies a practical reference
for whether fusion introduces avoidable damage.
        Clean decision thresholds remain frozen during corruption. This matches a
deployment in which the operator does not receive fault labels and recalibrate at every
severity. Known availability masks in complete-dropout tests are an explicitly different
information condition. The two sets of results measure responses to different
information about the fault.


6.8.2     Corruption Operators and Severity Definitions

Visual operators include pixel Gaussian noise with standard deviations 0.02, 0.05 and
0.10 on the normalized scale, Gaussian blur at severities one, two and four, and removal
of two, five or eight of sixteen selected frames. Sensor operators include Gaussian noise
scaled by 0.1, 0.5 or 1.0 training-channel standard deviations; removal of two, seven or
eleven channels; missing temporal blocks covering 10%, 30% or 50% of the sequence;
and six force/torque channels stuck at training medians.
        Sensor statistics and quality descriptors are recomputed from the altered input,
rather than retaining clean summaries beside corrupted signals. The core uses its fixed
operator realization protocol. A later augmentation study averages across five
stochastic test realizations. Its Gaussian-noise estimates therefore refer to a different
evaluation protocol. None of these operators is direct evidence of cross-machine shift,
clock drift or physical sensor recalibration.


6.8.3     Missingness Policies and Dropout Experiments

In the original complete-dropout test, the four evaluated fusion models used the
statistical sensor probability and threshold when vision was absent. When sensors were
absent, they used the visual predictor and its threshold. The evaluator received the
identity of the missing stream. The core gate rejects a sample with both streams absent.
The robustness report contains no empirical result for that condition. A later

                                            106
Chapter 6. REASSEMBLE Case Study: Representation, Fusion, and Robustness


quality-interaction pilot marked such predictions unavailable.
        A separate F6 training experiment samples visual-only dropout with probability
0.15, sensor-only dropout with probability 0.15 and both-present inputs with
probability 0.70. Both-absent inputs are never sampled. Because a
one-available-modality softmax has weight exactly one and the branches are fixed, its
missing-case output is structurally fixed and supplies no useful gradient for improving
the fallback prediction. Dropout training cannot make that output better under this
architecture. Its clean AP is 0.4377, changing ordinary F6 by −0.0037 [−.0085, .0003].


6.8.4     Predictive Performance and Calibration Under

          Degradation

Under strongest pixel Gaussian noise, F2 retains AP 0.3963, while F5 and F6 fall to
0.1288 and 0.1275. The intact sensor baseline remains at AP 0.4294. F2’s Brier score
nevertheless rises to 0.2968 and ECE to 0.4368, compared with clean sensor values
0.0804 and 0.0174. Ranking retention therefore conceals severe probability failure.
        Under sensor Gaussian noise at one training standard deviation, F2 AP is 0.2222
and F5/F6 are 0.1753/0.1788, below intact vision at 0.2307. F2’s Brier/ECE rise to
0.4798/0.5251. Strong blur gives F2/F5/F6 AP 0.4099/0.4061/0.4179, without a
resolved general gate advantage. These conditions demonstrate that combining an
intact and corrupted modality can underperform the intact branch. Detection of
corruption and calibrated uncertainty remain unresolved rather than being supplied
automatically by a fusion layer.


6.8.5     Gate Behaviour and Fallback Behaviour

No tested original gate/family combination establishes both an appropriate allocation
response and a smaller decline under the study’s criteria; no supported absolute
high-severity AP superiority follows. A gate weight can change with a quality input
without changing in a beneficial way. Conversely, a smaller performance decline from a
weaker clean starting point is insuﬀicient to establish improved reliability.

                                            107
Chapter 6. REASSEMBLE Case Study: Representation, Fusion, and Robustness


        Exact missing-modality equality follows programmed fallback. It demonstrates
consistent handling of known absence, while corruption recognition and useful recovery
remain separate capabilities. This is narrower than training an encoder or student to
retain performance after input loss, as in transferable missing-modality methods (Choi
& Lee, 2019; M. Ma et al., 2022; Neverova et al., 2015).


6.8.6     Interpretation and Untested Conditions

The core evidence rejects a broad claim of reliable adaptation for the tested gates. The
AP, calibration and intact-branch comparisons capture different consequences of
corruption. Robustness to known complete absence is largely architectural here;
robustness to unnoticed corruption is empirically weak.
        Natural missingness mechanisms, simultaneous partial failures, asynchronous
clocks, new machines, new operating sites and prospective early warning were not
established. The later augmentation study changes training exposure and tests several
held-out operator families, but the same recording population and already observed core
faults limit claims of independent discovery. An untested condition remains open rather
than receiving a favorable interpretation from the model’s name.

6.10       Study Synthesis and Remaining Questions

The follow-ups distinguish representation suitability, complementary information and
fusion effectiveness. Longer PatchTST training improves AP by .0297 (95%
recording-bootstrap CI [.0122, .0451]) but remains .1441 below statistics. Insuﬀicient
training therefore contributes to the initial gap without fully explaining it under this
recipe. Yet their same-sensor combination improves on statistics by .0110 [.0054, .0181].
A weaker standalone representation can retain useful complementary predictions; this is
not fusion of two sensing modalities.
        Last-quarter visual pooling raises AP by .0885 [.0424, .1293] with the same fitted
head size as whole-action pooling. Additional head parameters cannot explain that
contrast. Concentrated outcome cues or reduced dilution are plausible explanations, but
neither was isolated, and the observation still extends to completion. The selected
pooling variant also improves its separate F2 comparison by .0461 [.0260, .0632]. The


                                           113
Chapter 6. REASSEMBLE Case Study: Representation, Fusion, and Robustness


spatial-encoder and matched adaptation studies show that additional processing or
updated weights need not improve the downstream combination; inconclusive
adaptation intervals do not establish equivalence.
       Core F2 includes verified within-fold visual ordering gains, while its advantage
over averaging remains unresolved. Anchored adjustment improves clean AP over its

7.3     Dataset Challenges

Milling video can show the machine and process context while coolant, enclosure
geometry and occlusion conceal the tool–workpiece interaction. Successful decoding is
therefore insuﬀicient evidence that the labeled abnormality is optically observable. A
visual classifier may respond to operating context, lighting or experiment-specific
appearance rather than the underlying cutting event. The substantial visual
training–assessment gap reported below is compatible with this concern, but does not
identify its cause.
       The machine stream is also an observation of the process rather than direct,
instantaneous ground truth. Aggregated electrical, load and vibration-related
measurements can describe an interval while smoothing short events. Their timing and
update cadence limit any claim about event onset or control latency. Group identity
creates a further diﬀiculty: experiment folders provide a useful split boundary, but they
are not verified identities for physical tools, workpieces or sessions. These limits
motivate both the technical gates and the stricter grouping sensitivities.

8.6     Evaluation Protocol

The original protocol uses three outer task-disjoint folds and three inner OOF
assessments within each outer-training pool. Each branch separates gradient-fitting T,
checkpoint-selection V, calibration C and assessment A task groups. A final branch is
not automatically fitted to the entire outer-training pool: its T/V/C roles remain
distinct. The inner assessment probabilities build a meta bank without using examples
that fitted, selected or calibrated their generating branch.
       Checkpoint selection uses validation macro AP; the selected checkpoint is then
calibrated on C, and assessment uses a fixed threshold of 0.5. Failure AP, success AP
and their arithmetic mean are retained. With failure prevalence above 85%,
failure-positive AP alone can conceal poor success discrimination. Pooled AP is
calculated from disjoint outer predictions, separately for each seed. It is not the
arithmetic mean of fold APs, and seed repetition does not add independent episodes.
       The later matched twenty-epoch arms use the first twenty epochs of a fixed
cosine-50 schedule, while the hundred-epoch arms use cosine-100. Highest V macro AP
selects the checkpoint, then lower V BCE and the earliest epoch resolve ties. All
configured epochs are intended to execute even when the chosen checkpoint is earlier.
The twenty-epoch study has a complete three-inner OOF bank. The hundred-epoch
pilot instead reserves 788 meta episodes under one predeclared role, followed by 1,107
outer assessments. It is not a full nested three-fold hundred-epoch experiment.

9.5     Agreement and Disagreement With Previous

        Research

Pretrained spatial features are reasonable candidates because PatchCore demonstrates
useful industrial inspection from intermediate CNN features, while RT-DETR learns a
multiscale hierarchy for detection (Roth et al., 2022; Y. Zhao et al., 2024). Their
positive findings do not imply that every downstream component helps. The matched
REASSEMBLE spatial-encoder comparison favored CNN-based F2 over the hybrid
representation by .0158 AP (95% CI [.0058, .0248]). Different feature widths and head
sizes prevent attribution solely to attention. This limits transfer of the detector’s
architectural conclusions to action outcomes; it does not contradict its detection
benchmark.
       PatchTST’s favorable forecasting ablation supports local patch tokens and
channel-shared temporal encoding (Nie et al., 2023). Our longer-budget result confirms
that optimization duration affected the scratch classifier, yet statistics remained
stronger. Their successful same-sensor combination supports complementary predictive
use rather than replacement by the higher-scoring representation. Classical geometry’s
competitiveness in inspection is consistent with taking compact, task-relevant


                                            144

summaries seriously (Horwitz & Hoshen, 2023); it does not establish why these
particular robot statistics work. Likewise, TimeSformer’s divided spatial–temporal
attention motivates examining temporal organization (Bertasius et al., 2021). The
boundary-pooling gain supports sensitivity to aggregation, but uses an order-invariant
mean within a selected interval and does not test that learned-attention mechanism.
       The positive REASSEMBLE F2 contrast and negative external contrasts agree
with a conditional view of fusion. Welding’s image-only, audio-only and fused
penetration classifiers all reached 100% clean accuracy (Hua et al., 2026, Tables 1–3).
Published HANDOVER five-class accuracy fell from 69.1% with video and force/torque
to 67.9% after adding gripper state (Thoduka et al., 2024b, Table III); our binary,
differently fitted AP comparison is not a replication. Other settings do show gains:
acoustic–thermal gearbox monitoring reports 99.54% against 90.54% for thermal alone
under a random within-condition split (Ying et al., 2025, Table 2). Such results disagree
with an unconditional prediction of a gain here, not with the bounded negative findings
in MSM or ViFailback. Wang and colleagues identify unequal overfitting and
optimization diﬀiculties in jointly trained multimodal networks (W. Wang et al., 2020).
They supply plausible competing mechanisms, not a demonstrated cause of losses in our
separately fitted branches and stacker.
       Stacking’s use of cross-validated probability outputs motivates a small learned
combiner (Ting & Witten, 1999). Our within-fold audit establishes beneficial visual
ordering changes in the saved F2, while its pooled advantage over averaging remains
unresolved. This distinguishes complementary predictions from evidence that learning
the combiner is necessary. It also explains why the coeﬀicients cannot be read as causal
modality percentages.
       Adaptive weighting has a positive precedent: QMF links useful uncertainty
weighting to a generalization bound and reports gains under specified RGB–depth
corruption (Q. Zhang et al., 2023). Our anchored clean increment, .0210 [.0015, .0419],
supports a particular context-dependent adjustment, whereas the matched-exposure
robustness increment, .0097 [−.0034, .0228], remains unresolved. This is neither a

replication nor a refutation of QMF: the quality variables, links, training procedures

9.7     Threats to Validity and Unresolved Evidence

Three features of the data limit a causal interpretation of architectural differences.
First, the target may only approximate the phenomenon of interest. PHM has no
independent quantitative tooth-damage reference, MSM’s label mapping defines which
intervals count as abnormal, and some robotic outcomes remained ambiguous under
visual review. Second, the number of independently acquired units can be much smaller
than the number of predictions. Gear experiments, recording sessions, dates, tasks, and
participants capture different sources of dependence. Where identifiers were missing,
independence across those groups could not be verified. Third, several later analyses
revisited assessment populations examined during development. Intervals conditional on
fitted predictions and variation over a few seeds do not capture that selection history.
       Preprocessing before the separate four-arm PHM loop limits attribution of its
results to architecture. In other experiments, differences in calibration, capacity,
training budget or corruption exposure accompanied architectural changes. Some
historical configurations lack the detail needed to reconstruct training exactly.
ViFailback’s complete frozen/donor matrix is supplemented by the November 24
one-fold FULL20 comparison and FULL100 pilot. Their adaptation effects remain
inconclusive; the remaining adaptation fits and corruption study are unexecuted
(Chapter 8). The literature synthesis covers the full texts available to a single reviewer.

9.8     Implications for Multimodal System Design

In a monitoring application, the intended decision determines what constitutes failure,
how it can be confirmed independently, and which observations arrive in time to be
useful. Evaluating each stream on the same held-out units establishes what it can
predict alone. Equal averaging and a fitted static combination then provide references
for testing whether context-dependent weights add value. When an instrument declares
an outage, the remaining predictor supplies a natural reference. When faulty values still
appear plausible, the relevant test is whether the gate recognizes situations in which
trusting that stream would worsen the prediction.
       A prospective return to welding could reserve whole welding sessions, collect an
independent weld-quality measure, and fix the prediction horizon before examining
those outcomes. On the same welds, exposure- and calibration-matched models could
be assessed for clean performance, known absence, degraded readings, timing error, and
shifts between operating conditions. Paired effects and a prespecified tolerance for
clean-performance loss would clarify the operational cost of any robustness gain. Such a
test would show whether the modest gain for completed assembly actions carries over to
weld-quality decisions.

10.2        Supported Contributions

SQ1 is partially answered for the evaluated tasks and budgets. REASSEMBLE
statistics outperformed the scratch-trained PatchTST even after its matched training
extension, yet their combination improved AP by .0110 [.0054, .0181]. The frozen
ResNet18-vd CNN supplied a reusable spatial representation; last-quarter pooling
improved visual AP by .0885 [.0424, .1293] with unchanged head size. These findings
establish conditional suitability and complementary predictive use, not universal
preference for statistics, freezing or a Transformer. Learned temporal order, pretrained
time-series weights, audio and earlier prediction remain unresolved.
       SQ2 is answered comparatively within the assessed recipes, but remains open as
a general architecture-selection rule. The shared assembly cohort allowed feature
concatenation, probability averaging, logistic stacking, and scalar gates conditioned on
process context to be evaluated on the same actions. Feature concatenation reached AP
of .412. The simpler prediction-level combinations ranked failures more accurately under
the tested training procedures. The external results qualify that finding by identifying
tasks on which even the learned combination fell below its sensor component.
       For missing or degraded inputs (SQ3), a known missing stream could be handled
by routing predictions to the available model. When a stream remained present but
became unreliable, the two original gates evaluated under corruption showed no
consistent advantage. Under strong sensor noise in REASSEMBLE, stacking fell to AP
.222, below the intact visual predictor at .231. Anchored adaptation improved clean AP
by .0210 [.0015, .0419], but the matched-exposure robustness increment was .0097
[−.0034, .0228]. SQ3 is therefore only partially answered: explicit fallback is established,
while consistent adaptive benefit under degraded present inputs remains unresolved.
This gate and the later visual pooling method were evaluated in separate experiments.

10.3      Principal Limitations

Each dataset imposes a different evidential limit. PHM lacks an independent physical
reference, and the preprocessing order in its separate four-arm diagnostic prevents a
leakage-free interpretation of that experiment. The industrial and robotic datasets offer
incomplete information about independence at the level of tools, objects, or
participants. Complete-action and complete-episode predictions can use evidence that
only becomes visible near the outcome. Deploying a warning system would require a
prediction horizon that excludes such information.
       The comparison also depends on how the models were fitted. Calibration,
training budgets, and exposure to corruptions differed in some comparisons. Several
follow-ups revisited previously evaluated populations, and the reported bootstrap
intervals condition on the fitted models. The short-budget fine-tuning comparison did
not resolve a mechanism for the observed differences. The reporting cutoff includes the
November 24 ViFailback closure: 79 of 111 matched fits completed, with 32 FULL20 fits
unfinished and the corruption evaluation unexecuted. Completed adaptation intervals
include gains and losses; unfinished work supplies no negative model result. Where only
run summaries were available, they limited the scope for independent checks of

10.4       Priorities for Future Research

An independent welding study is the most direct next test of the original question. It
should specify the weld-level outcome and prediction time, pair the camera and process
measurements to that decision, and reserve complete new welding sessions for
assessment. An independent inspection should establish weld quality. For PHM, a
physical measure of damage linked to a known tooth would likewise distinguish
measurement validity from the ability to predict the photographic proxy. Independent
review of ambiguous robotic outcomes would strengthen the corresponding classification
targets.
       Within each study, the informative comparison is between the strongest
single-input predictor, equal averaging, a fitted static combination, and a gate trained
with comparable calibration opportunities and corruption exposure. Repeated tests of
known absence, unannounced corruption, temporal misalignment, and shifts between
physical groups would show which failure conditions justify adaptive weighting.
Prespecified margins for clean performance and grouped uncertainty estimates would
make the cost of a robustness improvement visible.
       The remaining transfer experiments require matched optimization budgets,
normalization procedures, and held-out tasks. For operational monitoring, prospective
tests should also measure warning time, false alarms, and end-to-end delay, and
evaluate how operators act on uncertain predictions. These measurements would
establish whether the modest ranking benefit observed on completed assembly actions
translates into a useful decision while a weld is still in progress.
