Physics-Informed Multi-Modal Transformer
Architectures for Robust Industrial Welding
            Anomaly Detection

Abstract
The registered title reflects the robotic welding proposal, and the implementation treats
physics-informed fusion as conditioning on observed physical process context without
imposing differential-equation or conservation-law constraints. After an audit
documented development exposure to the proposed Intel Welding test recordings,
evaluation centred on five adjacent industrial and robotic tasks, including a visual
comparison of the frozen ResNet18-vd CNN backbone in RT-DETR-R18vd alone and
with its hybrid Transformer encoder. A review of 94 full-text works examined fusion
methods, single-input baselines and tests of missing or degraded inputs. In one
weld-penetration study, images, sound and their combination achieved identical
clean-test accuracy.
       Independent validation of weld quality remains open. In PHM, already under
way when the welding split was audited, three sensor groups predicted a
photograph-derived gear-condition measure with a mean absolute error of 0.824
percentage points, compared with 0.804 for the training mean. Across 4,530 completed
REASSEMBLE actions from 148 recordings, failure average precision reached .429 for
sensors alone, .446 for equal averaging of video and sensor predictions, and .447 for
logistic stacking. The difference between fusion methods remained uncertain. Two
adaptive gates showed no consistent advantage under input loss or degradation. Sensor
models outperformed stacking in MSM milling and ViFailback manipulation, and equal
averaging in human–robot handover. Assembly provided the clearest evidence of a
fusion benefit. Across tasks, the results support evaluating learned fusion against both a
strong sensor model and simple averaging.
       Keywords: robotic welding, multimodal monitoring, sensor fusion, missing
modalities, failure classification

1.1     Industrial Monitoring and the Motivation for

        Multimodal Learning

Robotic welding motivated a practical question. If a camera records the appearance of
the weld pool while other instruments record sound or process signals, when does a joint
prediction improve on the strongest single-input model? Visual and acoustic welding
studies have already implemented learned fusion (Fan et al., 2026; Hua et al., 2026). In
one weld-penetration study, the image-only, audio-only, and fused models all achieved
100% accuracy on the reported clean test set (Hua et al., 2026, Tables 1–3). For that
comparison, the extra channel left no measurable margin for a clean accuracy gain.
Whether it could help under camera occlusion or acoustic interference is a separate
question that requires observations under those conditions.
       The registered title reflects the original Intel Welding proposal. In the
implementations, physics-informed means that fusion weights can depend on observed
physical process context, such as the assembly action being performed. The models
impose no differential-equation or conservation-law constraints. The gear study was
already under way when the Intel Welding audit found that acquisition sessions
spanned partitions and proposed test recordings had been examined during
development. The empirical evaluation consequently centred on five adjacent industrial

and robotic monitoring tasks. Independent welding validation requires fresh welds. In
the core assembly comparison, the visual classifier used the frozen ResNet18-vd CNN
backbone inside RT-DETR-R18vd. A separate visual comparison evaluated the
backbone together with the detector’s spatial hybrid Transformer encoder.
       The question across applications was what each input contributed to the
prediction. In the gear study, this first required a measurable indicator of condition.
Robotic assembly then supplied labelled actions with paired video and machine signals
for a direct comparison of fusion methods. Milling, manipulation, and human–robot
handover provided further tests on different operations. Each comparison is interpreted
against its own outcome, available inputs, and strongest single-input reference.



1.2     Problem Statement

A monitoring model requires a target with a physical or operational interpretation and
observations available at the intended decision time. A statistic extracted from a
post-run photograph can define a continuous target for gear-condition regression. A
label assigned to a completed assembly action defines an outcome-classification task. A
camera frame from the end of that action may contain decisive evidence that was
unavailable while the robot was executing it. These differences determine both what a
model can learn and where its predictions could be used.
       Estimating the value of an additional input also requires matching the
comparison. Visual and sensor observations must refer to the same event, and both the
fused model and its component predictors must be assessed on the same held-out units.
Changing the training budget, fitting a new calibration function, or exposing only one
model to simulated failures changes the interpretation of a score difference. Known
absence raises a different problem from an apparently valid sensor reading corrupted by
noise. Prior work on multimodal learning has shown that a joint model can perform
worse than its strongest component (W. Wang et al., 2020). The useful comparison
therefore includes strong single-input models, simple combinations, and explicit tests of



                                            2
Chapter 1. Introduction


input degradation.



1.3     Research Aim and Objectives

The aim is to determine when combining visual and machine-sensor evidence improves
industrial condition estimation or outcome classification. The implementation provides
interchangeable visual and sensor representations, unimodal prediction heads, and
fusion at the feature or prediction level. The comparison includes convolutional image
features, statistical summaries of sensor windows, and patch-based Transformer
encoders for sensor sequences. Static fusion uses fixed rules or fitted coeﬀicients. A
process-aware gate can change the relative weights with the context of an observation.
       The evaluation asks whether those choices improve ranking or regression error,
whether predicted probabilities and decisions remain useful, and how each method
behaves when an input is unavailable or degraded. Grouped assessment accounts for
dependence between observations from a shared experiment, recording, or task where
the necessary identifiers are available. The interpretation also considers costs of
computation, uncertainty in the target, and the extent to which a result carries over to
new operating conditions.



1.4     Main Research Question and Four Subquestions

The original exposé posed the following main research question.

      How can a process-aware multimodal AI architecture be designed and
      evaluated for robust industrial anomaly detection using robotic welding data
      as a case study?

Four subquestions address representation, fusion, robustness, and evaluation.

  SQ1. Which lightweight or pretrained Transformer-based representation components
         are suitable for visual, audio, and sensor time-series data in a multimodal
         industrial anomaly detection pipeline?

                                             3
Chapter 1. Introduction


  SQ2. How do unimodal baselines, simple concatenation fusion, modular late fusion,
           and process-aware gated cross-modal fusion compare in terms of predictive
           performance?

  SQ3. Can a gated cross-modal fusion architecture improve robustness when one or
           more modalities are missing, degraded, or unreliable?

  SQ4. How can multimodal architecture performance be evaluated beyond accuracy,
           including robustness, interpretability, modularity, and practical usability?

The welding case motivated all four questions. Audio remained relevant to the literature
review. For REASSEMBLE, the correspondence between action annotations and
waveforms was unresolved, and spoken outcomes posed an additional risk of revealing
the label. The predictive experiments therefore concentrated on vision and machine
sensing.



1.5        Scope and Evolution of the Research

The PHM study asked what the gear measurements could establish about condition.
Post-run photographs supplied a provisional continuous measure of candidate damage
area. The main sensor comparison held out whole experiments and tested whether three
groups of measurements could predict that measure more accurately than the training
mean. Their mean absolute error was 0.824 percentage points, compared with 0.804 for
the constant predictor (Table 5.2). The paired analysis left the difference unresolved.
An independent physical measurement would be needed to determine how faithfully the
photographic target tracks damage.
       REASSEMBLE supplied the principal fusion test because video and sensor
streams could be linked to native success or failure labels for the same completed
actions. Across 4,530 actions from 148 recordings, failure average precision was .429 for
the statistical sensor predictor, .446 for equal averaging of visual and sensor
probabilities, and .447 for logistic stacking (Tables 6.1 and 6.2). The paired comparison
supported a modest gain for stacking over its sensor component. Its advantage over

                                              4
Chapter 1. Introduction


equal averaging remained uncertain. The gates conditioned on quality alone or on
quality and action context showed no consistent additional benefit when streams were
absent or degraded. The measured gain applies to the fitted pipeline, which also
introduced a second probability model.
       The external implementations examined whether the same approach remained
useful on new targets. In MSM, camera and machine measurements covered milling
intervals labelled for abnormal cutting. For the 1,895 intervals in the primary
evaluation, average precision for abnormal cutting, averaged over three seeds, was .757
for stacking and .829 for logistic sensing (Table 7.2). In ViFailback, the target was the
outcome of a completed manipulation episode, and entire tasks were held out. Original
stacking had lower pooled macro average precision than the logistic sensor model at
each of three seeds. A separate experiment transferred a convolutional encoder learned
on REASSEMBLE and found mixed changes across seeds (Tables 8.2 and 8.3).
       HANDOVER examined whether video and force–torque signals from the same
part of a human–robot handover improved prediction of its recorded outcome. On 151
oﬀicial test trials, equal averaging achieved failure average precision of .803, compared
with .824 for the logistic sensor predictor (Table H.1). The participant-to-trial mapping
needed to verify participant-disjoint predictions for fitting a learned fusion model was
unavailable. The empirical result therefore concerns simple averaging on those trials.
Together, the three external studies show why a new application of a fusion pipeline
needs a fresh comparison with its own sensor baseline.

Multimodal Monitoring in Robotic Welding

During robotic welding, a camera can observe changes in the weld pool while a
microphone records the sound of the process. The two observations can respond
differently to the same change. The main research question asks how to design and
evaluate a process-aware architecture that uses such evidence to detect anomalies
reliably. To judge the value of a second input, the joint model and its strongest
single-input alternative must predict the same outcome on the same welds. Two
primary welding studies illustrate why this comparison matters.
       The dynamic multimodal measurement fusion network for welding (DMAW)
combined image and audio Transformer representations to classify four welding states.
Accuracy reached 96.1% with fusion, compared with 90.2% for the image model and
84.4% for the audio model. The reported evaluation leaves the grouping of windows by
welding run and performance during a controlled outage unresolved (Fan et al., 2026,
PDF p. 7, Table III). In a weld-penetration study, the image-only, audio-only, and fused
models each reached 100% accuracy on the clean test set. The fused model achieved
93.33% under the reported interference condition. Determining the benefit of fusion
during interference requires the single-input models to be evaluated under the
corresponding conditions (Hua et al., 2026, PDF pp. 18, 22, Tables 1–3). Together these

studies establish that learned visual–acoustic fusion already exists in welding and that
its observed advantage depends on the endpoint and comparator.
        The original proposal concerned Intel Welding. The subsequent audit
documented previous use of the proposed test population, which prevented an
independent weld-quality assessment (Chapter 1). The review therefore follows the

can be compared when a camera and machine signals observe the same operation. The

2.1      Review Scope and Search Methodology

The review traced industrial fusion claims to the methods, comparison tables, and
results in the publications inspected. A gap here means a comparison that those results
leave unresolved for a specified target, set of inputs, and assessment population. The
search and access records limit how broadly that inference can be made.


2.1.1     Research Themes and Search Questions

The representation theme asks which observations are available, what transformations
are applied, and whether a representation preserves information relevant to the target.
The fusion theme asks whether additional inputs improve a common prediction task
against credible single-input references. The robustness theme asks which absence,
corruption, or distribution-change regime was actually evaluated. The evaluation theme
examines independence, thresholds, uncertainty, resource measurement, and
reproducibility. These questions deliberately include negative outcomes: a fusion
mechanism should be informative even when an added modality provides little benefit.


                                            9
Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


        The review also distinguishes two objectives often grouped together. Learning
with several modalities to deploy a single-input model is a privileged-training problem.
Combining several live inputs during deployment is a multimodal-inference problem.
Cross-modal distillation and reconstruction connect these objectives, but their
acquisition assumptions differ. The taxonomy of Baltrušaitis et al. (2019) provides an
organizing vocabulary; industrial claims are appraised in the original studies rather
than inferred from that survey.


2.1.2     Sources, Search Terms, and Search Period

The literature review developed throughout the project. One documented search round
focused on work published in 2016–2026, with selected earlier methodological
contributions. Of the 120 candidates screened in that round, 39 met the inclusion
criteria. The corresponding full-text appraisal covered 37 of those works. The later
expansion identified 57 distinct full-text files after reconciling duplicate downloads and
checking work versions. The current comparison register comprises 94 distinct work
records, rather than an estimate of the size or prevalence of the entire field.
        Searches paired industrial monitoring and manufacturing terms with fusion
stage, attention, missing inputs, noise, and domain shift. Dataset searches included
MVTec AD, MVTec 3D-AD, Eyecandies, bearing datasets, and machine-sound
benchmarks. Publisher and repository searches located publications. Crossref supplied
metadata for identity checks (Crossref, 2020), and OpenAlex supported citation tracing
(Priem et al., 2022). Zotero linked bibliographic records to the inspected PDFs and
appraisal notes (Corporation for Digital Scholarship, 2026). Appendix A distinguishes
the original screening population from the later full-text appraisal.


2.1.3     Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

Direct industrial work concerns faults, condition, manufacturing quality, inspection or
robotic task outcomes. Dataset papers and credible unimodal methods are also relevant
because fusion needs a defined target and a strong reference. Transferable studies are

labelled explicitly: optimization failures, missing-input mechanisms, uncertainty
weighting and evaluation theory can inform industrial design without constituting
industrial validation (Bengio & Grandvalet, 2004; S. Lee et al., 2026; M. Ma et al., 2022;
W. Wang et al., 2020).
        A PDF can remain in the register while serving only as background or carrying
major methodological qualifications. Simulated robot regression, RGB–geometry
inspection, physical camera–telemetry monitoring and one-camera feature fusion occupy

Each appraisal records source identity and read version, target and prediction unit,

access and reliability interventions. Results are linked to pages, tables or figures.
Decisive PDF tables were checked where extraction could obscure input columns, values
or footnotes. Full-text appraisal establishes inspection, not experimental reproduction
or endorsement of every author conclusion.

assessment cohort supports a pipeline comparison; it does not isolate fusion if encoders,
supervision, augmentation or capacity also differ. Input-subset models, complete

2.1.5     Limitations of the Review

The sources are neither exhaustive nor statistically independent. Many inspection
papers reuse MVTec 3D-AD or Eyecandies, so repeated benchmark gains do not
constitute repeated validation across factories. Preprints are version-specific and some
publication details remain provisional. When few studies evaluated a condition, the
review offers limited evidence about that condition.
        The primary MVTec 3D-AD table gives 249 normal and 948 anomalous tests,
totaling 1,197; some later accounts give different counts (Bergmann et al., 2022,
Table 1). The welding penetration paper has inconsistent acquisition/training counts,
and a lightweight inspection paper gives different NwfDD values in its principal and
ablation tables (Hua et al., 2026, PDF pp. 12, 15)(Z. Zhang et al., 2024, PDF p. 10,
Tables IV, VI, VII). These problems reduce the precision of particular comparisons
without invalidating every observation in a source.



2.2      Industrial Monitoring Tasks and Supervision

Different monitoring tasks impose different assumptions about training data,
observations, and acceptable error. Their scores are therefore not interchangeable
evidence for a general architecture. The target definition must precede the comparison
of representation or fusion mechanisms.


2.2.1     Anomaly Detection, Defect Classification, and Failure

          Classification

Normal-only anomaly detection learns a nominal reference, whereas supervised defect
classification uses labelled fault or quality classes. MVTec AD and MVTec 3D-AD
exemplify the first setting; welding penetration recognition exemplifies the second
(Bergmann et al., 2019, 2022; Hua et al., 2026). HANDOVER introduces a different
endpoint: whether a completed interaction succeeded or failed through no approach, no
grasp, no release or object drop (Thoduka et al., 2024b). A task failure need not denote

                                           12
Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


physical damage or identify its cause.
        Supervision is consequently part of the comparison. The weakly supervised
inspection study reintegrates MVTec data because training needs anomalous
image-level labels, changing the ordinary normal-only contract (Z. Zhang et al., 2024,
PDF p. 8). ZSG-IAD trains reports and masks before evaluating held-out categories or
datasets; its zero-shot terminology concerns that boundary, rather than absence of
anomaly-supervised training (Q. Chen et al., 2026, PDF pp. 6, 8–9). Scores from these
tasks cannot be combined as interchangeable evidence for a general architecture.


2.2.2     Condition Estimation and Regression

Continuous prediction requires attention to units, reference measurements, and the
horizon at which a target is defined. Remaining useful life is not equivalent to an
instantaneous defect probability. Al-Dulaimi et al. (2019) evaluate a hybrid
CNN–LSTM on simulated C-MAPSS trajectories using RMSE and an asymmetric
prognostic score. Their two branches process the same selected telemetry channels; this
is evidence about complementary representations of sensor data, not physical
camera–sensor complementarity.
        The synthetic robot benchmarks of Altinses and Schwung (2025) address
cross-modal prediction, reconstruction, and downstream regression. Errors for different
reconstructed outputs cannot automatically establish which physical modality is most
useful for one common target. This distinction matters for proxy supervision: a system
may predict a derived condition index accurately without demonstrating that the index
measures deterioration. A defensible regression comparison specifies the target
construction, scale, fitted preprocessing, and common evaluation cases before
interpreting numerical improvement as better condition monitoring.

Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


2.2.3     Dataset-Provided Labels, Derived Targets, and Proxy

          Supervision

Reference labels have different evidential roles. Post-build CT provides flaw localization
for the voxel-level monitoring study of Petrich et al. (2021); microscopy and density
measurements support quality classes in J. Li et al. (2022); operando X-ray imaging
guides process-state labels in Pandiyan et al. (2022). The reference instrument may
therefore supervise the model without being available at inference. Counting it as an
input modality would overstate the deployed system’s information.
        Derived labels require a further separation between prediction and independent
validation. If a target is calculated from the same measurements supplied to the model,
low prediction error may reflect recovery of that calculation rather than an independent
physical state. Likewise, region-level annotations may assign a common quality class to
many neighboring observations. These choices can be useful, but their spatial or
temporal dependence affects the evaluation unit. The thesis consequently needs an
explicit account of which targets are native annotations, independent measurements, or
analytical proxies, and which conclusions each can support.


2.2.4     Retrospective Assessment and Online Monitoring

A predictor evaluated after a complete window is a retrospective assessor unless
prediction-time availability is constrained. HANDOVER explicitly states that its
baselines classify outcomes after the complete trial; causal online prediction remains
future work (Thoduka et al., 2024b, PDF p. 7, Section VI). A post-pass photograph,
layer summary and complete action therefore provide different information horizons
from prospective monitoring (Petrich et al., 2021; X. Zhang et al., 2026). This
distinction does not invalidate offline classification; it limits the operational claim.
        Computation must also be separated from observation. DMAW uses twenty
frames at 37 frames per second while reporting a shorter forward latency.
Approximately half a second of observation is therefore distinct from computational



                                             14
Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


delay (Fan et al., 2026, PDF pp. 6–7). Likewise, varying CNN–LSTM input duration
does not by itself establish warning before a defect occurs (Pandiyan et al., 2022). An
online claim needs a causal information cutoff, acquisition delay and defined end-to-end
workload.



2.3      Multimodal Data in Industrial Applications

Complementarity depends on independent observations, not simply on distinct tensor
shapes. The review therefore distinguishes physical acquisition channels from derived
views, and distinguishes ordinary pairing from the stronger correspondence required by
cross-modal reconstruction or localized process monitoring.


2.3.1     Visual and Video Observations

RGB, thermal images, process videos and geometry have different acquisition
assumptions. Depth, point clouds reconstructed from depth and estimated normals may
be several representations of one geometric source. GPAD combines RGB–depth
features with point-cloud priors; this does not establish three independent physical
sensors (M. Li et al., 2026, PDF pp. 4–7). RGB–geometry benchmarks describe
appearance and shape, rather than controller dynamics or motor current (Bergmann
et al., 2022; Bonfiglioli et al., 2023).
        Physical camera-plus-measurement precedents include thermal imaging with
vibration, welding images with sound, tool photographs with acoustic emission and
robot video with force–torque and gripper state (Hua et al., 2026; Huo et al., 2022;
Thoduka et al., 2024b; X. Zhang et al., 2026). Their units range from static inspection
to process windows and complete trials. The tool-wear camera is used after a cutting
pass, whereas preceding acoustic emission describes active cutting (X. Zhang et al.,
2026, PDF pp. 19–22). A sequence of images can also be processed without learning
temporal order: pooled frame features and a video Transformer are different
representations even when both consume multiple frames.

2.3.2     Sensor Time Series and Process Measurements

Machine measurements vary in physical origin and semantic meaning. Accelerometers
observe vibration, pressure sensors observe fluid behaviour, and controller logs may
describe commands, estimates, or tracking errors rather than independently measured
physical quantities. The harmonic-drive study combines high-frequency vibration with
servo information, whereas PGMTKD transfers pressure–vibration teacher knowledge to
a pressure-only student (C. Li et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2024). Their sensor availability
and deployment objectives differ despite both addressing missing information.
        Representations can further multiply apparent modalities without adding
measurements. Kullu and Cinar (2022) combine current and vibration with raw and
STFT views. Wan et al. (2024) derive statistical “vibration” features from two acoustic
sensors; they do not acquire an accelerometer channel. C. Wang et al. (2025) combine
source-domain features and decisions from vibration data. These studies remain useful
architectural comparators, but neither a second transform nor a second domain
establishes an additional physical observation. Acquisition inventories must therefore
precede counting modalities or attributing gains to sensor diversity.


2.3.3     Audio Observations

Audio can capture machine or process events that are weakly visible, but the mapping
between recorded sound and fault state depends on background conditions, microphone
placement, and synchronization. MIMII deliberately mixes factory background
recordings at multiple SNR levels; its fan baseline AUC decreases from .94 at +6 dB to
.70 at -6 dB (Purohit et al., 2019, Table 3). ToyADMOS provides miniature-machine
recordings and an explicit noise-mixing protocol, making it a useful acoustic reference
rather than a demonstrated camera–sensor advantage (Koizumi et al., 2019).
        In process monitoring, airborne acoustic signals may be paired with a melt-pool
camera or a photodiode. The distinction is in the instruments. Signal-to-image encoding
in J. Li et al. (2022) preserves an acoustic–optical-signal experiment, whereas Xie et al.
(2025) use actual camera observations. DCASE2020 Task2 provides audio-only baselines

                                            16
Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


under an evaluation protocol that differs from those of later domain-shift challenges
(Koizumi et al., 2020). Acoustic coverage in the review therefore motivates candidate
representations without implying predictive audio evaluation in every thesis case study.


2.3.4     Synchronization, Correspondence, and Modality

          Availability

Fusion assumes that observations refer to a compatible event, region, or time interval.
Stronger correspondence is required when a model predicts one modality from another
or multiplies aligned discrepancy maps. RGB–point-cloud feature mapping exploits
registered benchmark geometry; process fusion may instead need time synchronization
and projection into a common spatial grid (Costanzino et al., 2024; Petrich et al., 2021).
The digital-twin study registers sensor features to robot TCP positions, illustrating that
position can support correspondence without serving as an ordinary predictive feature
(L. Chen et al., 2023).
        Availability has several levels: a file may exist, a sample may have a paired
observation, and a usable signal may still be absent or degraded. None implies the
others. Modality completeness should therefore be recorded per prediction unit with an
explicit pairing rule. The acoustic–photodiode study explicitly lacks modality
registration, which qualifies conclusions drawn from its encoded-window fusion (J. Li
et al., 2022). The same distinction matters for camera and machine-sensor data. A
pairing record can identify unmatched events even when both files are present.



2.4      Representation Learning for Industrial

         Monitoring — SQ1

SQ1 concerns the relationship between representations and task requirements. The
evidence supports neither a universal preference for learned features nor an assumption
that a large pretrained encoder is always necessary. Representation quality interacts



                                            17

with supervision, preprocessing, sample size, and the resources allocated to fitting.


2.4.1     Engineered Features and Conventional Machine Learning

Engineered statistics, spectral descriptors and conventional classifiers remain important
references. Their relevance follows from predictive performance and information access,
not from architectural novelty. Classical geometric features can be highly competitive
for 3D inspection, and a multi-channel neural model can lose to a selected sensor
(Goodarzi et al., 2023; Horwitz & Hoshen, 2023). A strong reference prevents a weak
learned branch from defining an artificially easy fusion comparison.
        Industrial vision also has lightweight alternatives. The weakly supervised
inspection network uses one camera source, internal anomaly-aware features and a
compact CNN; its reported cost is 2.33 million parameters and 5.65 ms per image
(Z. Zhang et al., 2024, PDF p. 10, Table V). This is relevant representation and cost
evidence, but its anomalous training labels and modified MVTec supervision differ from
normal-only inspection. Conventional and learned references should therefore be
compared under the same target, assessment cases and fitted preprocessing rather than
ranked by model family.


2.4.2     Pretrained Visual Representations

Pretrained CNN and Transformer features reduce industrial fitting requirements while
importing substantial external learning. PatchCore, asymmetric student–teacher models
and cross-modal feature mapping show useful nominal-data representations under
different protocols (Costanzino et al., 2024; Roth et al., 2022; Rudolph et al., 2023).
Their success supports transfer as a candidate strategy, not an architecture-independent
guarantee.
        Vision-language inspection imports semantic context as well. DSE-AD combines
historical/current rail images with normal/abnormal position prompts and evaluates
pixel localization on another rail domain (Lei & Qi, 2025). Its paired-reference and
prompt assumptions differ from isolated image classification. Similarly, a detector

2.4.3     Temporal and Time-Series Representations

Temporal structure can be represented through statistics, spectra, recurrence, attention
or aggregation. PatchTST tokenizes channels into patches and shares Transformer
weights across channel-independent paths. Its supervised, masked-pretraining and
transfer experiments are different fitting regimes (Nie et al., 2023, PDF pp. 3–8). On
Weather horizon 96, the patch-plus-channel-independent model reports MSE .152
against .164 without patching; this supports the design within forecasting, not
superiority for robotic failure classification (Nie et al., 2023, PDF p. 9, Table 7).
        Video-time attention addresses a different structure. TimeSformer separates
temporal and spatial attention, while Video Swin introduces local spatiotemporal
attention (Bertasius et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2021). Neither is equivalent to averaging
independently encoded frames. The industrial CNN–LSTM RUL work also shows
dependence on observation length within its simulated task (Al-Dulaimi et al., 2019).
SQ1 therefore requires explicit windows, ordering, normalization, pretraining and
resource budgets. Selecting statistics after a bounded Transformer comparison remains
a substantive answer to suitability.


2.4.4     Audio Representations

Acoustic encodings include MFCCs, log-mel spectra, wavelet maps, GASF transforms
and learned features. The penetration network applies VMD and GASF, while DMAW
adapts Transformer representations for welding observations (Fan et al., 2026; Hua
et al., 2026). An image-shaped acoustic tensor remains sound evidence, not an
independently acquired camera stream. Spectra must also use the same available
interval as competing representations.

        The Audio Spectrogram Transformer supplies a relevant pretrained candidate,

supports careful recording and normalization choices rather than an automatic

2.4.5     Frozen Representations, Fine-Tuning, and

          Computational Constraints

Freezing an encoder, fitting a small head, and adapting an entire network allocate very
different statistical and computational resources. Comparing them requires disclosure of
which parameters change and what data influence selection. Benchmark methods based
on pretrained features can be effective with nominal training data, but their
representation-learning cost is partly external to the industrial experiment (Costanzino
et al., 2024; Roth et al., 2022). This is legitimate transfer learning, provided that the
comparison does not present it as equivalent to training from scratch.
        Distillation introduces another boundary between training and deployment.
PGMTKD’s multimodal teachers enrich a pressure-only student, while the gated
acoustic–vibration study reports a distilled LightGBM student but leaves ambiguity
about teacher-feature inputs and timing boundaries (Huang & Tong, 2026; C. Li et al.,
2025). Resource claims must account for feature extraction, teachers required at
inference, and preprocessing as well as the final predictor. For SQ1, the appropriate
conclusion is a need to compare explicit fitting recipes under feasible budgets, not to
assume that frozen features or full fine-tuning are intrinsically fairer.

2.5      Multimodal Fusion Strategies — SQ2

Fusion is classified here by the location of information exchange. Early integration
combines raw channels or engineered features; intermediate integration combines
learned representations; output-level fusion combines predictions or anomaly scores.
Hybrid systems use more than one stage. Author terminology is retained where needed,
but it does not override this operational distinction.


2.5.1     Unimodal References and Incremental Modality Value

The strongest credible single-input model defines the central SQ2 reference. Welding
makes the issue immediate: the penetration paper labels its own audio-only and
image-only models as achieving 100%, equal to clean fusion. Gains over other
backbones cannot establish an added-modality effect (Hua et al., 2026, PDF pp. 18, 22).
HANDOVER likewise reports 69.1% for video plus force–torque and 67.9% after adding
gripper state within the I3D-D family (Thoduka et al., 2024b, PDF p. 7, Table III).
These native-paper values are five-class outcome accuracies averaged over five trainings,
not binary failure AP.
        The tool-wear comparison reports MAE 6.6183 for its proposed model, 8.3002 for
visual ResNet50 and 60.5118 for its acoustic reference; other multimodal architectures
have errors above 45 (X. Zhang et al., 2026, PDF p. 24, Table 1). Architecture
differences prevent causal attribution to fusion alone, but the strong visual reference
changes the interpretation. Transferable naive multimodal failures and industrial
all-channel losses reinforce the need to report incremental effects against the best
admissible component (Goodarzi et al., 2023; W. Wang et al., 2020).


2.5.2     Feature Concatenation

Concatenation combines representations without a complex allocation mechanism,
making it a necessary reference. Its performance depends on feature scale, dimension,
regularization and encoder quality. M3DM’s direct-concatenation ablation is below its


                                            21
Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


point-cloud reference, whereas the complete hybrid system improves on both inputs
(Y. Wang et al., 2023, Table 4). MADFlow provides another protocol-specific
alignment-versus-concatenation comparison (Y. Li et al., 2025).
        CFA supplies explicitly transferable evidence: for DLinear on Energy, first
concatenation yields MSE .670 versus .263 without text and .243 with constrained
fusion (S. Lee et al., 2026, PDF p. 4, Table 4). Yet its own constrained model also loses
in some settings. Manufacturing fusion can similarly help one architecture and harm
another under the same broad task (J. Li et al., 2022, Table 3). Concatenation should
receive reasonable normalization and fitting; a weak implementation cannot establish
the necessity of a more elaborate operator.


2.5.3     Prediction-Level and Late Fusion

Late fusion preserves separately fitted predictors and combines outputs through
averaging, stacking or evidence rules. Thermal–vibration diagnosis uses separate CNN
softmax outputs with weighted Dempster–Shafer aggregation. Its clean reported
diagnostic rate is 100%, compared with 99.06% for IRT and 96.32% for vibration (Huo
et al., 2022, PDF p. 7, Table VII). Fixed weights and the sample-CV protocol qualify
the gain, but this is direct physical camera–sensor late-fusion evidence.
        HANDOVER compares late outputs with intermediate sensor integration into
RGB and optical-flow branches (Thoduka et al., 2024b, PDF pp. 5–7). Inspection map
fusion is different: cross-modal mapping and CPIR aggregate discrepancy maps that
need not be probabilities (Costanzino et al., 2024; Shangguan et al., 2025). Moreover,
some papers call pre-classifier representation combination late fusion (W. Wang et al.,
2020; Xie et al., 2025). A learned prediction combiner therefore requires an explicit
fitting bank, calibration contract and score scale, in addition to a common outer
assessment.

2.5.4     Gated and Context-Conditioned Fusion

Gates can allocate embeddings, blend maps, alter prediction coeﬀicients or balance
training objectives. DMAW reports 96.1% against 90.5% for summation and 92.6% for
its gate comparator (Fan et al., 2026, PDF p. 9, Table V). TC-MAF instead bounds
auxiliary RGB evidence around a multimodal anchor using normal-training dispersion
and local disagreement (Deng et al., 2026, PDF pp. 3–5). These are different adaptive
mechanisms.
        The conditioner is decisive. GCCF-AD describes dynamic fusion, but Eq. 8
specifies a learned scalar α = σ(ω) without an explicit sample-quality input (An et al.,
2026, PDF p. 3). PGMTKD’s teacher allocation uses training losses, while task-loss
balancing does not become a deployment quality measurement (C. Li et al., 2025;
X. Zhang et al., 2026). GAF-Net uses progressive feature feedback, and trustworthy
fusion uses evidence-based aggregation (Huang & Tong, 2026; Ying et al., 2025). These
mechanisms differ in which weights can vary, which inputs determine them and whether
the resulting predictions improve on a fixed combination. Process context does not
itself impose physical laws.


2.5.5     Comparability and Fairness of Fusion Evaluations

A shared dataset name does not establish a matched experiment. Cohort, supervision,
observation window, pretraining, feature dimensions, augmentation and selection
exposure can explain gains. The tool-wear study changes architecture alongside fusion;
incomplete federated imagery changes client data access as well as the integration
mechanism (X. Zhang et al., 2026; X. Zhao et al., 2026). These are informative pipeline
comparisons, but do not automatically isolate attention or quality conditioning.
        Same-evidence controls support narrower attribution. TC-MAF retains its
evidence sources while comparing bounded fusion with static mean, weighted scores,
rank fusion and a learned gate (Deng et al., 2026, PDF p. 7, Table 3). Conversely, the
disagreement-aware paper improves a local released-code baseline that is below the
published M3DM result (J. Xu et al., 2026, PDF p. 10, Table 4). Distilled predictors

also receive training information unavailable to ordinary unimodal models (C. Li et al.,
2025). Such settings are legitimate when identified. Pipeline benefit, modality necessity
and a causal fusion-operator effect remain different questions.



2.6      Reliability and Robustness — SQ3

Robustness is defined by an intervention and a prediction setting. Missing channels,
corrupted measurements, asynchronous observations, and a new machine are different
problems. The matrix records evidence for each regime separately; general robustness
language is not used to fill an untested cell.


2.6.1     Missing Modalities

Planned single-input deployment differs from unannounced loss in a model expecting
complete inputs. PGMTKD’s pressure-only student and incomplete RGB–geometry
methods establish useful reduced-input inference, while ModDrop and missing-modality
Transformers provide transferable mechanisms and counterexamples (C. Li et al., 2025;
M. Ma et al., 2022; Neverova et al., 2015; Sui et al., 2026). They do not establish every
possible outage pattern.
        TC-MAF explicitly evaluates unavailable 3D: I-AUROC falls from .9788 to .9330
and P-AUPRO from .9901 to .9586 (Deng et al., 2026, PDF p. 7, Table 3). Federated
substation work varies the fraction of incomplete clients, reaching 80%, which differs
from an arbitrary test-camera failure matrix (X. Zhao et al., 2026, PDF p. 29, Figure 8).
ZSG-IAD’s subset-input rows do not fully specify shared-checkpoint and training
exposure (Q. Chen et al., 2026). Missing training images in hot rolling constitute
another regime (L. Ma et al., 2025). Each comparison must state known masks,
training availability, replacement policy and all-inputs-absent behavior.

2.6.2     Noise and Partial Degradation

Physical camera robustness studies already exist. Thermal–vibration diagnosis injects
Gaussian noise and vibration bias/stuck faults; welding penetration recognition reports
artificial interference; acoustic–thermal and acoustic–vibration studies examine further
noise settings (Hua et al., 2026; Huang & Tong, 2026; Huo et al., 2022; Ying et al.,
2025). These interventions differ in affected streams, severity and exposure.
        At -2 dB, noisy IRT gives 95.26%, rising to 97.6% with fault-free vibration; noisy
vibration gives 36.86%, rising to 94.2% with fault-free IRT (Huo et al., 2022, PDF p. 7,
Table VIII). The healthy companion is part of that condition. The welding paper’s
interference result is less reproducible because operator, magnitude and exposure
matching are unclear (Hua et al., 2026). Synthetic SNR tests do not establish natural
simultaneous degradation, and ordinary defective objects are targets rather than
corrupted modalities. Filtering or a harsh-environment title alone cannot fill a
robustness cell.


2.6.3     Temporal Misalignment and Distribution Shift

A clock offset breaks correspondence; domain shift changes the assessment distribution.
Multiple sampling rates or image repetition do not demonstrate offset tolerance.
Registration studies establish why alignment matters, while OmniFuser’s repeated
within-pass images provide a strategy rather than a synchronization-failure benchmark
(L. Chen et al., 2023; Petrich et al., 2021; Z. Wang et al., 2025).
        Stronger transfer examples include training on one HANDOVER robot and
assessing another, and Subway-trained rail localization evaluated on high-speed rail
(Thoduka et al., 2024b, PDF p. 7, Table V)(Lei & Qi, 2025, PDF p. 10, Table III).
Target-adaptive studies, target-validated condition studies and independent-build
experiments have different access assumptions (Bevans et al., 2023; Chu et al., 2024;
Huang & Tong, 2026; C. Wang et al., 2025). Rebuilding normal memories on Eyecandies
with fixed fusion hyperparameters is useful recipe transfer, but not untouched-target or
cross-machine deployment (Deng et al., 2026; J. Xu et al., 2026). Genuine temporal

                                            25
Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


offsets, calibration drift and new physical groups need separately identified protocols.


2.6.4     Quality-Aware and Uncertainty-Aware Fusion

Signal quality, confidence, calibrated probability and evidence consistency are different
quantities. QMF motivates weighting through generalization upper bounds under
assumptions about uncertainty, loss and a common distribution; this is not a guarantee
of actual-risk superiority after arbitrary shift (Q. Zhang et al., 2023, PDF pp. 4–5). The
transferable UDML experiments train on controlled Gaussian intensities and
acknowledge that sample diﬀiculty can be mistaken for modality corruption (Wei et al.,
2026, PDF pp. 4–5, 9).
        QMF itself reports NYU scene-classification accuracy of 70.09% on clean inputs,
below concatenation at 70.30%, but 55.60% at corruption intensity ten, above late
fusion at 51.99% (Q. Zhang et al., 2023, PDF p. 8, Table 1). These non-industrial
RGB–depth results demonstrate a regime-specific benefit rather than universal
dominance.
        Results can also conflict across endpoints. The disagreement add-on improves
local MVTec image AUROC from .779 to .800 but reduces Eyecandies image AUROC
from .810 to .794 while improving pixel metrics (J. Xu et al., 2026, PDF pp. 10–11,
Tables 4–5). TC-MAF’s training-dispersion proxy has a smaller contribution than its
evidence/fusion structure (Deng et al., 2026). These findings require validation of the
conditioning signal and outcomes; changing attention, an uncertainty plot or an
evidence mass does not by itself establish calibrated reliability.


2.6.5     Modality Dropout, Augmentation, and Fallback

          Mechanisms

Training dropout exposes a model to selected absence patterns, imputation generates
substitutes and deterministic fallback routes to an available predictor. These
mechanisms can yield the same output under some conditions without learning the
same recovery. Survivor routing should therefore be evaluated as a policy, while

                                             26
Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


corrupted-but-present inputs test a different prediction problem.
        Fuzzy regularization represents failures through partial corruption and
whole-input replacement by constants or noise, evaluating Gazebo pose regression under
random sample splits of overlapping windows (Altinses & Schwung, 2023, PDF pp. 3–5).
This does not establish robustness to an unavailable input branch in a physical
deployment. OmniFuser asserts dropout and masking without a quantified outage table
(Z. Wang et al., 2025, PDF p. 10). Generated replacements can harm: replacing clean
depth with CycleGAN output worsens a synthetic robot benchmark’s MSE from
2.1745 × 10−3 to 13.472 × 10−3 (Altinses & Schwung, 2025, Table 7). Exposure-matched
static controls, explicit replacements and surviving-input references are consequently
necessary to distinguish learned recovery from imposed logic.



2.7      Evaluation Beyond Accuracy — SQ4

Practical usefulness depends on how predictions are assessed, selected, and interpreted.
Accuracy can be high when the target prevalence, grouping, or threshold makes an
experiment easier than its intended deployment. SQ4 therefore requires a broader
account of evidence than a single aggregate score.


2.7.1     Grouped Evaluation and Leakage Prevention

The independent unit may be a weld, tool, participant, recording, machine or build.
Random windows can share acquisition context or raw samples. Fuzzy regularization
forms twenty-step windows with stride one and randomly partitions examples;
unseen-trajectory assessment is not established (Altinses & Schwung, 2023, PDF
pp. 4–5). Weld-run grouping is also unresolved in the closest welding studies (Fan et al.,
2026; Hua et al., 2026). These observations identify dependence risks without
measuring an invented leakage magnitude.
        HANDOVER supplies an explicit participant-disjoint oﬀicial split, while an
adapted implementation still needs participant information for safe inner folds


                                            27
Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


(Thoduka et al., 2024b). Normalization, feature selection and HPO need the same
separation (Kapoor & Narayanan, 2023; Varma & Simon, 2006). Out-of-fold stacking
can protect outer assessment, but that does not prove every deeper meta-validation
dependency is absent. Repeatedly choosing follow-ups after observing assessment
outcomes also creates selection exposure even when each fit excludes its outer test
labels.


2.7.2       Discrimination, Calibration, and Decision Thresholds

Ranking, probability quality and thresholded decisions answer different questions.
AUROC does not specify a false-alarm operating point or calibrated probability.
MVTec AD shows that good ranking can coexist with poor thresholded localization,
while strict-FPR metrics reward another aspect of detection (Bergmann et al., 2019;
Cheng et al., 2025; Costanzino et al., 2024).
          The rail vision-language paper reports pixel metrics and optimal-threshold F1;
these are not image-level alarm accuracy under a training-selected threshold (Lei & Qi,
2025, PDF p. 7). In manufacturing, accuracy .9921 alongside precision .2487 on an
imbalanced layer illustrates the effect of prevalence and threshold (Petrich et al., 2021).
Validation-fitted calibration can improve in-distribution probability quality, while
dataset shift can undermine confidence estimates (Guo et al., 2017; Ovadia et al., 2019).
Proper losses such as NLL and Brier assess probability forecasts, with an explicit
distinction between loss orientation, calibration and discrimination (Gneiting & Raftery,
2007). A generated confidence field or evidence mass cannot replace probability-quality
evaluation. Threshold selection belongs to admissible training/validation, while
assessment reveals the resulting trade-off.


2.7.3       Uncertainty and Statistical Comparisons

Seed variation, acquisition-group sampling and selection uncertainty are distinct.
HANDOVER’s standard deviations describe five trainings on a fixed test set, whereas a
group bootstrap of saved predictions samples assessment units conditional on fitted

                                              28
Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


models (Thoduka et al., 2024b). Neither automatically includes all retraining or domain
uncertainty.
        CV folds share training data. The classical variance result establishes that no
universally unbiased CV-variance estimator exists under its stated assumptions;
ignoring covariance can substantially underestimate uncertainty (Bengio & Grandvalet,
2004, PDF pp. 8–10). This does not prohibit useful conditional intervals. It requires an
explicit estimand and dependence model. Small fusion effects, unclear repetition counts
and contradictory significance prose need similar caution (Gu et al., 2024; J. Xu et al.,
2025). Paired effects on common cases are preferable to unrelated score intervals. A
zero-containing interval does not establish equivalence, and a positive point estimate
does not establish practical importance without a meaningful criterion.


2.7.4        Interpretability and Its Limits

Scores, coeﬀicients, gate traces and maps can expose model behavior without supplying
a causal explanation. The attention debate includes both counterexamples to
faithfulness and evidence that trained weights contain useful information; the
appropriate conclusion is task-specific evaluation, rather than a title-level rule
(Wiegreffe & Pinter, 2019). Allocation changes alone do not show why a model predicts
correctly.
        ZSG-IAD measures schema validity, language consistency and
coarse-to-final-mask overlap. These support particular forms of self-consistency, not
independent physical defect causation or calibrated confidence (Q. Chen et al., 2026).
HANDOVER similarly leaves explanation through human-action predictions as future
research (Thoduka et al., 2024b). In the thesis, observable quality responses and
inspectable coeﬀicients can support descriptive transparency. Expert understanding,
causal attribution and usability require further evidence. Process context must also be
distinguished from a physical equation constraint such as PGMTKD’s pressure model
(C. Li et al., 2025).

2.7.5     Modularity, Reproducibility, and Computational Cost

A modular pipeline enables controlled replacement, but does not itself establish
robustness. An identified version, accessible code, executable processing and
independently reproduced findings provide different assurances (Pineau et al., 2021).
The local-versus-published M3DM discrepancy illustrates why dependencies, valid-point
filtering, checkpoints and scoring conventions matter (J. Xu et al., 2026).
        Cost comparisons also need a common boundary. CPIR reports lower FPS than
CFM, exposing a performance–speed trade-off (Shangguan et al., 2025). A lightweight
image network’s 5.65 ms excludes an independent sensor branch and acquisition delay
(Z. Zhang et al., 2024, PDF p. 10, Table V). TimeSformer’s cost depends on ImageNet
pretraining and workload (Bertasius et al., 2021). Hardware, batch size, caching,
synchronization and included stages determine what can be compared. Prospective
deployment and operator usability remain separate validation questions rather than
consequences of a successful offline forward pass.



2.8      Critical Synthesis and Implications for This

        Thesis

The strongest precedents for the welding question combine observations of the same
physical operation and evaluate them against a defined outcome. RGB–geometry
inspection, robot simulation, and studies of learning with missing inputs contribute
evidence about particular components. Their results have different implications for the
five implemented studies because the target, observation window, and conditions of
deployment change.


2.8.1     Findings Supported Across the Reviewed Studies

Distinct physical observations can improve a task-specific pipeline. DMAW,
thermal–vibration diagnosis, HANDOVER and tool-wear prediction provide relevant



                                            30
Chapter 2. Critical Literature Review


examples under their respective protocols (Fan et al., 2026; Huo et al., 2022; Thoduka
et al., 2024b; X. Zhang et al., 2026). Controlled evidence combination can also improve
RGB–geometry inspection, including a bounded rule with an explicit missing-3D result
(Deng et al., 2026). A broad claim that these combinations or reliability approaches are
absent is therefore untenable.
        The evidence does not select a universal winner. Planned single-input
deployment, complete absence, synthetic degradation and target-domain adaptation
remain different achievements (Altinses & Schwung, 2023; C. Li et al., 2025; X. Zhao
et al., 2026). Strong representations, labels and observation windows can matter as
much as integration. The supported finding is conditional benefit under a defined task
and protocol, with distinct limitations in acquisition independence, uncertainty and
operational validation.


2.8.2     Conflicting Findings and Unresolved Questions

Negative findings are substantive evidence. Welding unimodal equality, lower
HANDOVER performance after adding gripper state, worse multimodal tool-wear
alternatives than a strong visual reference and Eyecandies image-score deterioration
challenge unconditional fusion benefit (Hua et al., 2026; Thoduka et al., 2024b; J. Xu
et al., 2026; X. Zhang et al., 2026). Category-specific geometry losses, harmful
hydraulic-channel combinations and naive multimodal optimization failures reinforce
that conclusion (Bonfiglioli et al., 2023; Goodarzi et al., 2023; Horwitz & Hoshen, 2023;
W. Wang et al., 2020).
        Possible explanations include redundancy, optimization, noise, supervision and
limited data; these studies do not identify one universal cause. Questions about
independent groups, matched static controls, informative quality conditioning and
calibrated risk remain protocol-specific. Where tables within a study disagree, the
magnitude of the reported effect remains uncertain.

2.8.3    Implications for Representation and Fusion Selection

The welding results suggest an order for comparing architectures. First establish how
well each physical input predicts the chosen target under a feasible fitting procedure
(SQ1). Then compare equal averaging, a fitted static combination, and a
context-dependent alternative with the strongest constituent predictor on the same
assessment cases (SQ2). If the learned combination introduces another calibration step,
a gain over a single branch estimates the benefit of the fitted pipeline. Isolating the
contribution of the second input also requires a single-input control with the same
opportunity for recalibration. If a gate is intended to handle failures, its static
comparator needs comparable exposure to those failures during training (SQ3). The
uncertainty of each contrast depends on the physical units that were independently
assessed (SQ4).

3.2.2     RT-DETR Architecture and Component Roles

RT-DETR is a real-time detection architecture with a convolutional backbone, a hybrid
encoder for multiscale feature interaction, and a query-based decoder that predicts
detections (Y. Zhao et al., 2024). These blocks have different purposes. Backbone maps
encode local and hierarchical image patterns. The hybrid encoder combines within- and
across-scale information. The decoder and prediction heads produce object-level
outputs. A checkpoint trained for detection may supply weights to any chosen subset of
these blocks, but the checkpoint’s origin does not establish which blocks a downstream
program executes.

3.2.3     Backbone Extraction Versus Hybrid-Encoder or

          Full-Detector Execution

The core REASSEMBLE visual extractor uses the ResNet18-vd CNN backbone inside
the pretrained RT-DETR-R18vd checkpoint (PekingU/rtdetr_r18vd). After loading
the detector, the implementation selects its backbone and freezes its weights. It
spatially averages three feature maps to form an 896-dimensional descriptor per frame,
then averages the descriptors of 16 frames. A separately trained binary head predicts
the action outcome. This forward path bypasses the hybrid Transformer encoder,
detection decoder, and box heads.
        PHM’s scalar-regression route is different. It invokes the Ultralytics prediction
path with a frozen RT-DETR-L model and hooks three multiscale maps. Pooling
produces 768 features; the saved extraction adds latency, making the head input
769-dimensional. The PHM head therefore receives both image content and a timing
covariate. REASSEMBLE calls only the detector’s CNN backbone for the core
comparison. Later CNN-versus-spatial-encoder studies are additional recipes with their
own feature widths and budgets.


3.2.4     Frame Sampling, Feature Aggregation, and Temporal

          Modelling

Fixed sampling selects a reproducible number of frames across a complete action or
episode. It makes sequence cost predictable and prevents longer clips automatically
producing more visual tokens, but can miss brief contact or failure events. A frame
mean is invariant to permutation. Boundary pooling or temporal bins retain some
coarse position; a GRU or temporal Transformer learns order-dependent interactions.
These alternatives address different hypotheses and must be evaluated using matched
sampling and fitting rules before a difference can be attributed to temporal modelling.
        The core REASSEMBLE route uses 16 deterministic hand-camera frames resized
to 640 × 640, divided by 255, without additional ImageNet mean/standard-deviation

normalization. These are actual recipe choices rather than generic defaults for every
RT-DETR implementation. Dataset examples in Chapter 6 show sampled observations;
they are illustrative, not independent evidence of label correctness or a performance
effect.


3.2.5       Frozen Extraction and Visual Fine-Tuning

Frozen extraction keeps encoder parameters fixed while fitting a head. Fine-tuning
changes some or all encoder parameters. A comparison also depends on
normalization-layer state, augmentation, learning rates, early stopping, batch size, and
optimization steps. A fixed embedding cache can make nested evaluation practical; an
end-to-end model may require a new backbone fit inside every inner split. The
additional computational burden is not a reason to treat an unmatched budget
comparison as an isolated test of freezing.
          Generic COCO initialization, REASSEMBLE donor initialization, and
target-domain head fitting are distinct interventions. A cached donor embedding proves
that extraction occurred; it does not prove that a downstream classifier and fusion
comparison completed. In ViFailback, donor feature extraction preceded the completed
downstream fits. Chapter 8 compares those fits with the matched target-task control.

3.3.2     PatchTST Architecture

PatchTST represents a time series as patches rather than treating each timestamp as a
separate token. Its original formulation combines patching with channel-independent
processing, enabling a Transformer to model temporal structure within channels (Nie
et al., 2023). An architecture inspired by this design can be initialized from scratch; that
is not pretrained time-series weight transfer. The thesis uses the term PatchTST with
the implementation specified, because PHM’s custom regressor and REASSEMBLE’s
Hugging Face classifier differ in patching, aggregation, targets, and fitting.


3.3.3     Patching, Sequence Length, Padding, and Aggregation

For sequence length L, patch width P , and stride S, an unpadded regular grid contains
1 + ⌊(L − P )/S⌋ patches when L ≥ P . Padding conventions can change that count. A
patch embedding projects its values to a hidden vector, adds positional information,
and passes the sequence through Transformer blocks. Pooling must specify whether all
patches or only valid patches contribute.
        REASSEMBLE resamples the action to 512 progress positions and 22 channels.
Its scratch classifier uses width 32, two layers, four attention heads, feed-forward width

concatenation before a 128-dimensional projection. Scaling is disabled inside the model
because standardization is supplied externally. The implementation passes an
observed-value mask to the model. The call alone does not establish that attention
excludes every missing position.

3.3.4     Representation Learning Versus Direct Prediction

A learned encoder can be used to export an embedding, to predict the target directly, or
to support a fusion head. These roles differ in where supervision enters. An encoder
trained on all labels before a cross-validation split leaks target information even if a
later classifier is refitted correctly. Conversely, a generic frozen encoder can be cached
without using target labels, provided the cache remains tied to the correct sample
identities and preprocessing. Checking for leakage in each fold requires identifying
which samples were used to fit the preprocessing steps and which samples and labels
were used to train the encoder and the final predictor.

3.4     Prediction Models and Reference Baselines

3.4.1    Constant and Simple Reference Predictors

A regression constant estimated from training data establishes whether a learned model
improves on a basic location predictor under the selected loss. The training mean
minimizes squared error, while the median minimizes absolute error in the training
sample. For classification, a constant probability or a process-context model can expose
target prevalence and contextual predictability. These controls are particularly
important when a condition proxy is correlated with run order or when action identity
predicts failure frequency.


3.4.2    Linear and Logistic Models

Ridge regression minimizes squared error plus an L2 coeﬀicient penalty. Logistic
regression models p(y = 1 | x) = sigmoid(b + w⊤ x) and typically minimizes penalized
cross-entropy. Standardization, class weighting, regularization, and solver configuration
are part of the recipe. The REASSEMBLE sensor reference searches C ∈ {0.1, 1, 10}
within training partitions. A fit of the same model family to two branch probabilities is
a stacker, not a sensor representation. The fixed core F2 value C = 1 is not described as
a searched hyperparameter.


3.4.3    Neural Classification and Regression Heads

A compact head can use h = ReLU(W1 x + b1 ), dropout during fitting, and a scalar
output. In REASSEMBLE, the core visual and PatchTST heads used class-weighted
binary cross-entropy. The original context gates used unweighted binary cross-entropy.
Parameter counts describe the size of each fitted model. The visual head had 114,945
trainable parameters in addition to the frozen CNN. The sensor module had 109,505
trainable parameters across its PatchTST encoder, projection, and classifier, including
90,369 in the projection and classifier.

3.5     Fusion Mechanisms

3.5.1    Feature Concatenation

Feature-level fusion applies a predictor to [hv ; hs ], possibly after separate projection and
normalization. If qm = ϕm (hm ), one implementation is p = sigmoid(g([qv ; qs ])). It can
learn cross-feature interactions while increasing fitted capacity. The retained PHM
fused_all_three specifically concatenates embeddings from process-context, RMS and
condition-indicator encoders before a scalar head. Despite the class name
LateFusionRegressor, its forward path is feature fusion, rather than probability
stacking or image–sensor inference. Equal final embedding width does not imply equal
total encoder capacity.


3.5.2    Uniform Prediction Averaging

The core F1 baseline is pF1 = (pv + ps )/2. It has no fitted combination parameters,
although its branch predictors and calibrators are fitted. Its simplicity makes it a useful
comparator for a learned stacker. Applying a sigmoid to the mean logit generally yields
a different probability. When an input is missing, a rule for renormalization or
abstention must be specified separately.

3.5.6     Missing-Modality Routing and Fallback

With one available branch, Equation 3.2 sets its weight to one. The original
REASSEMBLE robustness evaluator returns that branch’s probability and uses its
threshold. The core gate rejects inputs for which both branches are absent.
Training-time dropout can affect the gate’s behavior when both inputs are present. In
the single-survivor case, the routing rule already fixes the output.
        Distillation is a different mechanism: multimodal information during training
can improve a student deployed with one input. Imputation is another: it estimates an
absent representation and introduces reconstruction assumptions. These mechanisms
need separate comparisons with a student trained directly on the surviving input. The
robustness outcome depends on which inputs were available during fitting and which
were lost during assessment.
        ModDrop exposes fusion to absent branches during fitting (Neverova et al.,
2015); EmbraceNet combines stochastic feature selection with known availability masks
(Choi & Lee, 2019); masked multimodal-Transformer training supports complete and
incomplete input tasks (M. Ma et al., 2022). Their missingness units, training exposure
and fallback assumptions differ. These transferable methods and their counterexamples
motivate an independently trained surviving-modality baseline; they do not establish
robustness of the thesis gate without testing its actual computation.

4.6     Grouping, Data Splits, and Leakage Prevention

The split unit determines the scope of the generalization claim. The principal PHM
sensor protocol holds out gear experiments, REASSEMBLE groups actions by
recording, MSM examines experiment and date groupings, and ViFailback separates
tasks. HANDOVER retains the published trial partitions. The available identifiers
allow neither independent verification of the reported participant separation nor
construction of participant-disjoint inner folds. For MSM, a date can span multiple
tools or operators, and physical equipment may recur across dates. A claim about a
new machine or factory requires grouping information at that level.
       Within an outer split, feature scaling, imputations estimated from data, model
selection, calibration, thresholds, and fusion fitting must use training-side information.
For stacking, the training bank contains predictions from models that did not fit the
corresponding labels. If a branch recipe is selected, that selection also belongs inside the
permitted training hierarchy. Even with an out-of-fold prediction head, assessment
information can enter the model through an encoder or normalizer fitted on the full
dataset. Each base prediction for the original REASSEMBLE stacker came from a
branch fitted without data from the corresponding recording. Within each outer split,
internal stacker validation reused the same out-of-fold (OOF) prediction bank built
from the outer training recordings. A branch that supplied a prediction for a stacker
training row could have been fitted on recordings held out for that stacker validation
split. The outer-assessment recordings remained excluded from fitting. Full
independence at the deeper validation stage has yet to be demonstrated. This
dependency limits the claim of strict nesting.
       The original REASSEMBLE cohort comprises 4,530 actions, 509 failures, and
148 recordings. Its five held-out partitions together cover this population. A later
753-action comparison uses one of these exposed outer partitions, not a new external
holdout. Cohort reconciliation uses sample and recording identities to distinguish

4.7     Model Fitting, Selection, Calibration, and

        Threshold Procedures

Each comparison defines trainable components, initialization, selection candidates,
training budget, and stopping rules. An epoch count alone does not establish matched
optimization if samples, batch sizes, early stopping, or cached representations differ.
The experimental programme included original comparisons, exploratory pilots and
matched short-budget diagnostics. Some larger experiments completed only a subset of
the planned fits.
       Calibration maps raw model scores to probabilities using training-side
predictions. Discrimination and calibration are different properties: a monotonic score
transformation can preserve ranking while changing probability errors. The core branch
calibrators use sigmoid logistic maps on selected inner out-of-fold scores. The original
softmax gates have no free final intercept or post-gate calibrator, whereas F2 has a free
intercept and no additional post-stacker calibration. These function-class and
post-processing choices remain visible in the comparison (Guo et al., 2017).
       A decision threshold is selected using permitted training predictions and a stated
objective, such as balanced accuracy. The original implementation searches a grid from
.01 to .99. Such a threshold is a protocol choice, not a cost-optimized industrial alarm
policy. At that threshold, sensitivity, specificity, precision, and false-alert burden give a
clearer account of performance than accuracy alone. Assessing operational acceptance
also requires information about stakeholder costs.

a PHM report uses 10,000 run-cluster replicates over 20 runs. These are intervals
conditional on the saved fitted models and the chosen resampling unit. They do not
capture all retraining, model-selection, seed, or new-site uncertainty. Five MSM dates
provide a particularly small basis for a date-cluster interval. Cross-validation folds are
overlapping training experiments, not independent replications; uncertainty should not
be inferred from a naive standard error over folds (Bengio & Grandvalet, 2004).

4.9     Robustness Evaluation Design

A robustness test specifies how the failure occurs, which modality and stage it affects,
and how severe it is. It also reports the number of realizations, any exposure to the
failure during training, and the observations and metric used. The availability mask
identifies a stream declared missing. A corrupted stream can remain available and
supply a prediction. Corrupting pixels targets a different stage from corrupting cached
visual features. Sensor corruption can affect raw channels, statistics, or logits.
Recompute any quality features meant to describe the corrupted observation.
       Clean and degraded absolute scores are both needed. A smaller clean-relative
drop can coexist with worse absolute performance. A gate can shift weight away from a
corrupted modality while still underperforming the intact unimodal predictor. The
relevant controls include the surviving branch, a static fusion model with matched
augmentation exposure, and any simpler parameterization needed to isolate adaptation.
Training with modality dropout establishes exposure to missing inputs. Recovery is
measured on held-out cases where the input is absent.
       The original two-input gate routes a single surviving branch with weight one.
With fixed branch outputs this makes single-input predictions identical regardless of
gate training. Both missing inputs raise an error; no predictive result is attributed to
that state. These tests leave open the effects of clock offsets, previously unseen
machines and novel failure mechanisms.

5.6.1     Motivation and Target Definition

Without organizer-supplied continuous damage labels, an explicit and reproducible
candidate-area statistic offered an engineering starting point. The implementation
measures dark, horizontally extended regions within a chosen flank ROI. Its percentage
denominator is the ROI area, so a change in ROI changes the numerical scale even if
the same pixels are selected. The term “candidate” is substantive: reflectance,
machining texture and acquisition artifacts may satisfy the image rule without being
physical damage. The target can consequently support a study of proxy prediction and
measurement sensitivity. It cannot by itself support a claim about tooth material loss
or maintenance necessity.


5.6.2     Image Processing and Aggregation

The inspected target code converts RGB to Lab luminance, applies CLAHE, estimates
a smooth background, and obtains a dark residual. Robust standardized residuals
define candidate pixels; morphological opening and closing and component-size/aspect
criteria retain extended regions. Candidate pixels divided by ROI pixels give the
per-view percentage. Historical v2 view maxima and the v3 deterministic-view rule then
produce tooth values, followed by the top-three run mean.
        The PHM scalar baseline invoked the Ultralytics RT-DETR-L prediction path
and recorded intermediate maps from three layers. The regression head used 768
spatially pooled image features and one additional value, extraction latency. Latency
depends on the computing environment and can confound interpretation of the
image-regression result. Separate PHM detection and multitask experiments used
heuristic pseudo-box supervision. The core REASSEMBLE classifier used the
ResNet18-vd CNN backbone inside its RT-DETR-R18vd checkpoint (Chapter 6).

6.1       Study Objectives and Research-Question

          Mapping

SQ1 concerns implemented visual and temporal representations relative to engineered
sensor statistics. SQ2 concerns early, late and adaptive fusion under shared recording
groups. SQ3 distinguishes known missing modalities, synthetic degradation of a present
modality and broader distribution shifts. SQ4 concerns the controls needed to interpret
these comparisons: native labels, group separation, inner selection, calibration, paired
uncertainty and computational budgets. The evaluation therefore includes simple
reference predictors and a strong unimodal competitor before interpreting multimodal
gains.
         The task is retrospective classification of an already completed action. It is
relevant to monitoring and automated inspection of industrial manipulation, but it is
not a direct replication of rotating-machine damage diagnosis. The observation horizon
includes the action’s end. Consequently, a representation that exploits late frames can
be useful here without demonstrating prediction before an unsuccessful event. The
dataset paper supplies acquisition context; the retained manifests and implementation
establish this thesis’s specific population and task (Sliwowski et al., 2025).

6.3     Dataset Challenges and Modality Availability

The retained modalities are hand-camera images and machine-sensor measurements:
three force channels, three torque channels, two gripper channels, seven joint-effort
channels and seven joint-velocity channels. These twenty-two channels include
operating state as well as direct interaction measurements. Complementarity is thus an
empirical question, because both modalities may encode action identity or progress
rather than independent evidence of failure.

audio, so the predictive experiment excluded the audio branch. This is an alignment
limitation, not a result showing that an audio model failed to learn. Similarly, seventeen
object categories do not establish seventeen disjoint physical objects. The available
relationship graph did not support a verified object-instance-disjoint test. The
recording-disjoint split evaluates transfer between recordings. Generalization to unseen
objects or sites and hardware-clock synchronization remain untested.

Sixteen distinct frames are selected deterministically at approximately uniform
positions within each completed action. The selection uses the recorded action interval;
it does not learn where failure begins. Sensor observations are associated with that
same action and resampled to 512 normalized progress positions. These positions are
not a 512-Hz sampling rate. Normalized progress enables fixed-shape fitting across
different action durations while changing the temporal interpretation of features.

They do not prove sub-frame synchronization from a shared hardware clock. Boundary
errors can affect both modalities jointly, and late visual information may reflect the

6.4.3      Missing, Invalid, and Inconsistent Observations

Sensor extraction requires at least 0.89 finite coverage per channel and avoids
extrapolation and interpolation across long gaps. Accepted residual missing values are
filled with training-partition means after channel standardization. Masks remain part of
the input preparation, but the retained PatchTST configuration does not convert the
observed mask into attention suppression. Passing a mask to an API and enforcing
missing-token attention are therefore different claims.

The gripper median-absolute-deviation outlier statistic is undefined for 1,562 rows and
is imputed from training data; sixteen quality fields are constant. These details qualify
the interpretation of a quality-aware gate: its input name alone does not establish that
it receives a reliable independent quality estimate. Conversely, the deliberate
complete-modality-loss experiments supply a known availability mask, which is stronger
information than detecting an unnoticed hardware failure.


6.4.4      Human Annotation Review and Its Limits

One reviewer completed 64 selected clips. Of 59 determinate judgments, 57 agreed with
the native outcome and two disagreed; five of 64 were indeterminate. Agreement among

6.4.5     Group Definitions and Split Checks

Five outer folds hold out whole recordings, with four recording-disjoint inner folds for
selection. Outer assessment populations contain 753, 964, 1,037, 906 and 870 actions;
their failure counts are 77, 109, 125, 99 and 99, respectively. The corresponding
recording counts are 26, 31, 32, 29 and 30. Summing the assessment rows recovers all
4,530 actions, 509 failures and 148 recordings exactly.
        Within each outer training set, the visual and sensor branches supplied
out-of-fold (OOF) predictions for training the logistic stacker. Each branch predicted an
action using a fit that excluded its recording. During inner validation of the stacker,
however, every split reused this bank. Some rows used to train the stacker could
therefore come from branch fits that had seen recordings assigned to the stacker’s inner
validation split. This indirect dependency affects internal model and threshold
assessment. It does not extend to the outer test: those recordings were excluded from
branch fitting, calibration, stacker training and threshold selection. Fully independent
inner validation would require rebuilding the branch prediction bank within every
stacker-validation split, including repeated branch fits and calibration across all five
outer folds. This additional nesting was beyond the training budget, so the outer scores
evaluate the procedure that reused the outer-training bank.



6.5      Task Formulation, Model Inputs, and Shared

         Evaluation Protocol

The positive class is failure. The principal ranking measure is average precision (AP),
computed by the average-precision implementation rather than trapezoidal integration
of a precision–recall curve. AUROC, balanced accuracy, macro-F1, failure recall and
precision, Brier score and fifteen-bin equal-width expected calibration error (ECE)

                                            77
Chapter 6. REASSEMBLE Case Study: Representation, Fusion, and Robustness


describe other aspects of performance. Precision–recall reporting is particularly
important at the observed minority-class prevalence (Saito & Rehmsmeier, 2015).
        Unless a later subsection states otherwise, reported core scores pool each action’s
outer-held-out probability across the five folds. Pooled AP is not the arithmetic mean
of five fold AP values. Training-only selection and sigmoid logistic calibration use
appropriate inner outputs. Hard-decision thresholds maximize inner balanced accuracy
over 0.01–0.99, with ties resolved toward 0.5. Calibration and threshold selection are
separate operations. Two thousand paired recording-cluster bootstrap resamples
quantify uncertainty conditional on the retained fitted models; they do not refit the
entire procedure or estimate all training-seed uncertainty. Nineteen structured
label-shift permutations preserve recording/action structure under their conditional
exchangeability assumption, giving a smallest plus-one probability of 0.05.



6.6       Experiment A: Unimodal Representations

The unimodal study establishes a reference hierarchy before fusion. Class-prior, majority
and action-type predictors reveal what can be achieved without detailed observations.
Engineered sensor statistics are then compared with the frozen visual representation
and a compact sensor Transformer. Identical retained action identifiers and recording
groups prevent changes in population from masquerading as representation gains.


6.6.1     Objective and Comparison Rationale

The unimodal comparison included simple predictors that can exploit process or sensor
information beyond class prevalence. The action-type predictor controls for the large
native failure-rate differences between pick, insert, remove and place. The
sensor-statistics model controls for useful low-complexity descriptions of force, torque
and motion. The two neural branches test different inductive biases within bounded
budgets; they do not represent exhaustive optimization of all visual or time-series
models.


                                            78
Chapter 6. REASSEMBLE Case Study: Representation, Fusion, and Robustness


        Both neural branches improved AP over the action baseline. Their AUROC
differences remained unresolved. A later test of incremental predictive value motivated
the visual–sensor fusion comparison.


6.6.2     Visual Representation Configurations

The core visual extractor loads the COCO-pretrained RT-DETR-R18vd checkpoint
(PekingU/rtdetr_r18vd) at the recorded revision. From the loaded detector instance,
it selects wrapper.model.backbone.model, the checkpoint’s own ResNet18-vd CNN
backbone, and freezes its parameters. The forward pass reads this backbone’s feature
maps and bypasses the hybrid Transformer encoder, detection decoder, and box heads
(Y. Zhao et al., 2024).
        Frames are resized to 640 × 640 using bilinear interpolation, converted to RGB
and divided by 255 without ImageNet mean/standard-deviation normalization. Spatial
means from three scales concatenate to 896 features per frame; the sixteen frame
descriptors are averaged. A frozen extractor feeds a 896 → 128 → 1 ReLU/dropout
head with 114,945 trainable parameters. Whole-action averaging discards temporal
order. Weighted binary cross-entropy, AdamW with learning rate 0.001 and weight
decay 0.001, batch size 128 and gradient clipping at one are used. Inner selection
chooses five or ten epochs; outer budgets are 5, 5, 10, 10 and 5.


6.6.3     Statistical Sensor and PatchTST Configurations

The statistical representation computes ten features per channel: mean, standard
deviation, median, minimum, maximum, lower and upper quartiles, range, RMS and
progress slope. Twenty-two channels therefore produce 220 features. A
training-standardized, class-balanced L2 logistic model selects C from 0.1, 1 and 10 by
mean inner AP. A subsequent unweighted C = 1 logistic sigmoid calibrates selected
out-of-fold raw scores.
        The neural branch is a scratch-trained Hugging Face PatchTST-style model with
context 512 × 22, patch length 32, stride 16, hidden width 32, two layers, four heads

                                           79
Chapter 6. REASSEMBLE Case Study: Representation, Fusion, and Robustness


and feed-forward width 64. Internal scaling is disabled; layer normalization and dropout
0.1 are used. Mean patch pooling and channel concatenation feed a 128-dimensional
projection and binary head, giving 109,505 trainable parameters. The observed mask is
passed but does not suppress attention in this configuration. Inner selection allows
three or six epochs and chooses six in every outer fold. This is a compact adaptation of
the patching idea, not a claim to reproduce all settings of the original PatchTST paper
(Nie et al., 2023).

6.7      Experiment B: Fusion Strategies

Six fusion families share the cohort and recording structure: probability averaging,
stacking, representation concatenation and three context-dependent gates. Their input
spaces and calibration stages differ.


6.7.1     Objective and Hypotheses

The first hypothesis is that the combined visual–sensor pipeline improves held-out
failure ranking relative to the retained sensor pipeline. The second is that action or
quality context improves allocation between branches. These questions are distinct: a
fixed map can use complementary predictions without an effective per-instance
reliability estimate. The incremental comparison also changes the fitted probability

benefit of an additional calibration or stacking stage. An otherwise identical sensor-only
meta-calibration control was not completed. Both sensor-only and simple static fusion
references are therefore needed when interpreting adaptive models.
        Clean gains do not establish corruption recognition; the subsequent experiment
tests frozen clean-trained models directly.

6.8      Experiment C: Missing and Degraded

         Modalities

The core robustness study froze the clean-trained statistical sensor and visual predictors
(U1 and U2), equal averaging (F1), the stacker (F2), and the two gates using quality
descriptors (F5 and F6). It perturbed one input at a time and also tested known loss of
a full input stream.


6.8.1     Robustness Questions and Tested Conditions

The central question is whether a gate both changes allocation in the expected direction
and limits predictive deterioration relative to F2. Absolute performance at high severity
is also necessary: a weak clean model can show a small decline merely because little
useful ranking remains to lose. An intact unimodal branch supplies a practical reference
for whether fusion introduces avoidable damage.
        Clean decision thresholds remain frozen during corruption. This matches a
deployment in which the operator does not receive fault labels and recalibrate at every
severity. Known availability masks in complete-dropout tests are an explicitly different
information condition. The two sets of results measure responses to different
information about the fault.


6.8.2     Corruption Operators and Severity Definitions

Visual operators include pixel Gaussian noise with standard deviations 0.02, 0.05 and
0.10 on the normalized scale, Gaussian blur at severities one, two and four, and removal
of two, five or eight of sixteen selected frames. Sensor operators include Gaussian noise
scaled by 0.1, 0.5 or 1.0 training-channel standard deviations; removal of two, seven or
eleven channels; missing temporal blocks covering 10%, 30% or 50% of the sequence;
and six force/torque channels stuck at training medians.

                                           85
Chapter 6. REASSEMBLE Case Study: Representation, Fusion, and Robustness


        Sensor statistics and quality descriptors are recomputed from the altered input,
rather than retaining clean summaries beside corrupted signals. The core uses its fixed
operator realization protocol. A later augmentation study averages across five
stochastic test realizations. Its Gaussian-noise estimates therefore refer to a different
evaluation protocol. None of these operators is direct evidence of cross-machine shift,
clock drift or physical sensor recalibration.


6.8.3     Missingness Policies and Dropout Experiments

In the original complete-dropout test, the four evaluated fusion models used the
statistical sensor probability and threshold when vision was absent. When sensors were
absent, they used the visual predictor and its threshold. The evaluator received the
identity of the missing stream. The core gate rejects a sample with both streams absent.
The robustness report contains no empirical result for that condition. A later
quality-interaction pilot marked such predictions unavailable.
        A separate F6 training experiment samples visual-only dropout with probability
0.15, sensor-only dropout with probability 0.15 and both-present inputs with
probability 0.70. Both-absent inputs are never sampled. Because a
one-available-modality softmax has weight exactly one and the branches are fixed, its
missing-case output is structurally fixed and supplies no useful gradient for improving
the fallback prediction. Dropout training cannot make that output better under this
architecture. Its clean AP is 0.4377, changing ordinary F6 by −0.0037 [−.0085, .0003].


6.8.4     Predictive Performance and Calibration Under

          Degradation

Under strongest pixel Gaussian noise, F2 retains AP 0.3963, while F5 and F6 fall to
0.1288 and 0.1275. The intact sensor baseline remains at AP 0.4294. F2’s Brier score
nevertheless rises to 0.2968 and ECE to 0.4368, compared with clean sensor values
0.0804 and 0.0174. Ranking retention therefore conceals severe probability failure.
        Under sensor Gaussian noise at one training standard deviation, F2 AP is 0.2222

                                                86
Chapter 6. REASSEMBLE Case Study: Representation, Fusion, and Robustness


and F5/F6 are 0.1753/0.1788, below intact vision at 0.2307. F2’s Brier/ECE rise to
0.4798/0.5251. Strong blur gives F2/F5/F6 AP 0.4099/0.4061/0.4179, without a

intact and corrupted modality can underperform the intact branch. Detection of
corruption and calibrated uncertainty remain unresolved rather than being supplied

6.8.5     Gate Behaviour and Fallback Behaviour

No tested original gate/family combination establishes both an appropriate allocation
response and a smaller decline under the study’s criteria; no supported absolute
high-severity AP superiority follows. A gate weight can change with a quality input
without changing in a beneficial way. Conversely, a smaller performance decline from a
weaker clean starting point is insuﬀicient to establish improved reliability.
        Exact missing-modality equality follows programmed fallback. It demonstrates
consistent handling of known absence, while corruption recognition and useful recovery
remain separate capabilities. This is narrower than training an encoder or student to
retain performance after input loss, as in transferable missing-modality methods (Choi
& Lee, 2019; M. Ma et al., 2022; Neverova et al., 2015).


6.8.6     Interpretation and Untested Conditions

The core evidence rejects a broad claim of reliable adaptation for the tested gates. The
AP, calibration and intact-branch comparisons capture different consequences of
corruption. Robustness to known complete absence is largely architectural here;
robustness to unnoticed corruption is empirically weak.
        Natural missingness mechanisms, simultaneous partial failures, asynchronous
clocks, new machines, new operating sites and prospective early warning were not
established. The later augmentation study changes training exposure and tests several
held-out operator families, but the same recording population and already observed core
faults limit claims of independent discovery. An untested condition remains open rather

than receiving a favorable interpretation from the model’s name.

The clearest result was the improvement in failure ranking from sensor statistics alone

7.3     Dataset Challenges

Milling video can show the machine and process context while coolant, enclosure
geometry and occlusion conceal the tool–workpiece interaction. Successful decoding is
therefore insuﬀicient evidence that the labeled abnormality is optically observable. A
visual classifier may respond to operating context, lighting or experiment-specific
appearance rather than the underlying cutting event. The substantial visual
training–assessment gap reported below is compatible with this concern, but does not
identify its cause.
       The machine stream is also an observation of the process rather than direct,
instantaneous ground truth. Aggregated electrical, load and vibration-related
measurements can describe an interval while smoothing short events. Their timing and
update cadence limit any claim about event onset or control latency. Group identity
creates a further diﬀiculty: experiment folders provide a useful split boundary, but they
are not verified identities for physical tools, workpieces or sessions. These limits
motivate both the technical gates and the stricter grouping sensitivities.

8.6     Evaluation Protocol

The original protocol uses three outer task-disjoint folds and three inner OOF
assessments within each outer-training pool. Each branch separates gradient-fitting T,

checkpoint-selection V, calibration C and assessment A task groups. A final branch is
not automatically fitted to the entire outer-training pool: its T/V/C roles remain
distinct. The inner assessment probabilities build a meta bank without using examples
that fitted, selected or calibrated their generating branch.

calibrated on C, and assessment uses a fixed threshold of 0.5. Failure AP, success AP
and their arithmetic mean are retained. With failure prevalence above 85%,

calculated from disjoint outer predictions, separately for each seed. It is not the

9.5     Agreement and Disagreement With Previous

        Research

Evidence from welding itself qualifies the expectation of a fusion advantage. In one
penetration-state study, the clean image-only, audio-only, and fused models each
reached 100% accuracy (Hua et al., 2026, Tables 1–3). Distinct physical measurements
may be available even when the reported endpoint leaves no measurable room for a
clean gain. In the published HANDOVER study, five-class outcome accuracy averaged
over five trainings was 69.1% with video and force/torque and 67.9% after gripper state
was added (Thoduka et al., 2024b, Table III). The present HANDOVER
implementation uses binary outcomes, different models, and failure AP. The common
finding is that an additional stream can lower performance on a shared outcome.
Absolute scores from the two experiments answer different questions.
       Other industrial studies report clear gains within their respective protocols.
Acoustic–thermal gearbox monitoring achieved 99.54% mean accuracy against 90.54%
for the stronger individual thermal stream on a random within-condition split (Ying
et al., 2025, Table 2). On MVTec 3D-AD, combining RGB and 3D inputs raised mean
image AUROC to 93.7% from 88.0% for RGB alone (Rudolph et al., 2023, Table 3).
These results concern different acquisition regimes and outcomes from completed-action
failure. Conversely, studies of multimodal optimization and industrial input subsets
identify losses against stronger individual channels (Goodarzi et al., 2023; Horwitz &
Hoshen, 2023; W. Wang et al., 2020). REASSEMBLE and the external studies provide
evidence in both directions. The performance differences alone cannot identify whether
limited visibility, an unsuitable representation, or the fusion training procedure caused a
particular loss.
       Previous work provides both a theory and experimental tests of quality-aware
weighting (Q. Zhang et al., 2023). RGB–geometry research tests absent 3D input (Deng
et al., 2026, Table 3), and fuzzy-regularized camera–telemetry fusion tests corruption in
simulated robot pose regression (Altinses & Schwung, 2023). Training-time removal of

malfunction, and adaptation with target-domain data impose different information
constraints. The remaining question for a particular industrial task is whether a quality

comparable observations, calibration, and training exposure.

9.7     Threats to Validity and Unresolved Evidence

Three features of the data limit a causal interpretation of architectural differences.
First, the target may only approximate the phenomenon of interest. PHM has no
independent quantitative tooth-damage reference, MSM’s label mapping defines which
intervals count as abnormal, and some robotic outcomes remained ambiguous under
visual review. Second, the number of independently acquired units can be much smaller
than the number of predictions. Gear experiments, recording sessions, dates, tasks, and
participants capture different sources of dependence. Where identifiers were missing,
independence across those groups could not be verified. Third, several later analyses
revisited assessment populations examined during development. Intervals conditional on
fitted predictions and variation over a few seeds do not capture that selection history.
       Preprocessing before the separate four-arm PHM loop limits attribution of its
results to architecture. In other experiments, differences in calibration, capacity,
training budget or corruption exposure accompanied architectural changes. Some
historical configurations lack the detail needed to reconstruct training exactly.
ViFailback evaluated clean predictions from frozen visual backbones. Target-CNN
adaptation and corruption effects were outside the final evaluation (Chapter 8). The
literature synthesis covers the full texts available to a single reviewer.
       Finally, the completed-window studies allowed the predictor to observe evidence
near the outcome. Synthetic blur, noise, or stream removal test specified interventions.
They give limited information about gradual calibration drift, unobserved timing faults,
or shifts to another physical machine. Transferring a predictor to an earlier decision
time or a different installation requires observations at that time and place.



9.8     Implications for Multimodal System Design

In a monitoring application, the intended decision determines what constitutes failure,
how it can be confirmed independently, and which observations arrive in time to be
useful. Evaluating each stream on the same held-out units establishes what it can


                                             125
Chapter 9. Integrated Discussion


predict alone. Equal averaging and a fitted static combination then provide references
for testing whether context-dependent weights add value. When an instrument declares
an outage, the remaining predictor supplies a natural reference. When faulty values still
appear plausible, the relevant test is whether the gate recognizes situations in which
trusting that stream would worsen the prediction.
       A prospective return to welding could reserve whole welding sessions, collect an
independent weld-quality measure, and fix the prediction horizon before examining
those outcomes. On the same welds, exposure- and calibration-matched models could
be assessed for clean performance, known absence, degraded readings, timing error, and
shifts between operating conditions. Paired effects and a prespecified tolerance for
clean-performance loss would clarify the operational cost of any robustness gain. Such a
test would show whether the modest gain for completed assembly actions carries over to
weld-quality decisions.

reference, and the preprocessing order in its separate four-arm diagnostic prevents a
leakage-free interpretation of that experiment. The industrial and robotic datasets offer
incomplete information about independence at the level of tools, objects, or
participants. Complete-action and complete-episode predictions can use evidence that
only becomes visible near the outcome. Deploying a warning system would require a
prediction horizon that excludes such information.
       The comparison also depends on how the models were fitted. Calibration,
training budgets, and exposure to corruptions differed in some comparisons. Several
follow-ups revisited previously evaluated populations, and the reported bootstrap
intervals condition on the fitted models. The short-budget fine-tuning comparison did
not resolve a mechanism for the observed differences. Some longer training runs and
planned sensor-degradation evaluations remained incomplete. Where only run
summaries were available, they limited the scope for independent checks of individual
predictions.



10.4       Priorities for Future Research

An independent welding study is the most direct next test of the original question. It
should specify the weld-level outcome and prediction time, pair the camera and process
measurements to that decision, and reserve complete new welding sessions for
assessment. An independent inspection should establish weld quality. For PHM, a
physical measure of damage linked to a known tooth would likewise distinguish
measurement validity from the ability to predict the photographic proxy. Independent
review of ambiguous robotic outcomes would strengthen the corresponding classification
targets.

Chapter 10. Conclusion and Future Work


       Within each study, the informative comparison is between the strongest
single-input predictor, equal averaging, a fitted static combination, and a gate trained
with comparable calibration opportunities and corruption exposure. Repeated tests of
known absence, unannounced corruption, temporal misalignment, and shifts between
physical groups would show which failure conditions justify adaptive weighting.
Prespecified margins for clean performance and grouped uncertainty estimates would
make the cost of a robustness improvement visible.
       The remaining transfer experiments require matched optimization budgets,
normalization procedures, and held-out tasks. For operational monitoring, prospective
tests should also measure warning time, false alarms, and end-to-end delay, and
evaluate how operators act on uncertain predictions. These measurements would
establish whether the modest ranking benefit observed on completed assembly actions
translates into a useful decision while a weld is still in progress.

met the inclusion criteria, 37 were excluded and 44 remained potentially relevant but

       The subsequent appraisal covered 59 PDF files. Two pairs were byte-identical,

these represented 57 additional works, bringing the total appraised to 94. This total

representation methods, dataset descriptions and contextual studies.

A.2      Extraction, Locators, and Versions

For each study, the appraisal recorded the prediction unit and target, physical inputs,
derived representations, fusion method, single-input comparisons and assignment of
data to training and evaluation. Missing inputs, noisy inputs and changes of operating
conditions were coded separately. Numerical findings cite the relevant page, table or
figure. Where the available text left a procedure unspecified, its status was recorded as
unknown.
       The appraisal distinguishes the published bibliographic record from the version
examined. Preprints and journal articles sometimes give different sample counts for the
same work. File hashes identify the version used for each extraction. The 37 initial
full-text records include 148 short quotations that locate the evidence for their codes.
The review compared reported methods and results without rerunning the published
experiments.

       Within-protocol input variants do not guarantee equal capacity, pretraining,
calibration or selection budget. Planned single-input deployment, missing training
images, generated substitution, stochastic dropout and unexpected inference outage are
different conditions. Ordinary defective objects, natural geometric gaps and fixed noisy
data are not automatically controlled corruption experiments. The codes describe this
inspected evidence and cannot estimate field-wide prevalence.

C.2      Temporal Representations
The PHM implementation defines a compact channel-independent PatchTST-style regressor

with default patch length sixteen, stride eight, hidden width 32, four attention heads, two

encoder layers, feed-forward width 64, dropout 0.1 and a 64-unit head. The configured input

width remains run-specific. A scalar output and the optional sorted-profile head answer

different target questions. The latter uses a base and positive softplus increments to enforce

ascending order; this guarantees an order-statistic profile but discards tooth identity. The

monotonicity does not demonstrate that the estimated profile matches actual damage.

       The REASSEMBLE temporal branch follows a separate configuration: patch length

32, stride sixteen, width 32, two layers, four heads, feed-forward width 64 and dropout 0.1. It

begins without pretrained temporal weights. Mean patch pooling and channel concatenation

feed a 128-dimensional projection and binary head, yielding 109,505 parameters in the

inspected implementation. Observed-value masks are retained and passed through the relevant

interface, but that does not establish attention suppression of every missing token. Missing

values are filled under training-only normalization. The source-specific mask semantics

therefore remain part of the implementation limitation rather than being inferred from the

model family (Nie et al., 2023).



C.3      Heads, Fitting and Calibration
The original visual head is a new 896–128–1 network with ReLU and dropout 0.1, containing

114,945 trainable parameters above a frozen backbone. Original visual epoch candidates are

five and ten; temporal candidates are three and six. Both use AdamW with learning rate

0.001, weight decay 0.001 and batch size 128. These candidates constitute the recorded

                                              160
Appendix C. Model Configurations and Implementation Details


selection procedure, not a broad optimizer search. Logistic regression separately compares

C ∈ {0.1, 1, 10} under inner-fold selection.

       Branch calibration used an unweighted sigmoid logistic fit to the selected inner

out-of-fold scores. The outer assessment labels were withheld from calibration. The original

procedure selected a threshold between 0.01 and 0.99 by maximizing inner out-of-fold balanced

accuracy, resolving ties toward 0.5. Later studies used a fixed threshold of 0.5. Comparisons

between those procedures therefore combine the effects of model and threshold changes.



C.4        Fusion Mechanisms and Attribution
Uniform fusion averages the two calibrated branch probabilities. F2 fits an unweighted L2

logistic model to those probabilities, with C = 1 and a new intercept and two coeﬀicients. It is

neither an F-beta metric nor a convex weighted average. Its coeﬀicients depend on score

scaling and are not causal percentages of modality importance.

       Feature fusion F3 combines a 128-dimensional visual projection with the 220 statistical

numerical features through separate projections, normalization and a fitted classifier. Its

recorded candidates use hidden widths 32 or 64 and ten or twenty epochs. Context gates

instead allocate weights over branch logits. Their candidates have hidden width eight or

sixteen and forty epochs. Action-only, quality-only and combined contexts remain different

models, as do a static gate with context held constant and a gate exposed to corrupted OOF

records.

       With one branch available, the explicitly masked convex-logit gate gives that branch a

weight of one. The resulting prediction follows from the specified masking rule. When both

branches are absent, some implementations reject the input and the external evaluation

records abstention and coverage.

       The later anchored adjustment is another distinct mechanism. It modifies frozen F2

probability coeﬀicients by bounded context-dependent factors, with a zero-initialized adaptive

output. It is not the same convex logit gate and need not have the same calibration or

threshold policy. Appropriate static and exposure-matched controls are necessary to identify

what, if anything, adaptation adds.

Research Chronology


G.1       Original Objective and Dataset Selection
The original research objective concerned process-aware multimodal architectures for robotic

welding. The exposé defined the research questions before the subsequent industrial and

robotic studies. The choice and timing of those studies were exploratory. Their relationship to

the original questions is a retrospective interpretation.



G.2       From the Welding Motivation to Two

          Task-Specific Studies
The PHM study began with image exploration and developed a continuous gear-condition

proxy. The September v3 target used one deterministic view per tooth, teeth 5–28 and a

top-three run aggregate. The earlier maximum-over-views targets and fixed image split

addressed different prediction settings. The available records do not establish the initial

reason for selecting PHM. Concerns about the validity of the proxy emerged through its

construction and evaluation.

       Re-examination of the Intel welding data identified shared sessions across partitions

and prior access to the proposed test set. No independent multimodal evaluation of weld

quality followed. PHM and REASSEMBLE provided settings for the architecture and

evaluation questions. In REASSEMBLE, native action outcomes and corresponding camera

G.3       Follow-Ups, Human Review and External Cases
September follow-ups examined longer sensor training, a same-sensor hybrid, anchored

H.1       Objective and Relationship to the Research

          Questions
HANDOVER tests whether paired video and force–torque signals can identify the recorded

outcome of a completed human–robot handover. The single-input predictors address

representation choice (SQ1), and averaging their probabilities tests the contribution of the

second input on the same trials (SQ2). For SQ3, controlled blur, sensor noise, and

single-stream fallback show how the baselines behave when input quality or availability

changes. For SQ4, class-specific decisions at a fixed threshold complement the ranking scores.

Missing participant identifiers limit the uncertainty analysis and prevent participant-disjoint

fitting of the proposed learned fusion model.

       The HANDOVER dataset paper compared video, force/torque and gripper inputs for

completed-trial failure detection (Thoduka et al., 2024b). Its five-class I3D model achieved

69.1% accuracy with video and force/torque, falling to 67.9% when the gripper input was

added. The authors also investigated temporal action modelling with auxiliary action

supervision. The binary experiment here evaluates a different target and uses AP, which

precludes a numerical comparison with those five-class accuracies.




                                                178
Appendix H. HANDOVER Supplementary Case Study


H.2       Dataset and Common-Support Cohort
The source describes successful and failed robot handovers (Thoduka et al., 2024b). The

evaluation uses the pinned Zenodo v1 release (Thoduka et al., 2024a), with 589 native trials

involving Toyota HSR and Kinova Gen3 and 17 participants reported by its datasheet. Native

outcomes are success, no approach, outcome 2, and drop. Outcome 2 means no grasp for

robot-to-human handover or no release for human-to-robot handover. The binary target maps

success to zero and every nonzero native outcome to failure. Trial IDs restart across oﬀicial

splits, so identities are split-qualified; an ID is not a participant surrogate.

        The native oﬀicial split contains 337 training, 101 validation and 151 test trials. The

common-support cohort retains 587: 335 train, 101 validation and all 151 test trials, after two

training trials fail the timing gate. The test has 41 successes and 110 failures, giving positive

prevalence 0.728477. Released outcome counts govern the analysis where a small datasheet

discrepancy changes the distribution of outcome 2 versus drop; it does not change the binary

failure total. The oﬀicial participant-based split is the publisher’s claim, not an independently

verified identity separation.



H.3       Preparation and Input Construction
The initial full-video analysis excluded 92 test trials because their final frames extended

beyond wrench coverage. Before scoring the models, the observation window was defined as

the intersection of camera and wrench coverage for every trial, class and robot. The 16

source-clock bin-centre frames and numerical statistics use that same window. All oﬀicial test

trials could then be assessed. The completed-trial label can still depend on an event after the

end of the shared window.

        For the authors’ resampling procedure, matching the nearest raw wrench timestamp

reproduced the supplied arrays under a maximum permitted gap of 0.1 s. Video presentation

timestamps and sensor time refer to separate clocks. Six statistics for each of six force/torque

channels give 36 numerical features. The HSR measures wrench directly, whereas the Gen3

estimates it from joint torques. Fourteen traces met the timing criterion but were zero on

every channel. These trials were included with their original labels.

H.4       Models and Fitting Procedure
The pretrained visual condition (P) used the frozen ResNet18-vd CNN backbone inside the

RT-DETR-R18vd checkpoint. Spatial and temporal means produced one 896-dimensional

vector per trial. A newly fitted 896–128–1 head trained for ten fixed epochs. Averaging the

frames removed their order. The numerical branch fitted training-only scaling and balanced

L2 logistic regression to the 36 statistics. A fixed HGB control, a training-prior predictor, and

equal averaging of the visual and logistic predictions completed the five baselines. Each was

evaluated under three fixed seeds. Learned stacking and the quality gates required participant

identifiers for their training predictions.

        HANDOVER uses statistical wrench descriptors in place of the REASSEMBLE

raw-sequence temporal branch. Its own data determine the visual head and sensor scaler.

None of the fitted source-classifier components or F2 coeﬀicients were transferred. The

planned R condition required a REASSEMBLE-trained CNN checkpoint that met the

HANDOVER selection criteria. No eligible checkpoint was established for that condition.

separately for each seed. Table H.1 presents the three-seed mean and descriptive spread. The

deterministic numerical branches repeat identical predictions across seed labels; their zero

spread does not imply independent replication.

H.6       Participant Identity and the Learned-Fusion

          Restriction
F2, F2-aug, QG-clean and QG-aug required out-of-fold branch probabilities from

participant-disjoint splits. The mapping from trial to participant was unavailable. Trial IDs,

robot IDs and task labels do not identify individual participants. The oﬀicial validation set

retained its original role, and none of these four fusion methods was fitted.

       The missing participant mapping also prevents estimation of a participant-cluster

interval. A trial bootstrap would treat repeated observations from the same person as

independent. The variation across seeds describes optimization on a fixed set of test trials.

Participant independence is reported for the oﬀicial split by the dataset authors, but could not

be checked from trial-level identities.



H.7       Interpretation of the HANDOVER Results
On the 151 handovers, logistic sensing ranked failures better than the visual model or simple

averaging. The HGB sensor model achieved the highest clean AP among the five baselines.

These results concern predictions made from the common camera and wrench observation

window.

       Whether a learned fusion rule could improve on sensing remains open for this cohort.

The proposed comparison requires a participant mapping or equivalent grouping information

to obtain independent training predictions for the fusion model. Without that comparison the

study provides no estimate of an effect from learned reliability weighting or transfer of

source-trained CNN weights.
