+\section{What the camera contributed}
00_thesus_writing_tracking/scientific_cross_mapping_20261125/applied_revision_01/working/LaTeX_Project/chapters/03_foundations.tex:22: trailing whitespace.
+The REASSEMBLE visual model began with a detector trained to find objects in images. RT-DETR has a convolutional backbone that extracts image features at several scales. Its hybrid encoder combines information across those scales. A decoder and detection heads then use the features to predict object classes and bounding boxes \parencite{Zhao2024RTDETR}. For the insertion study, the available label described whether the action succeeded. There was no target box marking the place where it failed.
00_thesus_writing_tracking/scientific_cross_mapping_20261125/applied_revision_01/working/LaTeX_Project/chapters/03_foundations.tex:24: trailing whitespace.
+The main experiment used the ResNet18-vd backbone from the \texttt{PekingU/rtdetr\_r18vd} checkpoint. Its weights stayed fixed. Each hand-camera frame was resized to \(640 \times 640\) pixels, and its pixel values were divided by 255. The recorded procedure used no further scaling with the ImageNet mean and standard deviation.
00_thesus_writing_tracking/scientific_cross_mapping_20261125/applied_revision_01/working/LaTeX_Project/chapters/03_foundations.tex:26: trailing whitespace.
+For each frame, the backbone returned three feature maps. A feature map records how strongly an image feature responds at different positions. The model took the average response across each map channel and joined the results. This gave 896 numbers per frame. Averaging those numbers across the 16 selected frames produced 896 numbers for the completed action. A separately trained classification head used them to estimate the probability of failure. The detector’s hybrid encoder, decoder, and bounding-box heads were bypassed in this experiment.
00_thesus_writing_tracking/scientific_cross_mapping_20261125/applied_revision_01/working/LaTeX_Project/chapters/03_foundations.tex:28: trailing whitespace.
+Two details of the insertion disappear during those averages. The first is the exact position of a feature in the image. The second is when that feature appeared. For example, one feature might respond strongly in a frame near contact and weakly in the other frames. Its average could be the same as a moderate response spread across several frames. The resulting 896 values would not tell the prediction head which pattern had occurred.
00_thesus_writing_tracking/scientific_cross_mapping_20261125/applied_revision_01/working/LaTeX_Project/chapters/03_foundations.tex:30: trailing whitespace.
+This limitation led to a specific comparison in Chapter~6. The main model averaged frames across the whole action. A later experiment pooled features from the last four of the sixteen sampled frames, giving the final part of the movement its own representation. Both models answered a question about a completed action. Testing an early warning would require an earlier decision time and would exclude every frame recorded after it.
00_thesus_writing_tracking/scientific_cross_mapping_20261125/applied_revision_01/working/LaTeX_Project/chapters/03_foundations.tex:32: trailing whitespace.

Pretrained convolutional features have been useful in industrial inspection \parencite{Roth2022Towards}, and the Vision Transformer study showed that the amount of pretraining data affects the comparison between convolution and attention \parencite{P010}. Video Swin keeps local information across both space and time, with a computing cost that depends on the clips and image crops used \parencite{P013}. TimeSformer reported an advantage for separate spatial and temporal attention on Kinetics-400 and Something-Something V2 \parencite[Table~1]{P034}. The question for REASSEMBLE was narrower: with the available recordings and training time, did retaining a selected part of the action help predict its outcome?

+\section{What the machine sensors contributed}
00_thesus_writing_tracking/scientific_cross_mapping_20261125/applied_revision_01/working/LaTeX_Project/chapters/03_foundations.tex:42: trailing whitespace.
+During a REASSEMBLE action, the robot recorded 22 channels: three force channels, three torque channels, two gripper channels, seven joint-effort channels, and seven joint-velocity channels. A brief change in force and a gradual rise in force can have the same average. The sensor study therefore compared two ways of reading the same measurements. One described the action with a small set of statistics. The other learned from short, overlapping parts of the signal.
00_thesus_writing_tracking/scientific_cross_mapping_20261125/applied_revision_01/working/LaTeX_Project/chapters/03_foundations.tex:44: trailing whitespace.
+For the statistical model, each channel supplied ten values: the mean, standard deviation, median, minimum, maximum, lower and upper quartiles, range, root-mean-square amplitude, and slope over action progress. Together, the 22 channels produced \textbf{220 features per action}. For a force channel, the maximum retained the highest value in the resampled force channel. The range described the distance between its lowest and highest values. The slope described whether force generally rose or fell as the action progressed. None of these values, on its own, identified the moment when the highest force occurred.
00_thesus_writing_tracking/scientific_cross_mapping_20261125/applied_revision_01/working/LaTeX_Project/chapters/03_foundations.tex:46: trailing whitespace.
+These simple features were a serious comparison for the learned model. They captured useful properties of the robot’s movement, and their compact size made them a practical reference with the available training data. Other industrial work has likewise shown that carefully chosen features can compete with learned representations, including in 3D anomaly detection \parencite{Horwitz2023Back}.

Two neighbouring positions did not necessarily represent the same number of milliseconds in different actions. +\section{What the experiments can establish}
00_thesus_writing_tracking/scientific_cross_mapping_20261125/applied_revision_01/working/LaTeX_Project/chapters/03_foundations.tex:274: trailing whitespace.
+The main REASSEMBLE comparison used a frozen camera backbone, a statistical sensor model, and fusion rules that combined their predictions. The statistical sensor branch was selected for fusion after the initial representation results were available. This was an exploratory choice. PatchTST remained a separate sensor-only comparison in the core study, and its performance under a limited training budget leaves open what longer training might achieve. Appendix~\ref{app:implementation} records the configurations, and Appendix~\ref{app:reproducibility} identifies which prediction paths were actually run.
00_thesus_writing_tracking/scientific_cross_mapping_20261125/applied_revision_01/working/LaTeX_Project/chapters/03_foundations.tex:276: trailing whitespace.
+The original research question also included sound. An Audio Spectrogram Transformer was a possible way to represent it \parencite{P011}. In REASSEMBLE, the checks could not establish a reliable common time reference between the audio and the labelled action. There were also concerns that information about the outcome could enter the audio prediction process. The study therefore left audio out of the predictive experiment. Its value for these actions remains open.
00_thesus_writing_tracking/scientific_cross_mapping_20261125/applied_revision_01/working/LaTeX_Project/chapters/03_foundations.tex:278: trailing whitespace.
+The recorded insertion illustrates the boundary of the chapter’s claims. The model had access to the whole action, so it could use visual evidence near the end. The sensor model had access to the same action through force and movement measurements, but the summaries it received had already lost much of the order of those measurements. The fusion experiment could test whether combining those two predictions helped rank failed actions on held-out recordings. It could not determine the physical cause of that insertion failure or the earliest time at which the robot could have detected it.
00_thesus_writing_tracking/scientific_cross_mapping_20261125/applied_revision_01/working/LaTeX_Project/chapters/03_foundations.tex:280: trailing whitespace.
+The later experiments changed individual parts of that setup. Pooling the last four sampled frames changed which frames contributed to the camera prediction. Longer temporal training changed the opportunity given to the sensor model. Anchored adjustment changed the fusion rule. The thesis did not run all three changes together. Each result therefore belongs to the configuration that produced it. Chapter~\ref{ch:methodology} describes the recording splits, training decisions, and assessment conditions needed to read those results correctly.
00_thesus_writing_tracking/scientific_cross_mapping_20261125/applied_revision_01/working/LaTeX_Project/chapters/03_foundations.tex:282: trailing whitespace.
+\textit{Implementation records: \EvidenceRef{E112}{}, \EvidenceRef{E114}{}, \EvidenceRef{E110}{}.}
00_thesus_writing_tracking/scientific_cross_mapping_20261125/applied_revision_01/working/LaTeX_Project/chapters/04_methodology.tex:4: trailing whitespace.
+Of the 4,530 completed robot actions in REASSEMBLE, 509 ended in failure. A model that predicted success every time would therefore be correct on almost 89\% of the actions. It would also miss every failure. For this study, a more useful question was whether the model gave higher failure scores to the actions that actually failed. To test what the camera added, the camera–sensor model and the sensor-only model had to predict the outcomes of the same actions, with the same recordings held out for assessment.
00_thesus_writing_tracking/scientific_cross_mapping_20261125/applied_revision_01/working/LaTeX_Project/chapters/04_methodology.tex:6: trailing whitespace.
+The PHM gear study needed a different test. There, the model predicted a number derived from a photograph taken after a gear run. Its error could be measured in percentage points against that photographic target. The same error could not tell us how closely the prediction matched physical tooth damage, because the study had no independent measurement of that damage.
00_thesus_writing_tracking/scientific_cross_mapping_20261125/applied_revision_01/working/LaTeX_Project/chapters/04_methodology.tex:8: trailing whitespace.
+These two cases explain the main design choice in this thesis. Each comparison starts with the physical event, the target attached to it, and the information available when the model makes its prediction. The choice of data split and performance measure follows from those decisions.

+\section{Fitting models, adjusting probabilities, and choosing thresholds}
00_thesus_writing_tracking/scientific_cross_mapping_20261125/applied_revision_01/working/LaTeX_Project/chapters/04_methodology.tex:119: trailing whitespace.
+Fitting a model, choosing a model, adjusting its probabilities, and choosing a decision threshold were separate steps. In REASSEMBLE, for example, training the sensor predictor determined how its features contributed to its score. Selecting its regularisation setting determined which fitted version went forward. A calibrator then used training-side predictions and outcomes to adjust the probabilities. Finally, a threshold converted each probability into a success or failure decision.
00_thesus_writing_tracking/scientific_cross_mapping_20261125/applied_revision_01/working/LaTeX_Project/chapters/04_methodology.tex:121: trailing whitespace.
+The main REASSEMBLE branch calibrators used sigmoid functions fitted to selected inner out-of-fold scores. Calibration can improve the meaning of a probability estimate, but the improvement needs to be checked on assessment data \parencite{Guo2017Calibration}. The F2 stacker then fitted a logistic combination of the two branch probabilities with its own intercept. There was no further calibration step after F2. The original context gates combined branch log-odds using weights that added up to one. They had neither a free final intercept nor a separate calibrator after the gate.
00_thesus_writing_tracking/scientific_cross_mapping_20261125/applied_revision_01/working/LaTeX_Project/chapters/04_methodology.tex:123: trailing whitespace.
+This difference matters when interpreting the gain from fusion. Suppose a sensor-only adjustment raises some probabilities while keeping all actions within one fold in the same order. The ranking within that fold stays the same. A second fold, however, has its own fitted adjustment. When the two lists are joined, a failure in the second fold can move above a success in the first. The \textbf{pooled} ranking can therefore change even though the order within each fold stays fixed.

+In the main REASSEMBLE comparison, F2 improved pooled failure ranking over the original statistical sensor predictor. F2 received both a camera score and an extra fitted step for combining scores. A sensor-only model given the same opportunity for second-stage fitting would help separate those effects. The original comparison had no independently refitted control of that kind. Chapter~6 examines the saved predictions to see what the fitted terms contributed. The complete gain cannot be assigned to the camera alone.

+For binary decisions, the recorded procedure examined thresholds from 0.01 to 0.99 using permitted training-side predictions and a stated objective, such as balanced accuracy. A score at or above the selected threshold counted as positive. This produced a reproducible decision for the experiment. A factory alarm would need additional information about the cost of a missed failure, a false alarm, or an unnecessary stop.

+\section{Measuring performance and uncertainty}
00_thesus_writing_tracking/scientific_cross_mapping_20261125/applied_revision_01/working/LaTeX_Project/chapters/04_methodology.tex:132: trailing whitespace.
+\subsection{What the reported scores mean}
00_thesus_writing_tracking/scientific_cross_mapping_20261125/applied_revision_01/working/LaTeX_Project/chapters/04_methodology.tex:134: trailing whitespace.
+In PHM, \textbf{mean absolute error} gives the average size of the difference between a predicted value and the photographic target. \textbf{Root mean squared error} gives greater weight to larger errors. Both retain the target’s units. The coefficient of determination, \(R^2\), compares prediction error with the variation in the observed targets. It can become negative, and it can change sharply when a held-out group contains little target variation. Tooth-level and run-level errors were therefore reported at their own levels.
00_thesus_writing_tracking/scientific_cross_mapping_20261125/applied_revision_01/working/LaTeX_Project/chapters/04_methodology.tex:136: trailing whitespace.
+For REASSEMBLE failure classification, \emph{precision} asks how many predicted failures were actual failures. \emph{Recall} asks how many of the actual failures were found. Lowering the decision threshold may find more failures while also raising more false alarms. Specificity measures how often successful cases were correctly recognised. Balanced accuracy gives equal weight to recall for the two classes, while the F1 score combines precision and recall for the designated positive class.
00_thesus_writing_tracking/scientific_cross_mapping_20261125/applied_revision_01/working/LaTeX_Project/chapters/04_methodology.tex:138: trailing whitespace.
+The main ranking measure was \textbf{average precision (AP)}. It gives a model credit for placing positive cases high on its list across different thresholds. The study used \texttt{average\_precision\_score}, which weights each precision value by the corresponding increase in recall. Calculating the area under a precision–recall curve by the trapezoidal rule is a different measure. AUROC also measures ranking, but a precision–recall analysis is particularly useful when the event of interest is uncommon \parencite{Saito2015PrecisionRecall}.
00_thesus_writing_tracking/scientific_cross_mapping_20261125/applied_revision_01/working/LaTeX_Project/chapters/04_methodology.tex:140: trailing whitespace.
+The failure rates in REASSEMBLE and ViFailback show why the positive class must be named. In REASSEMBLE, roughly 11\% of the actions failed. A model that gave every action the same score would have failure AP of about 0.11. In ViFailback, more than 85\% of the eligible episodes failed. The same constant-score rule would already have failure AP above 0.85, despite being unable to distinguish any two episodes. For ViFailback, the study therefore reported AP for failure, AP for success, and the mean of these two values. When both classes are present, that mean is 0.5 for a constant-score model. MSM used abnormal cutting as its positive class. These scores answer different questions across the three datasets.
00_thesus_writing_tracking/scientific_cross_mapping_20261125/applied_revision_01/working/LaTeX_Project/chapters/04_methodology.tex:142: trailing whitespace.
+Ranking also leaves open whether the probabilities are useful. The \textbf{Brier score} measures the average squared difference between the predicted probability and the recorded binary outcome \parencite{P055}. \textbf{Expected calibration error} groups predictions into probability ranges and compares the scores with the observed outcome frequencies in those ranges. Its value depends partly on how the ranges are chosen and how many cases fall in each. A model can rank failures well and still assign probabilities that are too high. Its probability estimates can also become less reliable when operating conditions change \parencite{P008}.
00_thesus_writing_tracking/scientific_cross_mapping_20261125/applied_revision_01/working/LaTeX_Project/chapters/04_methodology.tex:144: trailing whitespace.
+\subsection{Combining results and estimating uncertainty}
00_thesus_writing_tracking/scientific_cross_mapping_20261125/applied_revision_01/working/LaTeX_Project/chapters/04_methodology.tex:146: trailing whitespace.
+For its principal comparison, REASSEMBLE brought together the predictions for all held-out recordings and calculated failure AP once. Calculating AP separately in each fold and averaging the five results would answer a different question, because cases from different folds would never be ranked against one another. MSM calculated pooled abnormal-cutting AP for each training seed and then averaged across seeds. Changing a seed measures variation from fitting a model again. It does not create another independently recorded milling interval.
00_thesus_writing_tracking/scientific_cross_mapping_20261125/applied_revision_01/working/LaTeX_Project/chapters/04_methodology.tex:148: trailing whitespace.
+To compare two REASSEMBLE models, the analysis kept their predictions paired. If a recording was selected for a calculation, both models contributed predictions for the same actions in that recording. The core comparison repeated this \textbf{2,000 times}, sampling whole recordings with replacement and recalculating the difference in performance. This is a recording-level \emph{bootstrap}.
00_thesus_writing_tracking/scientific_cross_mapping_20261125/applied_revision_01/working/LaTeX_Project/chapters/04_methodology.tex:150: trailing whitespace.
+A PHM report used 10,000 bootstrap samples drawn at the level of the gear run. The data still came from only \textbf{20 runs across three gear experiments}. Runs from the same experiment could share conditions. More bootstrap draws make the numerical estimate more stable, but they cannot supply more independent gear experiments. The MSM date-based intervals had a similar limit: only five acquisition dates were available.
00_thesus_writing_tracking/scientific_cross_mapping_20261125/applied_revision_01/working/LaTeX_Project/chapters/04_methodology.tex:152: trailing whitespace.
+The intervals describe variation in the saved predictions under the chosen way of resampling. They do not cover every change that might occur after retraining the models or moving to a new installation. The intervals also leave out uncertainty from model selection and repeated use of the assessment data.
