## Overall Verdict

This empirical sequence has unusually strong methodological discipline for a master's thesis—its group-disjoint protocols, refusal to cherry-pick favorable splits, and honest reporting of null, negative, and unfinished work are major scientific assets. Structurally, however, Chapters 5–8 suffer from three problems that weaken the thesis's argument:

1. **A miscalibrated hierarchy of the external studies** that undersells MSM (the most complete external test) while over-framing ViFailback (which is incomplete on SQ3 and partial on adaptation).
2. **Defensive audit-log prose** in which repeated boilerplate disclaimers crowd out basic quantitative evidence—leaving several explicitly introduced baselines, controls, and sensitivities without reported numbers.
3. **Uneven presentation and duplication with Chapter 4**, especially in Chapters 5, 7, and 8.

The core empirical order (PHM → REASSEMBLE → MSM → ViFailback) is sound and does not need a cosmetic reshuffle, but targeted structural tightening, clearer evidence tables, and sharper separation from Chapters 4 and 9 are essential before submission.

*Review scope note:* This assessment is based on Chapters 4–8 and the two traceability documents (`THESIS_CODE_MAP.md` and PHM `THESIS_CODE_TRACEABILITY.md`). Chapters 1–3, Chapter 9, Appendices D/F/G/H, raw result tables, and rendered figures were not supplied; claims depending on those materials are flagged explicitly for verification rather than assumed verified.

## 1. Assessment of Each Dataset's Scientific Role

The proposed hierarchy needs one substantive correction: **MSM and ViFailback are currently framed backwards relative to the evidence they actually deliver.**

- **REASSEMBLE (Chapter 6) — Correctly identified as the central empirical study.** It is the only dataset that evaluates all four sub-questions (SQ1–SQ4) in full depth: unimodal representation hierarchy, six fusion families, systematic corruption and missingness stress tests, and targeted mechanistic follow-ups (training budget, temporal pooling, spatial encoder, fine-tuning, anchored adaptation, and exposure-matched corruption training) on a common 4,530-action cohort. Its evidence justifies anchoring the thesis here.
- **MSM (Chapter 7) — Currently undersold; its actual scientific role is the primary completed cross-domain (manufacturing) generalization and robustness test.** Calling MSM merely "a completed manufacturing comparison" and titling Chapter 7 *Dataset Adaptation and Evaluation Feasibility* makes it sound like a preliminary screening exercise (akin to REBOOT or Intel Welding). In fact, MSM is the *only* study outside REASSEMBLE that completed multi-seed clean evaluation, six target- and group-sensitivity protocols, **and** controlled SQ3 corruption/quality-gate experiments. Moreover, it returns directly to industrial manufacturing (metal cutting) and supplies the thesis's clearest counterexample to assuming multimodal stacking helps when a strong numerical baseline already exists.
- **ViFailback (Chapter 8) — Currently overstated as "the major extension"; its actual scientific role is a robotic task-generalization and representation-transfer extension (SQ1–SQ2, with partial adaptation and unexecuted SQ3).** ViFailback adds genuine scientific value that MSM cannot: multi-view robotics (three cameras), extreme majority-failure prevalence requiring both-class AP, task-disjoint generalization across 99 canonical tasks, and the thesis's *only* completed learned-representation transfer comparison (`DONOR20` vs. `FROZEN20`). However, its primary comparison was specified partly retrospectively (30 October 2026), target-CNN adaptation (`FULL20`/`FULL100`) covers only one outer fold and single-role pilots with inconclusive intervals, and SQ3 corruption testing was never run. Framing it as a **focused transfer and task-generalization study with bounded adaptation evidence** matches the completed record far better than "the major extension."
- **PHM2026 (Chapter 5) — Substantive investigation of target validity, proxy construction, and multi-source sensor fusion (R1/SQ4, SQ1, partial SQ2).** The proposed role for PHM is accurate, with one necessary sharpening: examiners will naturally ask why a study with *no inference-time image branch* sits in a multimodal vision–sensor thesis. Frame PHM explicitly as establishing **R1 (target validity and modality correspondence) and multi-source sensor-feature fusion under cross-experiment shift**, rather than letting it look like a failed visual–sensor fusion chapter. Its placement as Chapter 5 works well because its target-validity and constant-baseline lessons motivate why REASSEMBLE's native outcomes and paired inference modalities were needed next.

## 2. Chapter-by-Chapter Assessment of the Current Flow

### Front-loading major findings across Chapters 5–8

Introducing a brief orientation takeaway at the start of each chapter **helps an examiner** in a multi-dataset thesis where targets, units, and metrics shift every chapter. However, the current implementation is **inconsistent and disrupts motivation in Chapters 7–8**:
- Chapters 5 and 6 place the headline result in two unheaded opening paragraphs before §x.1.
- Chapters 7 and 8 put the headline result as sentence 1 *inside* §7.1 (*Motivation and Intended Comparison*) and §8.1 (*Motivation and Replication Objectives*), which states the empirical verdict before explaining why the study was conducted.
- All four drop raw decimal metrics (e.g., `0.8238`, `0.4473`) before defining the task unit or positive class.

Standardize on a **3–4 sentence unheaded chapter overview** before §x.1 stating the study's role, directional finding (qualitatively or with a single self-explanatory anchor), and scope boundary—then let §x.1 open cleanly with motivation and questions.

### Chapter 5 — PHM2026
- **What works:** The conceptual separation between deterministic target construction, target emulation, and physical damage validation (§5.6) is unusually sharp. Keeping v1/v2/median-28/v3 separate in Table `tab:phm-target-lineage` and explaining the three-experiment LOEO rotation clearly prevents common reviewer misunderstandings.
- **What fails:**
  - **Misleading opening figure:** `fig:phm-route` sits before §5.1 and depicts the secondary, latency-confounded RT-DETR-L scalar image-regression pipeline rather than the chapter's principal sensor pipeline or target-construction workflow.
  - **Missing quantitative results for introduced experiments:** §5.7.3 defines the historical fixed split and §5.6.4 states its v3 constant MAE (`0.7348`), yet the fixed-split fusion result (`0.6548` vs. `0.7348`, CI `[-0.548, +0.414]`, below the `0.15` pp criterion) is never reported. Similarly, §5.7.2 introduces Ridge regression (with its alpha grid), §5.6 discusses scalar regression, detection, multitask, and blind/DINOv2 diagnostics, and §5.8 discusses the four-arm study—yet almost none of their actual numbers appear in the chapter.
  - **Repetition of caveats:** The four-arm normalizer-leakage caveat is repeated four times across §4.6.1, §5.7.1, §5.7.3, §5.8, and §5.9.
  - **Unflagged evidence discrepancy:** §5.7.1 cites `E027` for "Minute-feature configuration," whereas the traceability records flag that `E027` is a misnamed directory whose provenance actually executed `train_late_fusion.py`.

### Chapter 6 — REASSEMBLE
- **What works:** Best-structured chapter in the thesis. The progression from Cohort/Preparation → Exp A (Unimodal) → Exp B (Fusion) → Exp C (Robustness) → Follow-ups → Synthesis directly mirrors SQ1–SQ4, and §6.10 synthesizes what was and was not combined without overclaiming.
- **What fails:**
  - **Experiment C (§6.8) lacks a results table and drops defined operators:** §6.8.2 defines seven corruption operators (pixel noise, blur, frame removal; sensor noise, channel removal, temporal block removal, stuck force/torque), but §6.8.4 reports numerical outcomes only for pixel noise, sensor noise, and blur in prose.
  - **Section 6.9 (Follow-ups) is an unnavigable wall of text:** Six dense subsections mix full-cohort (4,530 actions), Fold-0 subset (753 actions), 16-frame vs. 32-frame, and 2/20/100-epoch protocols with zero summary tables. An examiner cannot easily track which follow-up used which population or control.
  - **Dangling method in §6.5:** Nineteen structured label-shift permutations are specified in §6.5 ("smallest plus-one probability of 0.05"), yet no permutation result appears anywhere in Chapter 6.
  - **Redundancy with Chapter 4:** The 753-action / 26-recording reconciliation (§6.4.1), stacker OOF bank dependency (§6.4.5), and AP/pooled-AP definitions (§6.5) duplicate §§4.6.2–4.8.2.

### Chapter 7 — MSM
- **What works:** Preparation and alignment (§7.4) are crisp and scientifically compelling—the VMX9 `+49.73 s` vs. `+16.04 s` alignment check, 3:8 SAR letterboxing, and label-2 semantic boundary genuinely explain *why* data engineering matters for multimodal validity. Table `tab:msm-support` cleanly documents the six sensitivity protocols.
- **What fails:**
  - **Title misrepresents the chapter:** *Dataset Adaptation and Evaluation Feasibility* implies an inconclusive feasibility check rather than a completed SQ1–SQ4 evaluation.
  - **Collapsed results section (§7.8):** Clean primary scores, six-protocol sensitivities, five-date bootstrap intervals, corruption stresses, fallback behavior, seed-42 Brier/F1 trade-offs, and visual generalization limits are all compressed into one unstructured section with a single clean-AP table (`tab:msm-clean`).
  - **Unsubstantiated claims in prose:** §7.8 asserts logistic sensing beat F2 across all six protocols (giving numbers for only two), mentions a "substantial visual training–assessment gap" without reporting the training score, and lists seven corruption families while giving numbers for only two.
  - **No closing transition:** Chapter 7 ends abruptly on visual-annotation speculation, without explaining how MSM's outcome motivates Chapter 8 (ViFailback).
  - **Misplaced literature comparison:** The `\citet{P045}` tool-wear comparison in §7.1 interrupts the chapter's motivation; it belongs in Related Work or Chapter 9.

### Chapter 8 — ViFailback
- **What works:** Candid disclosure of the 30 October 2026 retrospective specification, the 24 November 2026 stopping cutoff, the distinction between completed (`FROZEN20`/`DONOR20`), partial (`FULL20`/`FULL100`), and unexecuted (`SQ3`) scope (`tab:vif-status`), and why high failure prevalence (`85.65%`) mandates both-class macro AP (`tab:vif-bothclass`).
- **What fails:**
  - **Completed controls without reported numbers:** §8.7.1 and §8.7.5 introduce the completed 36-fit high-camera control, the original clean quality gate, and its availability-only control—but report zero scores for them.
  - **Split results layout (§8.7 vs. §8.8):** Most results appear inside §8.7.1–§8.7.3, yet §8.8 unexpectedly drops extra fold-0 metrics (Brier `0.1539`, balanced accuracy `0.5` at threshold `0.5`) and an unelaborated finding about **negative sigmoid calibrator slopes reversing episode rankings**.
  - **Zero figures:** Despite having the most complex multi-camera input (3 views × 16 timestamps) and multi-protocol training matrix (20-epoch nested OOF vs. 100-epoch single-role pilot), ViFailback has no embedded figure.
  - **Cross-study leakage in §8.9:** Summarizing MSM, ViFailback, and HANDOVER together duplicates Chapter 9's synthesis.

## 4. Recommended Common Chapter Structure (With Justified Exceptions)

Common macro-template for Chapters 5–8:
1. **Unheaded Chapter Overview** (3–4 sentences: scientific role, qualitative main takeaway, scope boundary).
2. **Study Motivation and Research-Question Mapping** (why this dataset, which SQs/Rs it answers, what it does *not* test).
3. **Dataset, Prediction Task, and Challenges** (cohort, native vs. proxy target, observation horizon, physical/alignment constraints).
4. **Preparation, Cohort Construction, and Pre-Checks** (exclusions, temporal/modality alignment, input contracts, group definitions).
5. **Models, Adaptations, and Evaluation Protocol** (inputs, exact executed paths, baselines/controls, splits, metrics, uncertainty—cross-referencing Ch. 4 for shared mechanics).
6. **Empirical Investigations** (one subsection per investigation, following the internal micro-arc: **Input → Processing/Model → Output → Evaluation → Supported Finding → Limitation**).
7. **Synthesis, Limitations, and Transition to the Next Study** (what is established vs. unresolved, and the conceptual—not invented historical—link forward).

**Justified dataset-specific exceptions:**
- **Chapter 5 (PHM):** Keep *Target-Version History and Image-Derived Proxy Construction* as Investigation A before Sensor Prediction (Investigation B) and Diagnostics (Investigation C), rather than burying target construction inside Preparation.
- **Chapter 6 (REASSEMBLE):** Keep **Experiments A, B, and C as top-level sections**, followed by a dedicated **Follow-up Investigations** section with a master summary table.
- **Chapter 7 (MSM):** Keep **Implementation Checks / Bounded Development Run** as a brief subsection inside *Models and Evaluation Protocol* rather than a standalone top-level section.
- **Chapter 8 (ViFailback):** Keep the **Scope & Completion Status table (`tab:vif-status`)** near the top of the Empirical Investigations section so completed, partial, and unexecuted arms are clear upfront.
