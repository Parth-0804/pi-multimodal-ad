# What the comparisons establish together

The evidence supports a conditional empirical contribution: **representation quality, complementarity and the ability of a fitted fusion rule to use that complementarity are separate properties.** Better standalone prediction need not improve fusion; a weaker standalone representation can still add useful information; a clean adaptive improvement need not survive corrupted inputs. This interpretation emerges from the comparisons below. It is not a claim that their strongest components formed one evaluated architecture.

Unless marked newly recomputed, numbers and intervals below are **REPORTED results from inspected artifacts**. Direct inspection verifies what those artifacts contain; their training and bootstrap procedures were not rerun. AP means non-interpolated average precision, with failure positive in REASSEMBLE. The common core cohort contains 4,530 actions, 509 failures and 148 recordings. Follow-up models, branch recipes and aggregation differ even when the assessment cohort is shared.

## C1. PatchTST budget: insufficient optimization versus representation suitability

The matched short-budget control has AP 0.2557; the long-budget model has 0.2853, difference +0.0297 [0.0122, 0.0451]. This is not the original 0.2416 short-budget result. The long condition permits 100 epochs, a minimum of 10 and patience 12; no inner fit reaches the cap, and outer budgets use rounded median inner best epochs. Three seeds are combined by averaging probabilities before calculating pooled AP, not by averaging their AP scores. The original five outer/four inner recording partitions are retained.

**Demonstrates:** the original short optimization budget was consequential for this implementation. Improvement has the same AP direction in all five folds. **Rules out:** the claim that additional training made no difference. Its remaining deficit to statistics, −0.1441 [−0.1861, −0.1009], rules out the tested duration change as a sufficient explanation for the full standalone deficit. **Plausible:** inductive bias, progress resampling, limited labels, aggregation, normalization, architecture size or other optimization choices. **Cannot establish:** that all temporal encoders lack useful signal, that 100 epochs actually ran in every fit, or that forecasting-pretrained PatchTST would behave the same way.

Source: [executed extension handoff](../../implementations/REASSEMBLE/results/artifacts/reports/ADDITIONAL_IMPROVEMENTS_EXECUTED_HANDOFF.md); [budget comparison](../../implementations/REASSEMBLE/results/runs/followup_exploratory_studies/representation_and_robustness_studies/02_patchtst_training_budget/comparison/results.json).

## C2. Statistics plus PatchTST: useful information without standalone superiority

The fixed-C=1 probability stacker of long-budget PatchTST and statistics reaches AP 0.44035, versus 0.42939 for statistics: +0.01095 [0.0054, 0.0181], with positive fold AP differences in the reported five-fold analysis. This combines two representations of the same numerical sensor modality.

**Demonstrates:** the evaluated hybrid uses differences between these representation pipelines sufficiently to improve held-out ranking under its protocol. **Rules out:** “the lower standalone AP makes the temporal branch redundant for every downstream combination.” The within-fold directions also make a solely cross-fold alignment account inadequate, although this task did not recompute those hybrid ranks. **Plausible:** useful temporal summaries, different calibration/error structure, or interactions with the added fitting stage. **Cannot establish:** which temporal pattern is responsible, conditional mutual information, physical causation, general neural superiority, or a camera–sensor gain. A matched independently fitted statistics-only meta-model was not newly evaluated here.

Source: [hybrid comparison](../../implementations/REASSEMBLE/results/runs/followup_exploratory_studies/representation_and_robustness_studies/02_patchtst_training_budget/hybrid_comparison/results.json) and the C1 handoff.

## C3. Boundary pooling: where information is retained

Last-quarter pooling reaches visual AP 0.31915 against whole-action mean 0.23066: +0.0885 [0.0424, 0.1293]. The simultaneous lower bound over five AP contrasts is +0.0301. Both use the same frozen frame features and 114,945 fitted head parameters. The other reported visual APs are two halves 0.2556, thirds 0.3080, early-plus-late 0.2922 and ordered GRU 0.2250. Historical whole-mean parity is a **reported earlier check**, not newly replayed here.

**Demonstrates:** aggregation can materially change the usefulness of a fixed representation. **Rules out:** increased fitted parameter count as the explanation for the last-quarter versus mean contrast. **Plausible:** outcome-state visibility near completion, reduced dilution by earlier frames, task-specific content or nuisance differences. **Cannot establish:** causal localization of failure, the value of temporal order itself, or early-warning capability. Last-quarter averaging does not learn order within that quarter.

The selected pooling rule followed by F2 reaches 0.49344, +0.0461 [0.0260, 0.0632] over the original F2 on the same cohort. This connects aggregation to fusion usefulness, but its interval is conditional on selected representations and exposed recordings. It is not independent confirmation of the selection, and it does not test the combination with the same-sensor hybrid or anchored adaptation.

Source: [boundary-aware pooling handoff](../../implementations/REASSEMBLE/results/artifacts/reports/BOUNDARY_AWARE_VISUAL_POOLING_HANDOFF.md).

## C4. Frozen, spatially encoded and adapted visual representations

The matched five-epoch CNN-versus-hybrid-encoder comparison uses identical 16-frame inputs and fixed extractors. CNN visual/F2 APs are 0.221644/0.440764; hybrid-encoder APs are 0.171915/0.424979. The F2 difference is −0.015785 [−0.024804, −0.005800]. Feature widths are 896/768 and fitted heads differ in size. **Demonstrates:** executing the tested additional spatial components did not improve this recipe. **Rules out:** the assumption that more of a detection checkpoint necessarily helps this target. **Does not isolate:** attention, capacity, optimization or temporal modeling.

The matched 32-frame, two-epoch frozen/final-stage-tuned comparison covers two seeds and all 4,530 actions. Visual effects have opposite signs; F2 effects are −0.00080 and −0.00282, both with intervals spanning zero. Equal epochs do not mean equal compute. A separate 100-epoch frozen/full pilot reports 100 executed epochs in both arms but selects epochs 14/47. Its F2 AP difference is +0.0141 [−0.0008, 0.0291] on 753 actions; its secondary AUROC difference is +0.011623 [0.001070, 0.022797]. The latter is a positive exploratory conditional interval, not a resolved primary AP gain or independent confirmation. The restricted historical F2 is 0.3672, not its 4,530-action AP of 0.4473. Neither comparison establishes equivalence or a general failure of adaptation.

The 20-epoch-cap study completes 25 visual fits for one seed; its visual pooled AP 0.3121 differs from mean-fold AP 0.3086. Its missing F2 bank and deferred additional seeds are missing experiments, not negative fusion results. The source-epoch eligibility conflict also cannot be turned into a performance result.

**Plausible remaining explanations:** limited adaptation budget, seed sensitivity, changes in calibration or branch-error structure, and OOF-to-assessment differences. The matched diagnosis explicitly leaves the cause unresolved. A favorable standalone visual change would still need a paired fusion comparison.

Sources: [spatial-encoder result](../../implementations/REASSEMBLE/experiments/additional/rt_detr_encoder_comparison/results/run_01/comparison/RESULTS_SUMMARY.md), [matched fine-tuning diagnosis](../../implementations/REASSEMBLE/experiments/additional/frozen32_vs_finetuned32_fusion_diagnosis/results/comparison/RESULTS_SUMMARY.md), [100-epoch model comparison](../../implementations/REASSEMBLE/experiments/core/cnn100_static_sensor_f2_comparison/10_final_research_report/MODEL_COMPARISON_RESULTS.md), [paired comparisons](../../implementations/REASSEMBLE/experiments/core/cnn100_static_sensor_f2_comparison/10_final_research_report/PAIRED_STATISTICAL_COMPARISON.md), and [earlier checkpoint verification](../../implementations/REASSEMBLE/experiments/core/cnn100_static_sensor_f2_comparison/02_checkpoint_verification/VERIFICATION_REPORT.md). The 20-epoch status remains attributed to existing manuscript Chapter 6 §6.9.4 / E059; that historical handoff path was not located in the current source tree. It is not treated as a newly verified execution result or used to make a new performance claim in the proposed passages.

## C5. Static stacking: visual ordering and score alignment are both relevant

**Newly VERIFIED:** F2 and its sensor comparator use matching targets, fold identities and branch inputs. All five saved sensor coefficients are positive. Removing the visual term while retaining the jointly fitted intercept and sensor coefficient preserves each fold's sensor ranking exactly. Restoring vision improves AUROC in five folds and AP in four. The fifth AP difference is −0.00469. See [the full arithmetic audit](F2_VERIFICATION.md).

**Demonstrates:** the saved visual term contributes beneficial ordering changes on held-out actions beyond a strictly increasing transformation of the same sensor probability. **Rules out:** explaining every observed benefit solely by strictly increasing recalibration of that sensor score or pooled score alignment. **Plausible additional contribution:** fold-specific score transformations and jointly estimated coefficients. **Cannot establish:** a unique fraction of pooled improvement attributable to vision, causal modality importance, population-wide improvement or F2 superiority over F1. The reported F2−F1 AP interval [−0.007607, 0.011165] remains inconclusive.

The existing caveat should therefore be narrowed, not deleted: a matched sensor-only second-stage model would strengthen attribution of the full pipeline contrast, but its absence does not erase the observed within-fold visual ordering contribution.

## C6. Anchored adaptation: function class matters

The clean adaptive model freezes the fitted F2 anchor and applies multipliers `1+tanh(h_m(u))` to its branch-probability coefficients. It is a 42→16→2 context network, initialized at the anchor, not a softmax convex logit gate. AP is approximately 0.4683 versus anchor 0.4473 and constant-adjustment control 0.4457. Differences are +0.0210 [0.0015, 0.0419] and +0.0226 [0.0036, 0.0432]. AUROC differences remain unresolved; recall falls and ECE rises.

**Demonstrates:** this context-dependent adjustment improves clean AP beyond the anchor and its particular two-parameter global-adjustment control. **Rules out:** explaining the whole clean AP improvement merely by allowing those two global adjustments. **Plausible:** context-specific error correction, content/action information in quality descriptors, or additional network capacity. **Cannot establish:** a causal reliability estimator, superiority attributable solely to adaptivity at matched capacity, or success of the earlier F6 formulation. The original gates and anchored model differ in inputs to the link, intercept constraints and function class.

Source: [anchored comparison](../../implementations/REASSEMBLE/results/runs/followup_exploratory_studies/representation_and_robustness_studies/03_static_anchored_adaptation/comparison/results.json) and C1 handoff.

## C7. Exposure-matched corruption: clean improvement is not robustness

The extension gives static and adaptive models 50% clean, 25% image-Gaussian and 25% sensor-Gaussian exposure, with recomputed quality and no supplied fault identity. The principal six-condition adaptive−static mean AP difference is +0.0097 [−0.0034, 0.0228]. Static and adaptive optimization still differ: weighted static rows versus sampled neural views. Strong blur and channel dropout favor static point estimates; one sensor-noise condition favors adaptation over augmented static but not over clean-trained F2. No specified 0.01-margin clean noninferiority claim passes.

The later, distinct 753-action quality-interaction pilot uses identical 3,777 OOF parents and weighted augmented inputs for three heads. Its 27-parameter interaction model versus three-parameter static model gives primary mean AP +0.003012 [−0.026711, 0.032165]; clean preservation at its zero-loss margin is also unresolved. Adding quality without interactions gives a negative secondary contrast, −0.016636 [−0.030676, −0.002429]. This is not a new result for the earlier anchored model.

**Demonstrates:** matched exposure removes a major training-opportunity difference, but does not produce a resolved adaptive robustness advantage here. **Rules out:** using selected noisy-condition wins or the clean anchored gain as proof of general robustness. **Plausible:** imperfect quality proxies, clean/corrupt tradeoffs, capacity, optimization and insufficient assessment precision. **Cannot establish:** equivalence, absence of any possible benefit, or robustness to natural sensor faults. Held-out corruption families are held out of augmentation fitting; they are not a new independent population. Known-absence routing is identical across models and tests fallback, not an adaptive advantage.

The primary metrics average separately computed condition/realization APs; repeated corrupted versions do not become independent subjects. Intervals resample recordings and condition on fitted models and retained realizations.

Sources: C1 handoff; [quality-interaction pilot](../../implementations/REASSEMBLE/experiments/additional/adaptive_fusion_robustness/results/run_01/RESULTS_SUMMARY.md).

MSM's pooled training-prior AP 0.1454 is **not** its 17.73% cohort prevalence: fold-specific training priors can reorder pooled cases. This is another reason to preserve aggregation definitions. No cross-dataset AP mean or dataset-difficulty ranking is justified.

The studies isolate neither target definition, modality informativeness, observation window, representation quality, supervision nor class balance as the cause of the differences. Each changes several of these together. They do establish the following narrower implications:

1. **Targets define what useful information means.** PHM predicts a photograph-derived proxy; MSM predicts its label mapping; robotic studies predict recorded action/episode outcomes. Agreement with one cannot validate another. MSM label-2 sensitivity is a within-study target-definition check, not a complete explanation of cross-dataset variation.
2. **A window can exclude or dilute relevant evidence.** REASSEMBLE boundary pooling directly tests aggregation. Weak MSM or ViFailback visual AP could reflect visibility or representation, but those alternatives have not been separated by that experiment.
3. **The comparator matters.** Strong statistical and HGB baselines prevent a gain over a weak branch being presented as superiority over sensor modeling generally. A predictive visual branch need not improve the fitted two-input rule.
4. **Supervision and transfer must be named.** Reusing architecture with newly fitted dataset-specific heads is not unchanged-model generalization. ViFailback DONOR transfers REASSEMBLE-supervised CNN weights and fits fresh target-side components. Its FULL arms additionally update the target backbone under bounded protocols. None is zero-shot transfer of the complete REASSEMBLE predictor.
5. **Eligibility is part of the evidence.** HANDOVER's missing participant identity blocks its stipulated learned comparison. Trial-level substitution would change the question, not complete the original one.

## Integrated answer to the original questions

The original questions are retained from the existing thesis canon, grounded in exposé §3, pp. 2–3. The working-copy PDF was not newly text-extracted here; exact question wording below follows that existing canon rather than an asserted new PDF verification.

| Original question | Answer and status | Evidence / unresolved part |
|---|---|---|
| Main: How can a process-aware multimodal AI architecture be designed and evaluated for robust industrial anomaly detection using robotic welding data as a case study? | **Partially answered at the architectural/evaluation level; original welding application unresolved.** | Concrete comparisons and validity checks support an evaluation approach. There is no independent weld-quality validation, general robust architecture or online detector established by these results. |
| SQ1: Which lightweight or pretrained Transformer-based representation components are suitable for visual, audio, and sensor time-series data in a multimodal industrial anomaly detection pipeline? | **Partially answered.** | C1–C4 qualify suitability by task, budget and aggregation. Core vision executes a pretrained CNN, and PatchTST is scratch-trained. Audio and pretrained temporal-weight benefit remain open. No universal Transformer recommendation follows. |
| SQ2: How do unimodal baselines, simple concatenation fusion, modular late fusion, and process-aware gated cross-modal fusion compare in terms of predictive performance? | **Answered for the executed formulations and cohorts; only partially for the full proposed architecture family.** | Static prediction fusion helps REASSEMBLE over the sensor branch; its advantage over equal averaging is unresolved. Other datasets qualify its applicability. Scalar context gates are not token-level cross-attention. PHM's retained feature fusion combines sensor groups. |
| SQ3: Can a gated cross-modal fusion architecture improve robustness when one or more modalities are missing, degraded, or unreliable? | **Partially answered by bounded negative/inconclusive tests; general improvement unresolved.** | Identical fallback cannot show adaptive superiority. Original gates lack a consistent advantage; anchored clean improvement does not establish corruption robustness, and matched-exposure primary contrasts remain unresolved. |
| SQ4: How can multimodal architecture performance be evaluated beyond accuracy, including robustness, interpretability, modularity, and practical usability? | **Substantially answered as an evaluation method, partially demonstrated in application.** | Paired ranking, probability scores, class-specific outcomes, group uncertainty, labels and timing give distinct evidence. Coefficients are inspectable, not causal explanations. Modularity is a design recommendation; comparative modularity superiority, operator usability and end-to-end deployment are untested. |

The resulting contribution is an empirical account of **when the evaluated information can be used**, including limits and failed eligibility gates. It does not depend on producing a winning architecture. In particular, statistical superiority and hybrid benefit coexist; pooling improvement and unresolved adaptation coexist; a visual ordering contribution and unresolved F2-versus-average superiority coexist; clean adaptation and unresolved robustness coexist. These are compatible findings about different interventions, not inconsistencies to be removed.
